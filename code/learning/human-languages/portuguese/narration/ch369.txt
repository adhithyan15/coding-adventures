Portuguese, chapter 369: Com uma condição.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a condição — the condition.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to presume, then the Portuguese for plausible.

You'll want to know: a condição.
a condição — "the condition".
Vou contigo, com uma condição: voltamos cedo. I will go with you on one condition: we come back early.
Com a condição de + infinitive. Condições de trabalho — working conditions.

The word, taken apart.
condição is a learned borrowing from Latin condiciō "an agreement, a condition" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short), often spelt conditiō in later Latin, built on condīcere "to talk together, to agree on", from dīcere "to say".

Lesson 2 of 5.
desde que — provided that, as long as; since (in time).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for plausible, then the Portuguese for the condition.

You'll want to know: desde que.
desde que — "provided that, as long as; since (in time)".
Podes ir à festa desde que voltes antes da meia-noite. You can go to the party as long as you come back before midnight.
With the subjunctive it sets a condition: desde que voltes. With the indicative it means since: desde que cheguei.

The word, taken apart.
desde que is built from desde "since", usually traced to Latin dē + ex + dē, all meaning "from" (Lewis & Short), and que.

Lesson 3 of 5.
contanto que — as long as, on condition that.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the condition, then the Portuguese for provided that.

You'll want to know: contanto que.
contanto que — "as long as, on condition that".
Empresto-te o carro, contanto que tenhas cuidado. I will lend you the car, as long as you are careful.
Takes the subjunctive; a little more formal than desde que.

The word, taken apart.
contanto que is built from com "with", tanto "so much", inherited from Latin tantum "so much" (Lewis & Short), and que: "with so much as".

Lesson 4 of 5.
caso — in case, if (+ subjunctive); the case.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for provided that, then the Portuguese for as long as.

You'll want to know: caso.
caso — "in case, if (+ subjunctive); the case".
Caso chova, o jantar é dentro de casa. If it rains, dinner is indoors.
Caso + present subjunctive: caso chova. As a noun, o caso is the case: neste caso.

The word, taken apart.
caso comes from Latin cāsus "a falling, an event, a chance" (EtymDB, Lewis & Short), built on cadere "to fall": what befalls you.

Lesson 5 of 5.
consoante — depending on, according to; the consonant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for as long as, then the Portuguese for in case.

You'll want to know: consoante.
consoante — "depending on, according to; the consonant".
Vamos à praia ou ao cinema, consoante o tempo. We will go to the beach or to the cinema, depending on the weather.
Consoante + noun: consoante o preço. As a noun, a consoante is a consonant.

The word, taken apart.
consoante is inherited from Old Portuguese consõante (EtymDB), from Latin cōnsonāns "sounding together, agreeing" (Lewis & Short), on sonāre "to sound": what agrees with something goes according to it.
