Mandarin Chinese, chapter 389: In Case.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
bèiyòng — spare (kept for when needed).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to do ahead of time, then the Mandarin for a time zone.

You'll want to know: bèiyòng.
bèiyòng — "spare (kept for when needed)".
wànyī yàoshi diū le wǒmen hái yǒu bèiyòng de — If the keys get lost, we've still got a spare.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bèiyòng is built from bèi 'to provide against' and 用 'to use', both inherited from Middle Chinese, bijH and yowngH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'complete' and 'to guard against'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
到期 (dàoqī) — to fall due; to run out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a risk, then the Mandarin for as a precaution.

You'll want to know: 到期.
到期 — dàoqī — "to fall due; to run out".
fángzū xià ge yuè jiù dàoqī le — The rent falls due next month.

The word, taken apart.
到期 (dàoqī) is built from 到 'to arrive' and 期 'a set time', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tawH and gi (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'a limit' and 'an appointed meeting'. Middle Chinese gi would regularly give a rising tone, qí; the level tone of qī is irregular.
