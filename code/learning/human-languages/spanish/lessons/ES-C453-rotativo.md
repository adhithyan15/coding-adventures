# rotativo — it comes round again

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-03, ES-LEX-C347-WHERE-20] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say **la rueda** and **alrededor**. A third word off the same
Latin wheel is coming.

## You'll want to know: rotativo
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-LEX-C453-LIMPIEZA-03]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C453-LIMPIEZA-02] -->

**rotativo** — rotating, done in turns, on a rota.

*Un turno rotativo* — a rotating shift. *La limpieza es rotativa* — the
cleaning goes round the neighbours. *Presidencia rotativa* — a chairmanship
that passes from one member to the next.

The idea is always the same: the job does not stay with one person, it comes
round. **Rotar** is the verb, and **la rotación** the noun with the ending from
the lesson before this one.

## Grammar Lens: -ivo describes what a thing does
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C453-LIMPIEZA-03, ES-LEX-C453-LIMPIEZA-02] -->

**-ivo** makes an adjective meaning "of the kind that does this":

| verb | adjective |
|---|---|
| rotar | rotat**ivo** — that rotates |
| informar | informat**ivo** — that informs |
| decorar | decorat**ivo** — that decorates |

It is not about being in the act. A *turno rotativo* is not rotating at this
moment; it is a shift **of the rotating kind**. The ending names a habit or a
capacity, which is why *un cartel informativo* is informative whether or not
anybody is reading it.

English has the identical ending in *rotative*, *informative*, *decorative*,
and uses it the same way.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-C453-LIMPIEZA-03, ES-LEX-C449-TALLER-03] -->

Latin **rota**, wheel — for the third time, and it is worth stopping on.

| word | how the wheel got in |
|---|---|
| **la rueda** | straight down from *rota*, vowel broken by stress |
| **alrededor** | *retrō* bent sideways by *rota* |
| **rotativo** | lifted from *rota* whole, by scholars |

Three words, three routes out of one Latin noun. *Rueda* is what centuries of
speakers did to it. *Rotativo* is what a writer did to it in one step,
keeping the **o** intact because it never passed through anybody's mouth on
the way.

That difference — worn down by speech, or lifted whole from a book — is the
single most useful thing to notice about Spanish vocabulary, and here you can
see both outcomes of it side by side.
