Lesson 1 of 5.
उपत्यका (upatyakā) — a valley.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for radiance, then the Sanskrit for the ground.

You'll want to know: उपत्यका.
उपत्यका — upatyakā — "a valley".

The word, taken apart.
upatyakā is built on upa, "near, below", with the suffix -tyaka; Pāṇini 5.2.34 forms it beside adhityakā, "table-land", from adhi, "above". MW glosses it "land at the foot of a mountain, low-land" and "a valley".

Lesson 2 of 5.
मरुभूमिः (marubhūmiḥ) — a desert.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for the ground, then the Sanskrit for a valley.

You'll want to know: मरुभूमिः.
मरुभूमिः — marubhūmiḥ — "a desert".

The word, taken apart.
marubhūmi is built from maru, "wilderness, sandy waste", and bhūmi, "land" (MW). MW derives maru probably from √mṛ, "to die": the dead land. MW also gives it as the name of a country, Marwar.

Lesson 3 of 5.
काननम् (kānanam) — a woodland.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a valley, then the Sanskrit for a desert.

You'll want to know: काननम्.
काननम् — kānanam — "a woodland".

The word, taken apart.
kānana is a word for "forest, grove" (MW). Its origin is not settled: MW reports a derivation from a root √kan, while CDIAL 3028 cites Mayrhofer for taking it, with kāntāra, "wilderness", from Dravidian.

Lesson 4 of 5.
कुञ्जः (kuñjaḥ) — a bower, a thicket.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a desert, then the Sanskrit for a woodland.

You'll want to know: कुञ्जः.
कुञ्जः — kuñjaḥ — "a bower, a thicket".

The word, taken apart.
kuñja is a place overgrown with plants and creepers, a bower (MW; CDIAL 3226). Neither dictionary gives it a derivation, so its origin is uncertain. Hindi kũjṛā, "vegetable seller", is built on it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
शाद्वलम् (śādvalam) — a meadow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a woodland, then the Sanskrit for a bower.

You'll want to know: शाद्वलम्.
शाद्वलम् — śādvalam — "a meadow".

The word, taken apart.
śādvala is built on śāda, "young grass", with the suffix -vala, "abounding in" (MW): a place full of fresh grass. MW derives śāda from √śad, "to fall off", with a first sense "falling, dropping".
