# आख़िर (ākhir) — at last, in the end

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-GRAMMAR-C279-KAR-SEQUENCE-01, HI-GRAMMAR-C276-PAST-PERSON-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Before the new one: say "I ate and fell asleep". (*maiṁ khānā khākar so gayā*.) A story needs a word for its last step, and for a wait that ends.

## You'll want to know: आख़िर
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C279-AKHIR-01]; assesses=[HI-CONCEPT-C18-GHANTA-01, HI-LEX-C94-SPAN-04, HI-LEX-C90-ROUT-06, HI-LEX-C164-ACT164-04] -->

**आख़िर** — *ākhir* — "at last, finally".

> **दो घंटे के बाद आख़िर ट्रेन आई।**
>
> *do ghaṇṭe ke bād ākhir ṭren āī.* — "After two hours the train came at last."

With **में** it closes a story: **आख़िर में** (*ākhir meṁ*), "in the end".

> **आख़िर में हम घर पहुँचे।**
>
> *ākhir meṁ ham ghar pahũce.* — "In the end we reached home."

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**आख़िर** (*ākhir*) is usually traced to Arabic *ākhir*, "last, the end", which reached Hindi through Persian. Its **ख़**, with the dot below, is the letter Hindi writes in many words that came this way.
