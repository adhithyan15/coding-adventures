# ocho signos — sorted by what each one does to a sentence

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C424-01, ES-ORTH-C424-04] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Name as many of the eight marks as you can, in Spanish, before
reading the table. Aim for five.

## Grammar Lens: divide, join, or set apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C424-01, ES-ORTH-C424-02, ES-ORTH-C424-03, ES-ORTH-C424-04, ES-ORTH-C424-05, ES-ORTH-C424-06, ES-ORTH-C424-07, ES-ORTH-C424-08] -->

Eight marks, and every one of them is doing one of three jobs:

| mark | name | job |
|---|---|---|
| . | el punto | divides — a sentence, a paragraph, a text |
| , | la coma | divides — inside a sentence |
| : | los dos puntos | divides — and announces what follows |
| — | la raya | sets apart — a turn of speech |
| « » | las comillas | sets apart — a quoted or foreign word |
| ( ) | los paréntesis | sets apart — an aside |
| - | el guion | **joins** — two words into one name |
| / | la barra | divides — alternatives, or *per* |

Seven divide or set apart. **El guion** is the only one of the eight that joins,
which is the cleanest way to keep it from being confused with the mark it most
resembles.

## Grammar Lens: the three with plural names
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ORTH-C424-03, ES-ORTH-C424-05, ES-ORTH-C424-06] -->

| name | why plural |
|---|---|
| los dos puntos | two points in the mark |
| las comillas | they come as a pair |
| los paréntesis | so do these |

Each of the three is one mark to an English speaker and a plural noun in
Spanish, so each takes *los* or *las* and a plural verb.
