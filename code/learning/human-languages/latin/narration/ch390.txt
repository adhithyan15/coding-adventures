Latin, chapter 390: On One Condition.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
condiciō, condiciōnis — a condition, terms; a situation.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a load, then the Latin for compliance.

You'll want to know: condiciō, condiciōnis.
condiciō, condiciōnis — "a condition, terms; a situation".
Hāc condiciōne veniam, ut tū quoque veniās. I will come on this condition, that you come too.
condiciō, condiciōnis f. Hāc condiciōne ut + subjunctive: on condition that. It is also a person's situation in life. English condition.

The word, taken apart.
condiciō is built on condīcō "to agree on, to settle" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from con- "together" and dīcō "to say": what two sides say together. English condition comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
sīn — but if.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for compliance, then the Latin for a condition.

You'll want to know: sīn.
sīn — "but if".
Sī venīs, gaudeō; sīn minus, domī maneō. If you come, I am glad; but if not, I stay at home.
Sīn opens the second of two conditions, after a sī clause: if … but if. Sīn minus or sīn aliter, but if not.

The word, taken apart.
sīn is built from sī "if" and the particle -ne (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): if, on the other hand.

Lesson 3 of 5.
dummodo — provided that, so long as.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a condition, then the Latin for but if.

You'll want to know: dummodo.
dummodo — "provided that, so long as".
Omnia tibi dabō, dummodo vērum dīcās. I will give you everything, provided that you tell the truth.
Dummodo takes the subjunctive; dummodo nē, provided that not. It is dum, while, with an adverb meaning only.

The word, taken apart.
dummodo is built from dum "while, so long as" and modo "only" (Lewis & Short): only so long as.

Lesson 4 of 5.
modo — only; a moment ago; provided that (with the subjunctive).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for but if, then the Latin for provided that.

You'll want to know: modo.
modo — "only; a moment ago; provided that (with the subjunctive)".
Veniam, modo nē pluat. I will come, provided it does not rain.
Modo is the ablative of modus, a measure: only. Modo + subjunctive, provided that; nōn modo … sed etiam, not only … but also.

The word, taken apart.
modo is the old ablative of modus "a measure, a limit" (Lewis & Short): within measure, so only.

Lesson 5 of 5.
quamvīs — however much; although (with the subjunctive).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for provided that, then the Latin for only.

You'll want to know: quamvīs.
quamvīs — "however much; although (with the subjunctive)".
Quamvīs dīves sit, nōn est fēlīx. However rich he may be, he is not happy.
Quamvīs = quam + vīs, as much as you like; in classical prose it takes the subjunctive, while quamquam, although, takes the indicative.

The word, taken apart.
quamvīs is built from quam "as" and vīs "you wish", from volō (Lewis & Short): as much as you like.
