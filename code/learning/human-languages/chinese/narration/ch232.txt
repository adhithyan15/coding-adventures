Mandarin Chinese, chapter 232: An Accident.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
出事 (chūshì) — to have an accident; for something to go wrong.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a nurse, then the Mandarin for to prescribe.

You'll want to know: 出事.
出事 — chūshì — "to have an accident; for something to go wrong".
前天雪很大这儿出事了 qiántiān xuě hěn dà zhèr chūshì le — The day before yesterday the snow was heavy, and there was an accident here.

The word, taken apart.
出事 (chūshì) is built from 出 'to come out' and 事 'a matter', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyhwit and dzriH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to employ' and 'to set up'; 'a matter' is the everyday sense, not the gloss.

What you've built.
One more word for talking about the past.

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: zhuàng.
zhuàng — "to crash into; to bump into".
tā qí chē zhuàngdàole yí ge rén — He rode his bike into someone.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhuàng is inherited from Middle Chinese draewngH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to strike a bell'; the dictionary glosses its other reading 'to knock against'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
jiùhùchē — ambulance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to bleed, then the Mandarin for to crash into.

You'll want to know: jiùhùchē.
jiùhùchē — "ambulance".
gǎnkuài jiào jiùhùchē — Call an ambulance, quick!
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiùhùchē is built from jiù 'to rescue', hù 'to protect' and 车 'vehicle', all inherited from Middle Chinese, kjuwH, huH and tsyhae (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to protect' and 'to stop'.
