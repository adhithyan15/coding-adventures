## You'll want to know: shāngchǎng
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C190-TOWN190-03]; assesses=[] -->

*shāngchǎng* — "shopping centre".

> *zhōumò de shāngchǎng hěn rènao* — The shopping centre is busy at the weekend.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*shāngchǎng* is built from *shāng* 'trade' and *chǎng* 'an open ground', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *syang* and *drjang* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription). The dictionary glosses the second as a cleared ground for threshing grain, with a level tone, so the rising tone of chǎng is not the regular outcome; the sense 'trade' for the first is not given there either, so how both came is not settled.
