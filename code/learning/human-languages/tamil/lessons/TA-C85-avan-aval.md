# அவன் / அவள் — the pair underneath the respectful word

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-LEX-C37-AVAR-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say the respectful word for a person who is not here. Say whether it
tells you anything about their sex. (It does not.)

[PAUSE 2s] Today's two words do.

## You'll want to know: அவன் and அவள்
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TA-LEX-C85-AVAN-AVAL-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **அவன்** — *avaṉ* — **he**
> **அவள்** — *avaḷ* — **she**

These are the plain, familiar words, and **அவர்** was built on top of them: the
lesson that taught இவர் said so in passing, that it comes from இவன் and இவள்
with the same **-அர்** that makes நண்பர் out of நண்பன்.

So the ending you met on a word for *friend* is the same ending that turns a
familiar *he* into a respectful *that person*. **One suffix, three words, and it
does the same job in all of them.**

## Grammar Lens: choosing between them
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-LEX-C85-AVAN-AVAL-01, TA-LEX-C37-AVAR-01] -->

| about | you say |
|---|---|
| a man, familiarly | அவன் |
| a woman, familiarly | அவள் |
| either, respectfully | அவர் |

**The choice is about the relationship, not about the grammar.** அவன் and அவள்
go with somebody younger, a close friend, a child — the people you would use the
familiar நீ with rather than நீங்கள். Reaching for அவன் about a stranger or an
elder lands the way the familiar *you* would.

A learner in doubt is safer with **அவர்**. Being a shade too polite passes
without comment; the other direction does not.
