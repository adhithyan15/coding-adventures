Sanskrit, chapter 475: Sweat, Yawns and Sneezes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
स्विद्यति (svidyati) — sweats.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for flesh, then the Sanskrit for throbs.

You'll want to know: स्विद्यति.
स्विद्यति — svidyati — "sweats".
ग्रीष्मे कृषकः बहु स्विद्यति। grīṣme kṛṣakaḥ bahu svidyati. In summer the farmer sweats a lot.
Past (he …): सः बहु स्विन्नवान्, saḥ bahu svinnavān.

The word, taken apart.
स्विद्यति (svidyati) comes from the root √svid, "to sweat, to perspire"; MW's ppp svinna is "sweating" (MW).

क्षौति — kṣauti — "sneezes".
धूल्या सः क्षौति। dhūlyā saḥ kṣauti. The dust makes him sneeze.
Past (he …): सः क्षुतवान्, saḥ kṣutavān.

The word, taken apart.
क्षौति (kṣauti) comes from the root √kṣu, "to sneeze", a second-class verb whose vowel strengthens to au (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
हिक्का (hikkā) — a hiccup.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for sneezes, then the Sanskrit for scratches.

You'll want to know: हिक्का.
हिक्का — hikkā — "a hiccup".
सः शीघ्रं खादति, अतः तस्य हिक्का भवति। saḥ śīghraṃ khādati, ataḥ tasya hikkā bhavati. He eats too fast, so he gets hiccups.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
हिक्का (hikkā) MW gives "hiccup, sob, a spasmodic sound in the throat" and compares the form hekkā; MW gives no derivation, so its origin is uncertain (MW).
