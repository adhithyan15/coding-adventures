French, chapter 381: Permis ou non.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
autoriser — to authorise, to allow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for optional, then the French for the obligation.

You'll want to know: autoriser.
autoriser — "to authorise, to allow".
Les chiens ne sont pas autorisés dans le magasin. Dogs are not allowed in the shop.
Autoriser quelqu'un à + infinitive.

The word, taken apart.
autoriser comes from Old French auctorisier (EtymDB), from Medieval Latin auctōrizāre, built on auctor "author, guarantor", from augēre "to increase" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 6.
l'autorisation — the authorisation, the permission.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the obligation, then the French for to authorise.

You'll want to know: l'autorisation.
l'autorisation — "the authorisation, the permission".
Il faut une autorisation pour filmer ici. You need permission to film here.
Feminine. Close to la permission, but more official.

The word, taken apart.
autorisation is built from autoriser and -ation. The verb rests on Latin auctor "author, guarantor", from augēre "to increase" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 6.
exiger — to demand, to require.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to tolerate, then the French for the tolerance.

You'll want to know: exiger.
exiger — "to demand, to require".
Le patron exige beaucoup de ses salariés. The boss demands a lot of his employees.
Exiger que takes the subjunctive. Nous exigeons.

The word, taken apart.
exiger is a learned borrowing from Latin exigere "to drive out, to demand" (Lewis & Short), built from ex "out" and agere "to drive".
