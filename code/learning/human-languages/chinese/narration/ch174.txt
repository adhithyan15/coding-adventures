Mandarin Chinese, chapter 174: Jobs Around the House.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
guà — to hang; to hang up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a get-together, then the Mandarin for to pay for everyone.

You'll want to know: guà.
guà — "to hang; to hang up".
mén hòubian kěyǐ guà dàyī — You can hang your coat behind the door.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
guà is inherited from Middle Chinese kweaH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to hang', under a popular way of writing it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
nòng — to sort something out; to do; to handle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to pay for everyone, then the Mandarin for to hang something up.

You'll want to know: nòng.
nòng — "to sort something out; to do; to handle".
diànnǎo huài le nǐ huì nòng ma — The computer's broken: do you know how to sort it out?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
nòng is inherited from Middle Chinese luwngH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to play with'. Its first sound would regularly give lòng; how it came to be said with n is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
tīzi — ladder.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to sort something out, then the Mandarin for to lift together.

You'll want to know: tīzi.
tīzi — "ladder".
wǒmen yìqǐ tái tīzi — Let's carry the ladder together.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tīzi is built from tī and the noun ending 子, both inherited from Middle Chinese, thej and tsiX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'wooden steps'. In the word the second loses its tone.

Lesson 5 of 5.
dēngpào — light bulb.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to lift together, then the Mandarin for a ladder.

You'll want to know: dēngpào.
dēngpào — "light bulb".
dēngpào huài le wǒmen yào huàn yí ge — The bulb's gone, so we need to change it.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dēngpào is built from dēng 'lamp' and pào 'bubble', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tong and phaew (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'bubbles on water': the lamp's glass bubble. Phaew would regularly give a first tone, so how pào came about is not settled.

One more word for saying what I do.
