Russian, chapter 158: Alive, Dead, Hungry, Well-fed, Familiar.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
живой (zhivóy) — alive, lively.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for tasty, then the Russian for comfortable, convenient.

You'll want to know: живой.
живой (zhivóy) — "alive, lively".

The word, taken apart.
живой (zhivóy) from Proto-Slavic živŭ — inherited. Vasmer lists Lithuanian gývas, Sanskrit jīvá-, Latin vīvus and Gothic qius, all "alive": English quick, in its old sense "alive", is a cousin.

Lesson 2 of 5.
мёртвый (myórtvyy) — dead.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for comfortable, convenient, then the Russian for alive.

You'll want to know: мёртвый.
мёртвый (myórtvyy) — "dead".

The word, taken apart.
мёртвый (myórtvyy) from Proto-Slavic mĭrtvŭ — inherited from PIE mṛtós, "dead", reshaped after живой (zhivóy), "alive", as Latin mortuus was (Vasmer). Vasmer lists Sanskrit mṛtá-, "dead", and Greek brotós, "mortal".

The word, taken apart.
голодный (golódnyy) is голод, "hunger", with -ный. голод from Proto-Slavic goldŭ, which Vasmer relates to Sanskrit gṛdhyati, "is greedy".

Lesson 5 of 5.
знакомый (znakómyy) — familiar; an acquaintance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for hungry, then the Russian for well-fed.

You'll want to know: знакомый.
знакомый (znakómyy) — "familiar; an acquaintance".

The word, taken apart.
знакомый (znakómyy) is built on знак, "a sign, a mark", from знать, "to know": a person you know. знать is a cousin of English know and Latin (g)nōscere, "to get to know".
