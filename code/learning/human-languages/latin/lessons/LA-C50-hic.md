## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C50-POINT-01]; assesses=[] -->

**hic, haec, hoc** — "**this**," the one near me.

Latin points three ways, and this is the first of the three. **Hic** is the thing
in the speaker's own hand or reach.

> **Hic liber.** — This book (here, mine).

Three forms because it agrees with its noun: **hic** for a masculine one, **haec**
for a feminine one, **hoc** for a neuter one — the same three-way agreement *quī*
showed you last chapter.

The system survived. Spanish still points three ways — *este*, *ese*, *aquel* —
and it is Latin's system, arriving through a different set of words. Italian,
French and Portuguese all collapsed to two. So a Spanish speaker meeting *hic,
iste, ille* is meeting the shape of their own grammar in its original three
pieces.

English **ad hoc** is *hoc*, "for this"; a **hic jacet** is what an old gravestone
says: "here lies."
