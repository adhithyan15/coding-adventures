Marathi, chapter 34: Why, and Because.
4 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 4.
का (kā) — why.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Retrieve the two items before this one by name: the particle का that turns a statement into a yes-or-no question, and the single? that marks it on the page. Say a question with both.

You'll want to know: का at the front.
का? — kā? — why?
का आवडते? — kā āvaḍte? — Why do you like it?
The same two letters you have just learned to hang on the end mean why when they stand at the front, or on their own. That is not a coincidence and it is not an accident of spelling: both come from the Sanskrit question stem क- (ka-), (svalpaviraam) which also built काय ("what") and कसा ("how"). Marathi's whole question system is one syllable with different tails, and the same Indo-European k- is under Latin quis and English who.
Position is what keeps the two apart, so it is worth saying both aloud back to back:
मराठी आवडते का? — do you like Marathi? (particle, at the end).
मराठी का आवडते? — why do you like Marathi? (question word, in front of the verb).
Said on its own, का? is a complete turn: it is how you ask someone to explain what they have just said.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: marāṭhī āvaḍte kā? then marāṭhī kā āvaḍte?]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: kā? on its own, as a whole turn]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the three k-words together — kāy, kasā, kā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 25 seconds]
Ask whether the listener likes Marathi, then ask why — the same word in two positions.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does का mean at the front of a sentence? (Why.) And at the end? (It makes a yes-or-no question.) What decides which? (Where it stands.)
Sources: Wiktionary: का.


Lesson 2 of 4.
कारण (kāraṇ) — because.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You can now ask का?. Ask it out loud. Then notice that you have no way whatever to answer it.

Grammar Lens: कारण takes a whole sentence after it.
मी मराठी शिकतो कारण मला मराठी आवडते. — mī marāṭhī shikto kāraṇ malā marāṭhī āvaḍte. — I am learning Marathi because I like Marathi.
आणि, किंवा and पण put two things side by side as equals. कारण (kāraṇ) does something new: everything after it is subordinate — it exists only to explain the half in front. That is the first time in this book that one Marathi clause has been put inside the job of another.
The order is fixed and it is the useful way round: the claim first, the reason second. English can lead with because; Marathi's ordinary sentence does not.
कारण is Sanskrit कारण (kāraṇa), (svalpaviraam) "a cause," built on the root कृ (kṛ-), (svalpaviraam) "to do" — the same कृ inside करणे, the verb this book gave you for to do. Marathi's because is, at root, the doing of it.
A short answer works too. Asked का?, (svalpaviraam) you may reply with कारण (kāraṇ) and one clause, and nothing else is needed.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: kā? then answer it with kāraṇ …]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: malā chahā āvaḍto paṇ dūdh āvaḍat nāhī — equals — then mī marāṭhī shikto kāraṇ malā marāṭhī āvaḍte — not equals]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: which verb कारण (kāraṇ) is related to — karṇe]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 30 seconds]
Ask why, then answer with a reason: you are learning Marathi because you like it.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which half comes first in a Marathi कारण (kāraṇ) sentence? (The claim; the reason follows.) How is कारण (kāraṇ) unlike पण? (पण joins two equals; कारण (kāraṇ) puts one clause underneath the other.)
Sources: Wiktionary: कारण (kāraṇ).


Lesson 3 of 4.
, (svalpaviraam) — the mark that carries a list.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script: the comma does the joining work for all but the last pair until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say चहा आणि दूध आणि पाणी. Three things, two आणि. Out loud it is clumsy; on paper it is worse.

Script: the comma does the joining work for all but the last pair.
चहा, दूध आणि पाणी.
Marathi writes the comma exactly as English does: it stands in for the joining word between every pair except the last, where आणि is written out. The mark is the same shape you already know from the Latin alphabet, sitting on the baseline below the letters — not hanging from the headline like a Devanagari sign.
It has one more everyday job, and it is the one the writing task needs:
नमस्कार मीरा, मी अरुण. — namaskār Mīrā, mī Aruṇ.
A comma after the person you are addressing. Every message that opens by naming its reader wants one.
Marathi's own name for it, स्वल्पविराम, means "small pause," which is a better description than comma — the mark tells the eye to hesitate, not to stop.

Writing — copy, then place.
Write चहा, दूध आणि पाणी once. Then write नमस्कार मीरा, and check that the comma sits on the baseline, after the name.
[question — say your answer, then pause 25 seconds]
Write the three drinks as one written list, using the comma and one āṇi.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
In a written list of three, how many आणि do you write, and where? (One, before the last item.) What does the comma's Marathi name mean? (Small pause.)


Lesson 4 of 4.
Two turns: why, and because — asking for a reason and giving one, in two turns.

Warm-up.
[pause 5 seconds]
का at the end asks yes-or-no. का at the front asks why. Say one of each before you go on.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: marāṭhī āvaḍte kā? — ho — kā? — kāraṇ …]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: mī marāṭhī shikto kāraṇ malā marāṭhī āvaḍte]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — write: chahā, dūdh āṇi pāṇī]
[question — say your answer, then pause 30 seconds]
Take both turns: ask why, then answer with a reason.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which joining word puts one clause underneath another? (कारण (kāraṇ).) Which half of a कारण (kāraṇ) sentence comes first? (The claim.) Then stop.
