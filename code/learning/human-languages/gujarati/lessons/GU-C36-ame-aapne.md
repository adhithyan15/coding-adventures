# અમે / આપણે (ame / āpaṇe) — the two we

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-PRON-TE-01, GU-JOIN-ANE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] One lesson back: the pronoun for someone absent. Four chapters back,
at the top of its chapter: the word that joins two things.

- [YOU RECALL: *te*, and *ane*]

## The words, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-PRON-AME-01, GU-PRON-AAPNE-01]; assesses=[GU-JOIN-ANE-01] -->

English has one *we* and it is ambiguous. Gujarati has two, and they are not
interchangeable:

> **અમે** — *we*, **not including you**
> **આપણે** — *we*, **including you**

If you say **અમે** to someone, you have told them they are outside the group.
If you say **આપણે**, you have invited them in. Neither is more polite; they mean
different things, and the mistake is audible.

This distinction has a name — linguists call it exclusive and inclusive *we* —
and a great many languages make it. English speakers have to learn to hear a
choice they have never had to make.
