Tamil, chapter 73: Today, Yesterday, and the Year.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
இன்று (iṉṟu) — today.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did aḻu mean?

You'll want to know: இன்று.
இன்று (iṉṟu) — "today".
"Today".
You already have நாள் (nāḷ), the day itself, and நாளை (nāḷai), tomorrow. இன்று (iṉṟu) is the day you are standing in, and it completes the middle of that little set.
This is the careful, written form, and it is the one printed on a page. In fast speech you will also hear இன்னைக்கு (iṉṉaikku) for the same thing — worth recognising, but இன்று (iṉṟu) is the one to say.

What you've built.
The day you are standing in.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: iṉṟu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: nāḷ, then iṉṟu, then nāḷai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say siri, then say aḻu, then say iṉṟu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does இன்று (iṉṟu) mean, and which word already gave you "tomorrow"? ("Today"; நாளை.)


Lesson 2 of 6.
நேற்று (nēṟṟu) — yesterday.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did iṉṟu mean?

You'll want to know: நேற்று.
நேற்று (nēṟṟu) — "yesterday".
"Yesterday" — the third corner of the same triangle: நேற்று (nēṟṟu), இன்று (iṉṟu), நாளை.
The middle is a doubled ற்ற, the hard r held long, and it is the sound that most often catches a new speaker out. Say it slowly: the first ற has a புள்ளி and no vowel, so it leans straight onto the second.
With three words you can now place any day against the one you are in.

What you've built.
Two, and the days on either side of today are named.

[your turn — say: nēṟṟu]

[your turn — say: nēṟṟu, iṉṟu, nāḷai — the three, in order]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: nēṟṟu once more, holding the doubled ற]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say aḻu, then say iṉṟu, then say nēṟṟu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Say the three days in order, starting with yesterday. (நேற்று (nēṟṟu), இன்று (iṉṟu), நாளை.)


Lesson 3 of 6.
இன்று (iṉṟu) — a word that opens on a vowel.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: இன்று — a word that opens on a vowel and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the page: you can say iṉṟu and nēṟṟu. Now read the first one.

Script you'll notice: இன்று — a word that opens on a vowel.
இன்று (iṉṟu) puts together two things you have read separately: a word that begins with an independent vowel, and a புள்ளி making one consonant lean on the next.
[a table of 3 rows, read aloud]
piece: இ, i, the free-standing letter, because the word opens on the vowel.
piece: ன், ṉ, ன with a புள்ளி — no vowel of its own.
piece: று, ṟu, ற with the ு sign.
இ, ன், று becomes இன்று (iṉṟu).
This is why the letters ஓ and உ were taught on their own. A vowel at the front of a word cannot be a sign — there is nothing for it to sit on — so Tamil keeps a separate shape for exactly this job, and இன்று (iṉṟu) is that shape doing it.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — read: இ — then ன் — then று]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[once you have stopped driving — point: the letter that could only ever be a free-standing one, and say why]
[your turn — read: இன்று (iṉṟu), then ஓடு — two words that open on a vowel]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say iṉṟu, then say nēṟṟu, then read இன்று]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read இன்று (iṉṟu). Why is the first shape a letter and not a sign? (Because the word begins with the vowel, so there is no consonant for a sign to sit on.)


Lesson 4 of 6.
முன்பு (muṉbu) — before, earlier.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: read இன்று (iṉṟu), and say what it means.

You'll want to know: முன்பு.
முன்பு (muṉbu) — "before, earlier".
"Before" — the general one, for anything earlier than the moment you are standing in.
You already have பிறகு (piṟaku), "afterwards". முன்பு (muṉbu) is its opposite, and between them the two put events in order without your needing a single verb tense to do it.
The ன் in its middle is closed by a புள்ளி and leans on the ப after it — the same joint you read a moment ago inside இன்று (iṉṟu).

What you've built.
Before and after, and a day on each side of today.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: piṟaku, then muṉbu — the two directions in time]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: nēṟṟu, then muṉbu — one is a day, one is any earlier moment]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say nēṟṟu, then read இன்று (iṉṟu), then say muṉbu]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]

Lesson 5 of 6.
மாதம் (mādam) — a month.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did muṉbu mean?

You'll want to know: மாதம்.
மாதம் (mādam) — "a month".
A month.
You already know twelve of them by name — the Tamil months you met in their own chapter. This is the word for the unit itself, the one you need to say "one month" rather than to name a particular one.
And another ம் ending — like கோபம், பயம் and பணம் before it — on a word whose middle vowel is the long ா.

What you've built.
Above the day: the month.

Lesson 6 of 6.
ஆண்டு (āṇḍu) — a year.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: what did mādam mean, and what did muṉbu mean?

You'll want to know: ஆண்டு.
ஆண்டு (āṇḍu) — "a year".
A year — and it is the unit hiding inside a question you can already ask.
When you ask about someone's வயது (vayathu), their age, what is being counted is ஆண்டு (āṇḍu). The question was yours a long time ago; this is the word for what the answer is made of.
It opens on the free-standing ஆ, because the word begins with that vowel — the same reason இன்று (iṉṟu) opens on இ. In the middle is the ண்ட you first read in வண்டி.
Three sizes now, each holding the one before it: நாள், மாதம் (mādam), ஆண்டு (āṇḍu).

What you've built.
Five: today, yesterday, and the three units above the day.

Guided Practice.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say muṉbu, then say mādam, then say āṇḍu]
