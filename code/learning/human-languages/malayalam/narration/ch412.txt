Malayalam, chapter 412: In Short.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
ചുരുക്കത്തിൽ (curukkattil) — in short.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for actually, then the Malayalam for in reality.

You'll want to know: ചുരുക്കത്തിൽ.
ചുരുക്കത്തിൽ — curukkattil — "in short".
ചുരുക്കത്തിൽ, യാത്ര വളരെ നന്നായിരുന്നു. curukkattil, yātra vaḷare nannāyirunnu. In short, the trip went very well.
The locative of ചുരുക്കം, shortness.

The word, taken apart.
ചുരുക്കത്തിൽ (curukkattil) is native Dravidian: ചുരുക്കം, "contraction, shortness" (DEDR 2687), from ചുരുങ്ങുക, "to shrink", with the locative ending -ത്തിൽ.

Lesson 2 of 6.
മറിച്ച് (maṟiccŭ) — on the contrary.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for in reality, then the Malayalam for in short.

You'll want to know: മറിച്ച്.
മറിച്ച് — maṟiccŭ — "on the contrary".
അവൾ ദേഷ്യപ്പെട്ടില്ല, മറിച്ച് ചിരിച്ചു. avaḷ dēṣyappeṭṭilla, maṟiccŭ ciriccu. She did not get angry; on the contrary, she laughed.
It follows a negative clause and turns it round.

The word, taken apart.
മറിച്ച് (maṟiccŭ) is native Dravidian, the -ച്ച് form of മറിക്കുക, "to turn over" (DEDR 4761): "turning it over".

Lesson 3 of 6.
അഥവാ (athavā) — or; in other words.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for in short, then the Malayalam for on the contrary.

You'll want to know: അഥവാ.
അഥവാ — athavā — "or; in other words".
ചായ അഥവാ കാപ്പി, ഏതെങ്കിലും മതി. cāya athavā kāppi, ēteṅkiluṁ mati. Tea or coffee, either will do.
A bookish or. It can also mean if by chance: അഥവാ (athavā) അവൻ വന്നാൽ, if he happens to come.

The word, taken apart.
അഥവാ (athavā) is borrowed from Sanskrit athavā, "or" (CDIAL 231), from atha, "then", and vā, "or".

Lesson 4 of 6.
പൊതുവേ (potuvē) — generally.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for on the contrary, then the Malayalam for or.

You'll want to know: പൊതുവേ.
പൊതുവേ — potuvē — "generally".
പൊതുവേ കുട്ടികൾക്ക് മധുരം ഇഷ്ടമാണ്. potuvē kuṭṭikaḷkkŭ madhuraṁ iṣṭamāṇŭ. Children generally like sweets.
The ending -ഏ makes an adverb: പൊതു, common, പൊതുവേ (potuvē), in general.

The word, taken apart.
പൊതുവേ (potuvē) is native Dravidian: പൊതു, "common, general" (DEDR 4507), with the adverb ending -ഏ.

Lesson 5 of 6.
എന്നുവെച്ചാൽ (ennuveccāl) — that means; that is.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Malayalam for or, then the Malayalam for generally.

You'll want to know: എന്നുവെച്ചാൽ.
എന്നുവെച്ചാൽ — ennuveccāl — "that means; that is".
നാളെ അവധിയാണ്, എന്നുവെച്ചാൽ ക്ലാസ് ഇല്ല. nāḷe avadhiyāṇŭ, ennuveccāl klāsŭ illa. Tomorrow is a holiday, which means there is no class.
In speech often എന്നു പറഞ്ഞാൽ, if one says so.

The word, taken apart.
എന്നുവെച്ചാൽ (ennuveccāl) is a native Malayalam compound: എന്ന്, "thus (said)" (DEDR 868), and വെച്ചാൽ, "if one puts", from വെക്കുക, "to put" (DEDR 5549): "if one puts it that way".
