Mandarin Chinese, chapter 418: Checking In.
6 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
zhíjī — to check in for a flight.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to declare goods at customs, then the Mandarin for duty-free.

You'll want to know: zhíjī.
zhíjī — "to check in for a flight".
wǒmen xiān zhíjī ránhòu qù ānjiǎn — First we check in, then we go through security.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhíjī is built from zhí 'to be on duty' and 机 'a plane', both inherited from Middle Chinese, driH and kj+j (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to hold' and 'to meet with'.

Lesson 3 of 6.
停飞 (tíngfēi) — to be grounded (of planes).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to check in for a flight, then the Mandarin for the formalities.

You'll want to know: 停飞.
停飞 — tíngfēi — "to be grounded (of planes)".
fēijī tíngfēi le wǒmen zuò gāotiě qù Běijīng — The planes are grounded: we're taking the high-speed train to Beijing.
The new character, in pieces: 停 (tíng) — eleven strokes in two pieces: on the left the person piece 亻; on the right a dot and a bar, a small 口, a short stroke down and a bar that hooks like a lid, a bar, and a vertical with a hook. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
停飞 (tíngfēi) is built from 停 (tíng) 'to stop' and 飞 'to fly', both inherited from Middle Chinese, deng and pj+j (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to rest, to stop' and the second 'to fly, to soar'.

Lesson 4 of 6.

[once you have stopped driving — read: each one aloud, with nothing said first — nǐ, gāo]

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — the person piece, then a tall right side.
停 (tíng).
Two pieces, left before right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, 亻, two strokes. The same two as in 你.
The right piece, nine strokes. A dot and a bar, then a small 口, three strokes: the top of 高. Under them a short stroke down and a bar that turns down and hooks: a lid. Then a bar across, and a vertical down the middle that hooks to the left at the foot.
2 + 9 = eleven.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 停 (tíng), all eleven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces does 停 (tíng) have? (Two.) Which comes first? (亻, the person piece.) How does the last stroke end? (With a hook to the left.)
Source: Hanzi Writer Data 停 (tíng), pinned stroke paths and medians.
