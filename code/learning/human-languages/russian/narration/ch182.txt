5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
теперь (tepér) — nowadays, now (after a change).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for April, then the Russian for May.

You'll want to know: теперь.
теперь (tepér) — "nowadays, now (after a change)".

The word, taken apart.
теперь (tepér) from older топерво, "only now": Vasmer explains it from тот, "that", with первый, "first".

Lesson 2 of 5.
каникулы (kaníkuly) — school holidays.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for May, then the Russian for nowadays.

You'll want to know: каникулы.
каникулы (kaníkuly) — "school holidays".

The word, taken apart.
каникулы (kaníkuly) probably came through Polish kanikuła from Latin canīcula, "little dog", the star Sirius: diēs canīculārēs, "the dog days", the hottest weeks of the year (Vasmer).

Lesson 3 of 5.
день рождения (den rozhdéniya) — a birthday.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for nowadays, then the Russian for school holidays.

You'll want to know: день рождения.
день рождения (den rozhdéniya) — "a birthday".

The word, taken apart.
день рождения (den rozhdéniya) is "the day of birth": день, "day", with рождения, a form of рождение, "birth", from родить, "to give birth", and род, "kin". The -жд- is Church Slavonic.

Lesson 4 of 5.
выходной (vykhodnóy) — a day off.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for school holidays, then the Russian for a birthday.

You'll want to know: выходной.
выходной (vykhodnóy) — "a day off".

The word, taken apart.
выходной (vykhodnóy) is short for выходной (vykhodnóy) день, "a day off", from выход, "a going out": вы-, "out", with ход, "a going".
