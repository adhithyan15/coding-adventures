Spanish, chapter 656: En la aduana.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el aduanero — the customs officer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to feel sick, then the Spanish for the waistcoat.

You'll want to know: el aduanero.
el aduanero — "the customs officer".
El aduanero abre mi maleta. The customs officer opens my suitcase.
A woman is la aduanera. As an adjective: el control aduanero.

The word, taken apart.
aduanero is built on aduana "customs", which comes from Andalusian Arabic ad-dīwān "the office", Arabic dīwān (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
el visado — the visa.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the customs officer, then the Spanish for customs.

You'll want to know: el visado.
el visado — "the visa".
Para este país necesitas visado. You need a visa for this country.
Spain says el visado; Latin America says la visa.

The word, taken apart.
visado is built on visar "to endorse, to stamp as seen", which is usually linked to visa; Spanish visa was borrowed from French visa, from Latin vīsa "things seen" (EtymDB), built on vidēre "to see".

Lesson 4 of 5.
sellar — to stamp; to seal.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for customs, then the Spanish for the visa.

You'll want to know: sellar.
sellar — "to stamp; to seal".
En la frontera me sellaron el pasaporte. They stamped my passport at the border.
Sellar un sobre — to seal an envelope.

The word, taken apart.
sellar is inherited from Vulgar Latin sigellāre, Latin sigillāre "to seal" (EtymDB), from sigillum "a seal", a little signum "sign".

Lesson 5 of 5.
registrar — to search; to register, to record.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the visa, then the Spanish for to stamp.

You'll want to know: registrar.
registrar — "to search; to register, to record".
La policía registró el coche. The police searched the car.
Registrarse is to check in or sign up: registrarse en el hotel.

The word, taken apart.
registrar is built on registro "register" (EtymDB), which comes from Medieval Latin registrum, from regesta "things recorded", built on gerere "to carry, to carry out".
