# encontrar — meeting, finding, and coming face to face

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

[PAUSE 2s] English "meet" covers two different events: seeing someone you know,
and meeting someone for the very first time. Portuguese keeps them apart, and it
has already taught you half the answer.

## You'll want to know: A plain -ar verb with two jobs
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-LEX-ENCONTRAR-02]; assesses=[] -->

| | |
|---|---|
| eu **encontro** | nós **encontramos** |
| tu **encontras** | vocês/eles **encontram** |
| ele/ela/você **encontra** | |

*Encontrar* is **to find** and **to run into** — one verb, because both are
coming upon something.

> **Encontro o livro.** — "I find the book."
> **Encontramo-nos no sábado ao meio-dia.** — "We are meeting on Saturday at
> midday."

That second sentence adds *nos*, "each other," and the *-s* of *encontramos*
drops out in front of it. Portugal hangs the little word on the back like that;
Brazil more often puts it in front: *nós nos encontramos*.

## Grammar Lens: Which meeting is which
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-GRAMMAR-ENCONTRAR-04]; assesses=[] -->

*Encontrar* meets someone you already know. For meeting a person for the **first
time**, Portuguese uses **conhecer** — the same verb that means being acquainted
with a person or a city:

> **Encontro o Pedro.** — "I am meeting Pedro." (You know him.)
> **Conheci o Pedro.** — "I met Pedro." (That was the first time.)

English collapses the two, and has to add "for the first time" when it matters.

## The word, taken apart: face to face
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[PT-ETYMON-ENCONTRAR-03]; assesses=[] -->

**encontrar** ← a Vulgar Latin ***incontrāre***, from *in* plus ***contrā***,
"facing, opposite, against." To meet is to end up **in front of** someone.

English borrowed the same verb through French as **encounter**, and shortened it
to **counter** — to counter is still to go against. The quiet one is **country**:
*(terra) contrāta*, "the land lying opposite," the ground spread out in front of
you.

And every encounter ends. The goodbyes of the fourth chapter go here: **até
logo**, **até amanhã**, **adeus**.

## Wrap-up Recall

[PAUSE 3s] Which verb meets a stranger for the first time, and which meets a
friend? (***Conhecer***; ***encontrar***.) Arrange it: "We are meeting on
Saturday at midday." (***Encontramo-nos no sábado ao meio-dia***.) Where did
*sábado* come from, and what does *meio-dia* say literally? (*Sabbatum*, the
**Sabbath**; "**mid-day**".) So which weekday is *segunda-feira*, and why?
(**Monday** — *domingo*, the Lord's day, counts as the first.) And what is inside
*encontrar*? (*In* + *contrā* — coming **face to face**; hence *encounter*, and
*country*.)
