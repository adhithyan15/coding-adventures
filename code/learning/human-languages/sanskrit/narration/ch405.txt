Sanskrit, chapter 405: Hurry and Speed.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
जवः (javaḥ) — swiftness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for takes cover, then the Sanskrit for deserted.

You'll want to know: जवः.
जवः — javaḥ — "swiftness".
अश्वस्य जवः महान् अस्ति। aśvasya javaḥ mahān asti. The horse's swiftness is great.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
जवः (javaḥ) comes from the root √ju, "to hasten"; MW gives java as speed, velocity, swiftness (MW).

Lesson 2 of 5.
सत्वरम् (satvaram) — in a hurry; hastily.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for deserted, then the Sanskrit for swiftness.

You'll want to know: सत्वरम्.
सत्वरम् — satvaram — "in a hurry; hastily".
यात्री सत्वरं स्थानकं गच्छति। yātrī satvaraṃ sthānakaṃ gacchati. The passenger goes to the station in a hurry.

The word, taken apart.
सत्वरम् (satvaram) is built from sa, "with", and tvarā, "haste", from the root √tvar, "to hurry"; MW gives satvara as making haste and its neuter as an adverb (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
प्रधावति (pradhāvati) — dashes off; rushes forward.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for swiftness, then the Sanskrit for in a hurry.

You'll want to know: प्रधावति.
प्रधावति — pradhāvati — "dashes off; rushes forward".
बालः लोकयानं प्रति प्रधावति। bālaḥ lokayānaṃ prati pradhāvati. The boy dashes off towards the bus.
Past (he …): सः लोकयानं प्रति प्रधावितवान्, saḥ lokayānaṃ prati pradhāvitavān.

The word, taken apart.
प्रधावति (pradhāvati) is built from pra, "forward", and the root √dhāv, "to run", which also gives dhāvati, "runs"; MW gives pra-dhāv as to run forward, run off or rush, with the participle pradhāvita, "run off, set out" (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
आयाति (āyāti) — arrives; comes.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for dashes off, then the Sanskrit for last.

You'll want to know: आयाति.
आयाति — āyāti — "arrives; comes".
अन्तिमं लोकयानं विलम्बेन आयाति। antimaṃ lokayānaṃ vilambena āyāti. The last bus arrives late.
Past (it …): लोकयानम् आयातवत्, lokayānam āyātavat.

The word, taken apart.
आयाति (āyāti) is built from ā, "towards", and the root √yā, "to go"; MW gives ā-yā as to come near, to arrive, with the participle āyāta (MW).
