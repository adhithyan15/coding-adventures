Lesson 1 of 5.
raeda, raedae — a travelling carriage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for unlucky, then the Latin for blessed.

You'll want to know: raeda, raedae.
raeda, raedae — "a travelling carriage".

The word, taken apart.
raeda came from Gaulish: Quintilian counts raeda among the Gallic words in Latin (Lewis & Short). Its Celtic root is the one for riding, a cousin of English ride.

Lesson 5 of 5.
amphitheātrum, amphitheātrī — an amphitheatre.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an aqueduct, then the Latin for the public baths.

You'll want to know: amphitheātrum, amphitheātrī.
amphitheātrum, amphitheātrī — "an amphitheatre".

The word, taken apart.
amphitheātrum was borrowed from Greek amphitheatron, "a double theatre" (Lewis & Short), from amphi, "around, on both sides", and theatron, "a theatre" (EtymDB 2.1): seats all the way round.
