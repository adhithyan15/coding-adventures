Lesson 1 of 7.
la antigüedad — antiquity, the ancient world; age.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the television news, then the Spanish for the incident.

You'll want to know: la antigüedad.
la antigüedad — "antiquity, the ancient world; age".
En la Antigüedad no había relojes como los de hoy. In ancient times there were no clocks like today's.
At work it means seniority: años de antigüedad. Una tienda de antigüedades sells antiques.

The word, taken apart.
antigüedad comes from Latin antīquitās, antīquitātem 'age, antiquity' (Lewis & Short), built on antīquus 'old'.

Lesson 2 of 7.
el presente — the present.

You'll want to know: el presente.
el presente — "the present".
Hay que vivir el presente. You have to live in the present.
As an adjective it means present: Estaba presente. In class, ¡Presente! — here!

The word, taken apart.
presente is a learned borrowing from Latin praesēns 'at hand, present' (Lewis & Short), built on prae 'before' and the verb esse 'to be'.

Lesson 3 of 7.
el porvenir — the future (what lies ahead).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for antiquity, then the Spanish for the present.

You'll want to know: el porvenir.
el porvenir — "the future (what lies ahead)".
Nadie sabe qué nos traerá el porvenir. Nobody knows what the future will bring us.
A little literary; it often means someone's prospects: un joven con porvenir.

The word, taken apart.
porvenir is built on por and venir 'to come', which is inherited from Latin venīre 'to come' (EtymDB). The future is what is still to come.

Lesson 4 of 7.
antiguo — old, ancient; former.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the present, then the Spanish for the future.

You'll want to know: antiguo.
antiguo — "old, ancient; former".
Vivimos en un edificio muy antiguo. We live in a very old building.
Before a noun it can mean former: mi antiguo jefe — my old boss.

The word, taken apart.
antiguo is inherited from Latin antīquus 'old, of former times' (EtymDB), through old Spanish antigo; it is built on ante 'before'.

Lesson 5 of 7.
antiguamente — in the old days, formerly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the future, then the Spanish for old.

You'll want to know: antiguamente.
antiguamente — "in the old days, formerly".
Antiguamente la gente viajaba a caballo. In the old days people travelled on horseback.
Usually with the imperfect: Antiguamente aquí había un río.

The word, taken apart.
antiguamente is built on antiguo and the ending -mente, from Latin mente 'with a mind'. Antiguo comes from Latin antīquus 'of former times' (EtymDB).
