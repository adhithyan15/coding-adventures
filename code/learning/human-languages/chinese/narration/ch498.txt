Lesson 1 of 6.
tǐyù — physical education (a school subject).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for knowledge, then the Mandarin for to go on to further study.

You'll want to know: tǐyù.
tǐyù — "physical education (a school subject)".
wǒ zuì xǐhuan de kè shì tǐyù — My favourite lesson is PE.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tǐyù is built from 体 'the body' and yù 'to rear', both inherited from Middle Chinese, thejX and yuwk (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'the body' and the second 'to nourish' and 'to grow'.

Lesson 2 of 6.
球场 (qiúchǎng) — sports ground; pitch; court.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to go on to further study, then the Mandarin for physical education.

You'll want to know: 球场.
球场 — qiúchǎng — "sports ground; pitch; court".
wǒmen zài qiúchǎng tī le yí ge xiàwǔ zúqiú — We played football on the pitch all afternoon.
The new character, in pieces: 球 (qiú) — eleven strokes in two pieces: on the left the jade piece of 理 lǐ, four strokes; on the right seven strokes, a bar, a long vertical with a hook, a dot and a rising stroke on the left, a short and a long falling stroke on the right, and a last dot at the top right. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
球场 (qiúchǎng) is built from qiú 'a ball' and 场 'a ground', both inherited from Middle Chinese, gjuw and drjang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'a threshing floor'. It glosses the first character 'a fine jade'; the sense 'a ball' belongs to a second character with the same sound, glossed 'a ball of hair to be struck', which modern writing spells the same way.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script — the jade piece, then seven strokes.
球 (qiú).
Two pieces, left before right.
The left piece, four strokes, as on the left of 理: two bars, a vertical through them, then a stroke rising to the right.
The right piece, seven strokes. A bar. A long vertical down through it that hooks at the foot. On the left, a dot and a stroke rising to the right. On the right, a short stroke falling to the left and a long stroke falling to the right. Last, the small dot at the top right.
4 + 7 = eleven.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 球 (qiú), all eleven, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces does 球 (qiú) have? (Two.) Which character you know has the same left piece? (理.) Which stroke comes last? (The small dot at the top right.)

Lesson 4 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for physical education, then the Mandarin for a sports pitch.

You'll want to know: 球门.
球门 — qiúmén — "goalmouth; goal (in football)".
他站在球门前边 tā zhàn zài qiúmén qiánbian — He's standing in front of the goal.

The word, taken apart.
球门 (qiúmén) is built from 球 (qiú) 'a ball' and 门 'a gate', both inherited from Middle Chinese, gjuw and mwon (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which says the character is made of two door leaves.
