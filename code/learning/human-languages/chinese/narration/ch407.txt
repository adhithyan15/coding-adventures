Mandarin Chinese, chapter 407: Pulling In.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
进站 (jìnzhàn) — to pull into the station (of a train or bus).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a motor car, then the Mandarin for a luggage rack.

You'll want to know: 进站.
进站 — jìnzhàn — "to pull into the station (of a train or bus)".
火车进站了现在检票 huǒchē jìnzhàn le xiànzài jiǎnpiào — The train's pulling in: they're checking tickets now.

The word, taken apart.
进站 (jìnzhàn) is built from 进 'to go forward, to go in' and 站 'a station', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsinH and treamH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription).

What you've built.
One more word for getting around.

ruǎnwò — "soft sleeper".
ruǎnwò bǐ yìngzuò guì — A soft sleeper costs more than a hard seat.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
ruǎnwò is built from ruǎn 'soft' and wò 'to lie down', both inherited from Middle Chinese, nywenX and ngwaH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'soft' and the second 'to sleep'.

Lesson 5 of 6.
dòngchē — D-class bullet train.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for an empty seat, then the Mandarin for the end of the line.

You'll want to know: dòngchē.
dòngchē — "D-class bullet train".
zuò dòngchē qù Běijīng yào duōjiǔ — How long does the bullet train take to Beijing?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dòngchē is built from dòng 'to move' and 车 'vehicle', both inherited from Middle Chinese, duwngX and tsyhae (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to stir, to move'. It is a short form of a longer name, a train whose carriages move under their own power.
