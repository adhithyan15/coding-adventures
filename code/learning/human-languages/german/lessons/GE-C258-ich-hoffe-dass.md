# ich hoffe, dass — hopes and dreams

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-FROH-01, GE-GRAMMAR-GEFUEHL-DASS-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I was glad the train came at last". (*Ich war froh, dass
der Zug endlich kam.*) That looks back. This lesson looks forward.

## Grammar Lens: ich hoffe, two ways
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-HOFFEN-DASS-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-C136-WUNSCH136-03, GE-GRAMMAR-DASS-VERB-ENDE-01, GE-LEX-BALD-01, GE-LEX-C139-VERB139-02] -->

**hoffen**, to hope, takes a *dass* clause like *finden* and *glauben*:

> *Ich hoffe, dass du bald* **kommst**. — I hope you come soon.

When the one hoping and the one doing are the same person, German can
drop *dass* and use **zu** with the infinitive at the end:

> *Ich hoffe, dich bald* **zu besuchen**. — I hope to visit you soon.

That *zu* is the German "to" of "to visit". It stands right before the
infinitive, and both go to the end.

## Why it's said this way: mein Traum ist es, … zu …
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-TRAUM-ZU-02]; assesses=[GE-LEX-C240-THING240-03, GE-LEX-C66-VERBEN3-02, GE-LEX-C148-VERB148-05] -->

For a bigger ambition, German builds a frame around the dream:

> *Mein Traum ist es, nach Japan* **zu reisen**. — My dream is to travel
> to Japan.

The *es* holds a place for the dream, and the comma opens it: *nach Japan
zu reisen*. The infinitive goes last, with *zu* in front of it.

With a separable verb, *zu* slips inside, between prefix and verb:
*mitnehmen*, **mitzunehmen**. *Ich hoffe, meine Familie mitzunehmen.*

You can say the same dream more plainly, too: *Ich möchte nach Japan
reisen.* The frame with *es* puts more weight on the dream.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-C240-THING240-03] -->

**Traum** ← Middle High German *troum* (EtymDB 2.1), which EtymDB sets
beside English *dream*: one word, by the usual account from Proto-Germanic
*\*draumaz*. **hoffen**, from chapter one hundred and thirty-six, goes back
to Middle High German *hoffen*, and EtymDB sets English *hope* and Dutch
*hopen* beside it; the usual account adds that the word reached High German
from Low German.
