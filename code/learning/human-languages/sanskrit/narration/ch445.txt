Sanskrit, chapter 445: Stars by Night and Day.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
उल्का (ulkā) — a shooting star; a meteor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for the dark fortnight, then the Sanskrit for gleams.

You'll want to know: उल्का.
उल्का — ulkā — "a shooting star; a meteor".
रात्रौ आकाशे उल्का पतति। rātrau ākāśe ulkā patati. At night a shooting star falls across the sky.
A feminine noun.

The word, taken apart.
उल्का (ulkā) comes from the root √uṣ, "to burn", to which MW refers it; MW gives ulkā as a fiery phenomenon in the sky, a meteor, a firebrand (MW).

ध्रुवः — dhruvaḥ — "the pole star".
ध्रुवः उत्तरायां दिशायां तिष्ठति। dhruvaḥ uttarāyāṃ diśāyāṃ tiṣṭhati. The pole star stands in the north.
A masculine noun.

The word, taken apart.
ध्रुवः (dhruvaḥ) comes from the root √dhṛ, "to hold, to bear", as MW gives it: MW gives dhruva as fixed, firm, and as the pole star, the star that does not move (MW).

Lesson 3 of 5.
ग्रहणम् (grahaṇam) — an eclipse.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a shooting star, then the Sanskrit for the pole star.

You'll want to know: ग्रहणम्.
ग्रहणम् — grahaṇam — "an eclipse".
अद्य सूर्यस्य ग्रहणं भविष्यति। adya sūryasya grahaṇaṃ bhaviṣyati. Today there will be an eclipse of the sun.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
ग्रहणम् (grahaṇam) is built on the root √grah, "to seize", which also gives gṛhṇāti, "takes": a seizing; MW gives grahaṇa as seizing and as an eclipse, the sun or moon being seized (MW).

Lesson 4 of 5.
दिवा (divā) — by day.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for the pole star, then the Sanskrit for an eclipse.

You'll want to know: दिवा.
दिवा — divā — "by day".
दिवा आकाशे सूर्यः अस्ति, नक्तं चन्द्रः। divā ākāśe sūryaḥ asti, naktaṃ candraḥ. By day the sun is in the sky, by night the moon.

The word, taken apart.
दिवा (divā) comes from div, "the sky, day", of which MW gives it as an old case form; the same word gives divya, "divine"; MW gives divā as by day, often set against naktam, "by night" (MW).

Lesson 5 of 5.
यापयति (yāpayati) — passes the time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an eclipse, then the Sanskrit for by day.

You'll want to know: यापयति.
यापयति — yāpayati — "passes the time".
वयं प्रपायां रात्रिं यापयामः। vayaṃ prapāyāṃ rātriṃ yāpayāmaḥ. We pass the night at the water shed.
Past (he …): सः तत्र रात्रिं यापितवान्, saḥ tatra rātriṃ yāpitavān.

The word, taken apart.
यापयति (yāpayati) is built on the root √yā, "to go", which gives yāti, "travels", in its causative, "makes go": to let time go by, to pass it (MW).
