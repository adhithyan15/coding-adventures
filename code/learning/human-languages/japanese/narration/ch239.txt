Japanese, chapter 239: Weekend Pastimes.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
おどる (odoru) — to dance.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a programme, then the Japanese for boring.

You'll want to know: おどる.
おどる — odoru — "to dance".
まつりで みんなと おどりました。 — matsuri de minna to odorimashita — "I danced with everyone at the festival."
Polite おどります, odorimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
おどる (odoru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.
さんぽ (sanpo) — a walk; a stroll.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to dance, then the Japanese for to go fishing.

You'll want to know: さんぽ.
さんぽ — sanpo — "a walk; a stroll".
けさ いぬと こうえんを さんぽしました。 — kesa inu to kōen o sanpo shimashita — "This morning I took the dog for a walk in the park."

The word, taken apart.
さんぽ (sanpo) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are san, "scatter" (Middle Chinese sanX) and po, "step, walk" (buH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 4 of 5.
てじな (tejina) — a magic trick; conjuring.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to go fishing, then the Japanese for a walk.

You'll want to know: てじな.
てじな — tejina — "a magic trick; conjuring".
おじさんは まつりで てじなを しました。 — ojisan wa matsuri de tejina o shimashita — "My uncle did magic tricks at the festival."

The word, taken apart.
てじな (tejina) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed: it is built from te, "hand", and shina, "goods, article", with sh voiced to j (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
