French, chapter 342: Évaluer et juger.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
évaluer — to evaluate, to assess.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the precision, then the French for to clear up.

You'll want to know: évaluer.
évaluer — "to evaluate, to assess".
Le professeur évalue notre travail chaque mois. The teacher assesses our work every month.
Évaluer le prix d'une maison — to estimate it.

The word, taken apart.
évaluer is usually explained as a French formation from é- and Old French value "worth", the old participle of valoir, which is inherited from Latin valēre "to be strong, to be worth" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
juger — to judge.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to clear up, then the French for to evaluate.

You'll want to know: juger.
juger — "to judge".
Il ne faut pas juger les gens sur leurs vêtements. You should not judge people by their clothes.
Nous jugeons, with an e to keep the g soft.

The word, taken apart.
juger is inherited from Latin iūdicāre "to pass judgement" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from iūdex "judge", itself built from iūs "law" and dīcere "to say".

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: le jugement.
le jugement — "the judgement".
J'ai confiance en ton jugement. I trust your judgement.
In court, un jugement is a ruling.

The word, taken apart.
jugement is inherited through Old French jugement from Late Latin iūdicāmentum (EtymDB), built on iūdicāre "to judge" and iūdex "judge" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
subjectif — subjective.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to judge, then the French for the judgement.

You'll want to know: subjectif.
subjectif — "subjective".
C'est un avis très subjectif. It is a very subjective view.
Feminine subjective: une impression subjective.

The word, taken apart.
subjectif comes from Latin subiectīvus (EtymDB), formed on subiectus, the participle of subicere "to throw under, to place under" (Lewis & Short), built on iacere "to throw".

Lesson 5 of 5.
impartial — impartial, fair.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the judgement, then the French for subjective.

You'll want to know: impartial.
impartial — "impartial, fair".
Un juge doit rester impartial. A judge must stay impartial.
Feminine impartiale, plural impartiaux.

The word, taken apart.
impartial is built from in- "not" and partial "taking sides", a learned formation on Latin pars, partis "part, side" (Lewis & Short).
