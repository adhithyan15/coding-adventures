Italian, chapter 382: Prima dell'esame.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ripassare — to revise, to go over (again).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the doctorate, then the Italian for the exam session.

You'll want to know: ripassare.
ripassare — "to revise, to go over (again)".
Stasera ripassiamo la storia per l'esame. Tonight we are going over history for the exam.
It also means to come by again: ripassa domani.

The word, taken apart.
ripassare is built from ri- "again" and passare "to pass", a verb inherited from Vulgar Latin passāre, built on Latin passus "a step" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
l'interrogazione — the oral test (at school); the questioning.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the exam session, then the Italian for to revise.

You'll want to know: l'interrogazione.
l'interrogazione — "the oral test (at school); the questioning".
Se domani c'è l'interrogazione di storia, stasera studio. If there is an oral history test tomorrow, I will study tonight.
At school the teacher questions one pupil aloud in front of the class: essere interrogato.

The word, taken apart.
interrogazione is a learned borrowing from Latin interrogātiō "a questioning, an examination" (EtymDB), from interrogāre "to question", built from inter "between" and rogāre "to ask".

Lesson 3 of 5.
memorizzare — to memorise, to learn by heart.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to revise, then the Italian for the oral test.

You'll want to know: memorizzare.
memorizzare — "to memorise, to learn by heart".
Devo memorizzare venti parole al giorno. I have to memorise twenty words a day.
On a phone, also to save: memorizzare un numero. Imparare a memoria is more colloquial.

The word, taken apart.
memorizzare is built on memoria "memory", a learned borrowing from Latin memoria (Lewis & Short), with the verb ending -izzare; the modern word was probably helped along by English memorize.

Lesson 4 of 5.
gli appunti — the notes (taken in class).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the oral test, then the Italian for to memorise.

You'll want to know: gli appunti.
gli appunti — "the notes (taken in class)".
Mi presti i tuoi appunti? Ieri ero malato. Can you lend me your notes? I was ill yesterday.
Prendere appunti — to take notes. The singular appunto also means a reproach; the adverb appunto means exactly.

The word, taken apart.
appunti is built on appuntare "to jot down, to pin", from a- and punto "point", which is inherited from Latin punctum "a prick, a point" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
il manuale — the textbook, the manual.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to memorise, then the Italian for the notes.

You'll want to know: il manuale.
il manuale — "the textbook, the manual".
Il manuale di storia ha cinquecento pagine. The history textbook has five hundred pages.
As an adjective: lavoro manuale — work with your hands.

The word, taken apart.
manuale is a learned borrowing from Latin manuālis "of the hand, held in the hand" (Lewis & Short), from manus "hand": a book to keep at hand.
