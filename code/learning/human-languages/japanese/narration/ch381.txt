Japanese, chapter 381: The Ferry Crossing.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ふなたび (funatabi) — a journey by boat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for dozing off, then the Japanese for a mouthful.

You'll want to know: ふなたび.
ふなたび — funatabi — "a journey by boat".
しまへの ふなたびは たのしかったです。 — shima e no funatabi wa tanoshikatta desu — "The boat trip to the island was fun."

The word, taken apart.
ふなたび (funatabi) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from funa, "boat" (from fune) and tabi, "journey"; its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).

Lesson 2 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a mouthful, then the Japanese for a journey by boat.

You'll want to know: こうろ.
こうろ — kōro — "a sea route; a course".
この こうろは なみが たかいです。 — kono kōro wa nami ga takai desu — "The waves are high on this sea route."

The word, taken apart.
こうろ (kōro) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are kō, "navigate" (Middle Chinese hang) and ro, "route" (luH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
