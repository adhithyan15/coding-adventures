Urdu, chapter 402: Long Journeys.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
مسافت (masāfat) — a distance travelled; a long way.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a bouquet, then the Urdu for to wave.

You'll want to know: مسافت.
مسافت — masāfat — "a distance travelled; a long way".
پہاڑوں تک کی مسافت لمبی ہے۔ pahāṛoṅ tak kī masāfat lambī hai. It is a long way to the mountains.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
مسافت (masāfat) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic masāfa, "a distance"; it is a cousin of مسافر and سفر.

Lesson 2 of 5.
رہنما (rahnumā) — a guide; a leader.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to wave, then the Urdu for a distance travelled.

You'll want to know: رہنما.
رہنما — rahnumā — "a guide; a leader".
پہاڑوں میں ایک رہنما ہمارے ساتھ تھا۔ pahāṛoṅ meṅ ek rahnumā hamāre sāth thā. In the mountains a guide went with us.
Masculine or feminine, by the person meant.

The word, taken apart.
رہنما (rahnumā) is usually traced to Persian rahnumā, "one who shows the way", built from rah, "a way" (a short form of راہ), and numā, "showing".

Lesson 3 of 5.
تھکانا (thakānā) — to tire (someone) out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a distance travelled, then the Urdu for a guide.

You'll want to know: تھکانا.
تھکانا — thakānā — "to tire (someone) out".
لمبے سفر نے سارا کو تھکا دیا۔ lambe safar ne sārā ko thakā diyā. The long journey wore Sara out.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے گھوڑے کو تھکایا, us ne ghoṛe ko thakāyā.

The word, taken apart.
تھکانا (thakānā) is built on تھکنا, "to get tired", which comes from sthakk, "stop, halt", a form CDIAL reconstructs (CDIAL 13737): this is the form for tiring someone else.

Lesson 4 of 5.
سلانا (sulānā) — to put (someone) to sleep.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a guide, then the Urdu for to tire (someone) out.

You'll want to know: سلانا.
سلانا — sulānā — "to put (someone) to sleep".
ماں نے گاڑی میں بچے کو سلایا۔ māṅ ne gāṛī meṅ bachche ko sulāyā. The mother got the child off to sleep in the car.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے بچے کو سلایا, us ne bachche ko sulāyā.

The word, taken apart.
سلانا (sulānā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin sulānā, "lulls to sleep", under Sanskrit svāpáyati (CDIAL 13928); it is the making-sleep partner of سونا.

Warm-up.

کنڈکٹر نے صبح سب مسافروں کو جگایا۔ kanḍakṭar ne subah sab musāfiroṅ ko jagāyā. In the morning the conductor woke all the passengers.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے سارا کو جگایا, us ne sārā ko jagāyā.

The word, taken apart.
جگانا (jagānā) is inherited: CDIAL lists Hindi jagānā beside jāgnā, "to wake", under Sanskrit jāgrati, "are awake" (CDIAL 5175).
