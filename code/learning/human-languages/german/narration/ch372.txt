German, chapter 372: Etwas vereinbaren.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
vereinbaren — to agree on, to arrange (formally).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the rejection, then the German for the confirmation.

You'll want to know: vereinbaren.
vereinbaren — "to agree on, to arrange (formally)".
Wir haben einen Termin für Dienstag vereinbart. We have arranged an appointment for Tuesday.
Inseparable: wir vereinbaren, wir haben vereinbart.

The word, taken apart.
vereinbaren is inherited from Middle High German vereinbæren (EtymDB), built on einbære "united", from ein "one", Proto-Germanic ainaz: two sides become one.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ausmachen — to arrange (informal); to switch off; to matter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for to agree on, then the German for the arrangement.

You'll want to know: ausmachen.
ausmachen — "to arrange (informal); to switch off; to matter".
Wir haben ausgemacht, dass wir uns um acht treffen. We arranged to meet at eight.
Separable: wir machen aus, wir haben ausgemacht. Also to switch off: das Licht ausmachen; and to matter: Das macht nichts aus — that does not matter.

The word, taken apart.
ausmachen is built from aus "out" and machen "to make" (EtymDB): you make a plan out between you.

Lesson 4 of 5.
der Zeitplan — the schedule.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the arrangement, then the German for to arrange.

You'll want to know: der Zeitplan.
der Zeitplan — "the schedule".
Wir sind gut im Zeitplan: Das Haus ist im Juni fertig. We are well on schedule: the house will be finished in June.
Plural: die Zeitpläne. For trains it is der Fahrplan.

The word, taken apart.
Zeitplan joins Zeit "time", Proto-Germanic tīdiz (EtymDB), to Plan, borrowed from French plan and going back to Latin plānus "level, flat" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): a drawing laid out flat.
