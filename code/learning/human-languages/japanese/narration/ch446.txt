5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
くしゃみ (kushami) — a sneeze.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for nausea, then the Japanese for a bruise.

You'll want to know: くしゃみ.
くしゃみ — kushami — "a sneeze".
かぜで くしゃみを しました。 — kaze de kushami o shimashita — "I sneezed because of my cold."

The word, taken apart.
くしゃみ (kushami) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; JMdict writes it in kana (JMdict).

Lesson 3 of 5.
さむけ (samuke) — a chill; the shivers.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a sneeze, then the Japanese for a runny nose.

You'll want to know: さむけ.
さむけ — samuke — "a chill; the shivers".
ねつが あって、さむけが します。 — netsu ga atte, samuke ga shimasu — "I have a fever and the shivers."

The word, taken apart.
さむけ (samuke) is a mixed word: native samu, "cold", the stem of samui, joined to Sino-Japanese ke, "spirit, feeling" (Middle Chinese khj+jH), a reading borrowed from Chinese (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 5.
たいちょう (taichō) — physical condition; shape.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a chill, then the Japanese for gargling.

You'll want to know: たいちょう.
たいちょう — taichō — "physical condition; shape".
せんしゅうから たいちょうが よく ありません。 — senshū kara taichō ga yoku arimasen — "I have not been in good shape since last week."

The word, taken apart.
たいちょう (taichō) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are tai, "body" (Middle Chinese thejX) and chō, "harmonize" (dew) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
