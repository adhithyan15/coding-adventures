Urdu, chapter 499: Cotton and Thread.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
کپاس (kapās) — cotton (the plant).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a large clay pot, then the Urdu for to heat in a fire.

You'll want to know: کپاس.
کپاس — kapās — "cotton (the plant)".
کسان نے اس سال کپاس بوئی۔ kisān ne is sāl kapās boī. The farmer sowed cotton this year.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
کپاس (kapās) is inherited from Sanskrit karpāsa, "the cotton plant" (CDIAL 2877).

Lesson 2 of 5.
دھننا (dhunnā) — to card (cotton).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to heat in a fire, then the Urdu for cotton (the plant).

You'll want to know: دھننا.
دھننا — dhunnā — "to card (cotton)".
دادی نے رضائی کے لیے کپاس دھنی۔ dādī ne razāī ke liye kapās dhunī. Grandmother carded cotton for the quilt.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے کپاس دھنی, us ne kapās dhunī.

The word, taken apart.
دھننا (dhunnā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin dhunnā, "to beat, to scutch cotton", under Sanskrit dhūnōti, "shakes" (CDIAL 6846).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

چرخہ — charkhā — "a spinning wheel".
نانی شام کو چرخہ چلاتی تھیں۔ nānī shām ko charkhā chalātī thīṅ. Grandmother used to work the spinning wheel in the evening.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
چرخہ (charkhā) is usually traced to Persian charkha, "a wheel", built on charkh, "a wheel; the turning sky".

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to card (cotton), then the Urdu for a spinning wheel.

You'll want to know: کاتنا.
کاتنا — kātnā — "to spin (thread)".
وہ چرخے پر دھاگا کاتتی ہے۔ voh charkhe par dhāgā kāttī hai. She spins thread on the spinning wheel.
Past, with نے (he or she …): اس نے دھاگا کاتا, us ne dhāgā kātā.

The word, taken apart.
کاتنا (kātnā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin kātnā, "spins", under kartati, a form it reconstructs (CDIAL 2855).
