## You'll want to know: tuìcháo
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C445-SEA445-05]; assesses=[] -->

*tuìcháo* — "to ebb (of the tide)".

> *xiàwǔ tuìcháo de shíhou wǒmen qù hǎibiān zhǎo bèiké* — In the afternoon, when the tide goes out, we look for shells on the shore.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*tuìcháo* is built from *tuì* 'to draw back' and *cháo* 'the tide', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *thwojH* and *drjew* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to retreat' and the second as the tide.
