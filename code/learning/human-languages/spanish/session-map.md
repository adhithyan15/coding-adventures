# Session Map — Chapter 1 (Hola and Buenos Días)

How Chapter 1's five lessons compose into car-ride sessions, with the
spaced-repetition schedule. Mechanics in
[`HL00`](../../../specs/HL00-human-language-curriculum-framework.md).

Chapter 1 is built **atom-first**: teach the pieces, then assemble them.
*hola*, then *bien* (and its adjective *bueno/buena*), then *día* (and the
plural rule), then *buenos días* assembled from *bueno + días* with
agreement, then a practice lesson. Grammar arrives exactly where a word
needs it — plurals with *día*, agreement with *buenos días* — never
front-loaded.

Reviews are scheduled in **session-counts, not calendar days**: a word from
session *N* is resurfaced at *N+1*, *N+3*, *N+7*, *N+15*. Each lesson is a
few minutes, so a short commute can hold one or two.

## Session-by-session

| Session | Reviews due | New lesson(s) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | — (bootstrap) | `L01` hola |
| 2 | `L01` (N+1) | `L02` bien (+ bueno/buena) |
| 3 | `L01` (N+3), `L02` (N+1) | `L03` día (+ plurals) |
| 4 | `L02` (N+3), `L03` (N+1) | `L04` buenos días (assemble + agreement) |
| 5 | `L01` (N+7), `L03` (N+3), `L04` (N+1) | `L05` practice — hola & buenos días |

By session 5 the chapter's four words are all in the review rotation, and
`L05` recombines them. Review lessons (fresh-combination resurfacings, not
verbatim repeats) are generated from the due column as each session is
reached.

## What comes next (recursive)

Chapter 2 answers the question `L05` ends on: **"when someone says *buenos
días* to you, what do you say back?"** Responding opens its own small cycle
of atoms (how-are-you, likewise, and the answer *bien* you already have).
After that, the same atom-first pattern repeats for the other times of day:
*tarde* → *buenas tardes*, then *noche* → *buenas noches* — each time word,
then assembly. Sentences grow as the grammar accumulates piece by piece.
