Urdu, chapter 36: Letters With Marks of Their Own.

Lesson 1 of 4.
د (dāl) — the letter inside خدا.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: د and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write خ and چ once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say خدا (khudā), "God". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: د.
د — dāl, the sound d.
It is inside خدا (khudā). Like ا and و, which you already write, د (dāl) refuses to join leftward: it joins the letter before it, never the one after.

Writing: د.
1. start here: begin at the independent form's upper tip and descend through the folded shoulder.
2. turn left along the baseline without lifting — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 0. The pen never leaves the paper.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Zer o Zabar, Dāl, re, and wāw, independent د (dāl) handwriting animation and Dāl instructions (Northwestern University Libraries Open Textbook, 2026).

Lesson 2 of 4.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: ڑ and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write چ and د (dāl) once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say پڑھنا (paṛhnā), "to read". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ڑ.
ڑ — ṛe, an r made by curling the tongue back and flicking it forward.
It is inside پڑھنا (paṛhnā), "to read". It is ر with the same small curled-back mark you put on ٹ. Urdu uses one mark for one idea: tongue curled back.

Writing: ڑ.
1. start here: draw the independent re-series body downward.
2. without lifting, continue curving to the left.
3. after one lift, draw the small retroflex mark downward, back upward, and down again to close its loop — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 1.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Zer o Zabar, Fe, qaf, te, dal, and re, independent ڑ (ṛe) calligraphic and handwriting animations and Re instructions (Northwestern University Libraries Open Textbook, 2026).

Lesson 3 of 4.
ھ (do-chashmī he) — the two-eyed he inside ٹھیک.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: ھ and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write د (dāl) and ڑ (ṛe) once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say ٹھیک (ṭhīk), "fine, well". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ھ.
ھ — do-chashmī he, "two-eyed he".
It is inside ٹھیک (ṭhīk) and پڑھنا (paṛhnā). It almost never makes a sound of its own. It rides on the letter before it and adds a puff of breath: ٹ + ھ (do-chashmī he) is ṭh, ڑ (ṛe) + ھ (do-chashmī he) is ṛh. Two round loops, the "two eyes".

Writing: ھ.
1. start here: circle the right eye clockwise from the upper center.
2. continue down and left along the baseline without lifting.
3. without lifting, reverse at the left edge and rise around the left eye.
4. without lifting, close at the center and finish with the low leftward sweep — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 0. The pen never leaves the paper.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Zer o Zabar, Chhoṭī he, do-chashmī he, chhoṭī ye, baṛī ye, and punctuation, independent ھ calligraphic and handwriting animations and Aspiration with do-chashmī he instructions (Northwestern University Libraries Open Textbook, 2026).

Script you'll notice: گ.
گ — gāf, the sound g.
It opens گرمی (garmī), "heat". It is ک, which you already write, with a second slash above the first.

Writing: گ.
1. start here: draw the independent stem downward.
2. flow right to left through the flatter bowl and finish with the hook without lifting.
3. after one lift, draw the long slash down from the upper right toward the stem.
4. after another lift, draw the shorter floating slash above the first — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 2.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Zer o Zabar, Pe, gāf, alif, and lām, independent گ calligraphic and handwriting animations and Gāf instructions (Northwestern University Libraries Open Textbook, 2026).
