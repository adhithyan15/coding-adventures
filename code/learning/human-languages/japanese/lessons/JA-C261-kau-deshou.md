# 〜たら … でしょう (tara … deshō) — what would happen

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[JA-GRAMMAR-C260-TARA-01, JA-LEX-C141-DESHOU, JA-GRAMMAR-C254-OPINION-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "if I have time, I will see a film". (*jikan ga attara, eiga o mimasu*.) Then "it will probably rain tomorrow". (*ashita ame ga furu deshō*.)

## You'll want to know: -たら … でしょう
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[JA-GRAMMAR-C261-WOULD-01]; assesses=[JA-LEX-C41-SCHOOL41-04, JA-LEX-C26-FAMILY26-04, JA-LEX-CHICHI] -->

> **おかねが あったら、ちちは かうでしょう。**
>
> *okane ga attara, chichi wa kau deshō.* — "If he had the money, my father would buy it."

Japanese has no separate form for "would". The **-たら** clause supposes, and **でしょう** makes the result a guess: "if there were money, he would probably buy it". The same sentence, said about a real plan, is "if he has the money, he will buy it"; the situation tells you which.

> **じかんが あったら、ははは くるでしょう。**
>
> *jikan ga attara, haha wa kuru deshō.* — "If she had time, my mother would come."

## You'll want to know: -たら … と おもいます
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[JA-GRAMMAR-C261-WOULD-02]; assesses=[JA-LEX-C28-VERBS28-04, JA-LEX-C26-FAMILY26-03] -->

For yourself, **と おもいます** does the same job:

> **じかんが あったら、いくと おもいます。**
>
> *jikan ga attara, iku to omoimasu.* — "If I had time, I think I would go."

> **おかねが あったら、かうと おもいます。**
>
> *okane ga attara, kau to omoimasu.* — "If I had the money, I think I would buy it."

What would have happened in the past, said with regret, needs other forms, which come later.
