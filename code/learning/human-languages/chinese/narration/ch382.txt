6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
zhíbān — to be on duty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for nationwide, then the Mandarin for a sense of security.

You'll want to know: zhíbān.
zhíbān — "to be on duty".
jīntiān wǎnshang wǒ zhíbān — I'm on duty tonight.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
zhíbān is built from zhí 'to meet, to hold' and bān 'a turn', both inherited from Middle Chinese, driH and paen (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to hold' and 'to meet with' and the second 'to hand out tokens'. The sense 'a shift' is not given there.

Lesson 2 of 6.
保安 (bǎo'ān) — security guard.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a sense of security, then the Mandarin for to be on duty.

You'll want to know: 保安.
保安 — bǎo'ān — "security guard".
小区门口有两个保安 xiǎoqū ménkǒu yǒu liǎng ge bǎo'ān — There are two security guards at the gate of the estate.
The new character, in pieces: 保 (bǎo) — nine strokes in three pieces: the person piece 亻, then 口 high on the right over a tree of four strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
保安 (bǎo'ān) is built from 保 'to keep' and 安 'calm', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pawX and 'an (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to guard' and 'to keep safe': keeping the peace, and then the person who does it.

Script — the person piece, then 口 over a tree.
保 (bǎo).
Three pieces: the person piece on the left, then 口 over a tree on the right.
The person piece 亻, two strokes, as in 你 and 他.
口, three strokes, high on the right: a short vertical, a stroke that runs right and turns down, then the bar that closes the foot.
The tree under it, four strokes. A long bar. A vertical down through it. A stroke falling to the left from the crossing, then one falling to the right.
2 + 3 + 4 = nine.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 保 (bǎo)? (Nine.) What sits over the tree? (口.) Which piece comes first? (亻, the person piece.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
jiānkòng — security camera; CCTV.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to be on duty, then the Mandarin for a security guard.

You'll want to know: jiānkòng.
jiānkòng — "security camera; CCTV".
yīnwèi yǒu jiānkòng suǒyǐ xiǎoqū lǐ hěn ānquán — There are cameras, so the estate is safe.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiānkòng is built from jiān 'to watch over' and kòng 'to pull', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kaem and khuwngH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to look down on and inspect' and the second 'to draw' and 'to report'. The camera is a modern sense.

xúnluó is built from xún 'to go round' and luó 'a patrol', both inherited from Middle Chinese, zwin and laH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to go about looking' and the second 'roving soldiers'.
