Latin, chapter 319: Vigilēs.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
sīphō, sīphōnis — a fire pump; a siphon.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to smoke, then the Latin for to go up in flames.

You'll want to know: sīphō, sīphōnis.
sīphō, sīphōnis — "a fire pump; a siphon".
Oppidum nūllum sīphōnem habēbat. The town had no fire pump.
Masculine, 3rd declension, a Greek word. A Roman governor once wrote that a burning town had no sīphō and no buckets.

The word, taken apart.
sīphō was borrowed from Greek siphōn, "a pipe" (Lewis & Short): a small pipe, a drinking tube, and a fire engine. English siphon comes from the same Greek word.

Lesson 3 of 5.
restinguō, restinguere — to put out, to quench.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a fire pump, then the Latin for a fire bucket.

You'll want to know: restinguō, restinguere.
restinguō, restinguere — "to put out, to quench".
Vigilēs incendium aquā restinxērunt. The watchmen put out the fire with water.
restinguō, restinguere, restinxī, restinctum: close to exstinguō (taught). Also of thirst: sitim restinguere.

The word, taken apart.
restinguō is built on an old verb stinguō, "to quench", with re- (Lewis & Short); exstinguō is built on the same verb.

Lesson 4 of 5.
excubiae, excubiārum — the watch, guard duty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a fire bucket, then the Latin for to put out.

You'll want to know: excubiae, excubiārum.
excubiae, excubiārum — "the watch, guard duty".
Vigilēs per tōtam noctem excubiās agēbant. The watchmen kept watch all night long.
Feminine, plural only: lying out of doors on guard. Excubiās agere: to keep watch.

The word, taken apart.
excubiae is built on ex, "out", and cubō, "to lie down" (Lewis & Short): a lying out on guard.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ambūrō, ambūrere — to scorch, to singe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to put out, then the Latin for the watch.

You'll want to know: ambūrō, ambūrere.
ambūrō, ambūrere — "to scorch, to singe".
Ignis manūs eius ambussit, sed vīvus fūgit. The fire scorched his hands, but he got away alive.
ambūrō, ambūrere, ambussī, ambustum: to burn all round, not right through.

The word, taken apart.
ambūrō is built on ūrō, "to burn", with amb-, "around" (EtymDB): to burn round the edges.
