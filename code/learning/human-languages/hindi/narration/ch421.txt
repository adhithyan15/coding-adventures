Hindi, chapter 421: Settlements and Towns.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
बस्ती (bastī) — a settlement; a poor quarter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to roll, then the Hindi for to dig into.

You'll want to know: बस्ती.
बस्ती — bastī — "a settlement; a poor quarter".
नदी के पार एक छोटी बस्ती है। nadī ke pār ek choṭī bastī hai. There is a small settlement across the river.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
बस्ती (bastī) is usually traced to Sanskrit vasati, "staying, a dwelling-place" (Monier-Williams), from the root vas, "to dwell". A बस्ती can be a hamlet or a crowded poor quarter of a city.

Lesson 2 of 6.
क़स्बा (qasbā) — a small town.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for to dig into, then the Hindi for a settlement.

You'll want to know: क़स्बा.
क़स्बा — qasbā — "a small town".
मेरे नाना एक छोटे क़स्बे में रहते हैं। mere nānā ek choṭe qasbe meṁ rahte haiṁ. My grandfather lives in a small town.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
क़स्बा (qasbā) is usually traced to Arabic qaṣaba, "a chief town, a fortress", which came through Persian; in Hindi a क़स्बा is bigger than a village and smaller than a city.

Lesson 4 of 6.
पड़ोस (paṛos) — the neighbourhood; next door.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a small town, then the Hindi for a resident.

You'll want to know: पड़ोस.
पड़ोस — paṛos — "the neighbourhood; next door".
हमारे पड़ोस में एक नई दुकान खुली है। hamāre paṛos meṁ ek naī dukān khulī hai. A new shop has opened in our neighbourhood.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
पड़ोस (paṛos) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit prativeśa, "neighbouring; a neighbour" (CDIAL 8598), which lists Hindi paṛos, "neighbourhood". पड़ोसी, a neighbour, is built on it.

Lesson 5 of 6.
देहात (dehāt) — the countryside; villages.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Hindi for a resident, then the Hindi for next door.

You'll want to know: देहात.
देहात — dehāt — "the countryside; villages".
मेरे दादा देहात में रहते हैं। mere dādā dehāt meṁ rahte haiṁ. My grandfather lives in the countryside.
Masculine.

The word, taken apart.
देहात (dehāt) is usually traced to Persian dehāt, "villages", the plural of deh, "a village"; in Hindi it is the countryside as a whole, set against the town.
