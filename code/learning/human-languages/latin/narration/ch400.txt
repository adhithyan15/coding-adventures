Latin, chapter 400: Teaching.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
doceō, docēre — to teach.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to deceive, then the Latin for to call to witness.

You'll want to know: doceō, docēre.
doceō, docēre — "to teach".
Magister puerōs litterās docet. The teacher teaches the boys their letters.
doceō, docēre, docuī; its participle follows in a later lesson. Two accusatives, the person and the thing: puerōs litterās docet. English doctor, first a teacher, and docile.

The word, taken apart.
doceō is inherited from Proto-Italic dokeō (EtymDB), usually taken as a causative beside decet "it is fitting": to make someone take a thing in. English doctor and doctrine come from it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
īnstituō, īnstituere — to train up, to instruct; to set up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to teach, then the Latin for to educate.

You'll want to know: īnstituō, īnstituere.
īnstituō, īnstituere — "to train up, to instruct; to set up".
Puerōs ad virtūtem īnstituēbat. He trained the boys in virtue.
īnstituō, īnstituere, īnstituī, īnstitūtum, in + statuō: to set up, then to set a young mind in order. Īnstitūtum is its noun.

The word, taken apart.
īnstituō is built from in and statuō "to set, to place" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), the vowel weakened inside the compound. English institute comes from it.

Lesson 5 of 5.
praecipiō, praecipere — to instruct, to advise; to take beforehand.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to train up, then the Latin for to equip.

You'll want to know: praecipiō, praecipere.
praecipiō, praecipere — "to instruct, to advise; to take beforehand".
Medicus mihi praecēpit ut quiēscerem. The doctor told me to rest.
praecipiō, praecipere, praecēpī; prae + capiō: to take beforehand, then to lay down a rule: praecipiō tibi ut, I instruct you to. English precept.

The word, taken apart.
praecipiō is built from prae "before" and capiō "to take" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short): to take in advance, then to tell in advance. English precept comes from it.
