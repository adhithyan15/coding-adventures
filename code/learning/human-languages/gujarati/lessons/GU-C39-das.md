# દસ (das) — ten, and one word with two spellings

## You'll want to know: દસ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GU-LEX-DAS]; assesses=[GU-SCRIPT-DA-01, GU-SCRIPT-SA-01, GU-LEX-NAV, GU-LEX-AATH, GU-LEX-NUMBERS-ONE-TO-FIVE] -->

> **દસ** — *das* — **ten**

**દ** then **સ**. Two consonants, no signs, nothing new.

**એક, બે, ત્રણ, ચાર, પાંચ, છ, સાત, આઠ, નવ, દસ.**

Ten words, and the count is closed at last.

## The word, taken apart — the same number, spelled twice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GU-LEX-DAS, GU-SCRIPT-SHA-01, GU-SCRIPT-SA-01] -->

Wiktionary hands **દસ** down from Sauraseni Prakrit *dasa*, and that from
Sanskrit *dáśa*. Sanskrit's *ś* — the sound of **શ** — arrives in Gujarati as
plain **સ**.

Then the entry adds one more line, and it is the interesting one: **દસ** is a
**doublet** of **દશ**.

| spelling | said |
|---|---|
| **દસ** | *das* |
| **દશ** | *daś* |

One Sanskrit word, two Gujarati words, both alive. The everyday one wore the
sibilant down; the other kept the **શ** the Sanskrit had. You have held both
letters for a long time, so you can read either without being told which is
which — and that is worth knowing before you meet the second one printed
somewhere.
