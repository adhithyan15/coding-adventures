Portuguese, chapter 344: Avaliar e julgar.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
avaliar — to evaluate, to assess.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to stress, then the Portuguese for to point out.

You'll want to know: avaliar.
avaliar — "to evaluate, to assess".
O professor avalia os alunos todas as semanas. The teacher assesses the pupils every week.
Also to value: avaliar uma casa — to value a house.

The word, taken apart.
avaliar is built on valia "value, worth", from the verb valer, which is inherited from Latin valēre "to be strong, to be worth" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
o juízo — the judgement; good sense.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to point out, then the Portuguese for to evaluate.

You'll want to know: o juízo.
o juízo — "the judgement; good sense".
Ele tem pouco juízo: conduz depressa demais. He has little sense: he drives too fast.
Ter juízo — to be sensible; perder o juízo — to lose your mind.

The word, taken apart.
juízo comes from Latin iūdicium "a judicial investigation, a judgement" (Lewis & Short), built on iūdex "judge"; Spanish juicio and Italian giudizio are its cousins (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
julgar — to judge; to think, to believe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to evaluate, then the Portuguese for the judgement.

You'll want to know: julgar.
julgar — "to judge; to think, to believe".
Não é justo julgar uma pessoa pela roupa. It is not fair to judge a person by their clothes.
Also to think: julgo que sim — I think so.

The word, taken apart.
julgar is inherited from Latin iūdicāre "to judge, to pass sentence" (EtymDB), built on iūdex "judge" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
objetivo — objective, impartial; the aim.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the judgement, then the Portuguese for to judge.

You'll want to know: objetivo.
objetivo — "objective, impartial; the aim".
Um juiz tem de ser objetivo. A judge has to be objective.
As a noun, o objetivo is the aim: o meu objetivo é aprender.

The word, taken apart.
objetivo is a learned borrowing from Medieval Latin obiectīvus (EtymDB), built on obiectum "a thing set before (the mind)", from obicere, on iacere "to throw" (Lewis & Short).
