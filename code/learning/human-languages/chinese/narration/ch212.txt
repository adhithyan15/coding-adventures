Mandarin Chinese, chapter 212: Services in Town.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
dǎyìn — to print out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for map, then the Mandarin for everywhere.

You'll want to know: dǎyìn.
dǎyìn — "to print out".
wǒ xiǎng dǎyìn liǎng zhāng zhàopiàn — I'd like to print two photos.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dǎyìn is built from 打 'to strike' and yìn 'a seal', both inherited from Middle Chinese, taengX and 'jinH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second as a seal of office.

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 5.
chōngzhí — to top up (a card or a phone).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to print out, then the Mandarin for to photocopy.

You'll want to know: chōngzhí.
chōngzhí — "to top up (a card or a phone)".
nǐ de shǒujī chōngzhí le ma — Have you topped up your phone?
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chōngzhí is built from chōng 'to fill' and zhí 'a value', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tsyhuwng and driH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'full' and the second 'to hold' and 'to match'. The dictionary gives the second a departing tone, so the rising tone of zhí is not the regular outcome, and how it came is not settled.
