# Unit 18: Adjective Agreement

## Warm-up

[PAUSE 2s] Say *el problema* and *la mano*. Now: an adjective describing
either of those has to match — today's unit is that matching rule.

## New Material

**alto / alta** — tall
**pequeño / pequeña** — small
> ← Latin *altus*, *pinnaculum*-family diminutive roots respectively.
> *alto* is also the singing-voice term in English (literally "high").

**grande** — big
> ← Latin *grandis* (→ English *grand*, *grandiose*). One of a small set
> of Spanish adjectives with the **same form for both genders** — *grande*
> doesn't change, unlike *alto/alta*.

## Grammar Lens

Spanish adjectives **agree in gender and number with the noun they
describe** — *el libro alto* (masc.), *la casa alta* (fem.), *los libros
altos* (masc. pl.), *las casas altas* (fem. pl.). English adjectives never
change form at all ("tall book," "tall house," "tall books" — *tall*
stays *tall* no matter what). This is the same agreement logic from
Unit 16's articles, just extended to a second word type — once a noun's
gender is set, everything describing it has to match, article and
adjective alike. A handful of adjectives (*grande*, and others ending in
*-e* or a consonant, like *feliz*, "happy") don't change by gender at all,
only by number (*grande/grandes*) — worth noting as the exception, not the
rule.
