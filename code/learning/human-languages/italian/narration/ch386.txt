Italian, chapter 386: Infrangere le regole.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
violare — to violate, to break (a rule).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to authorise, then the Italian for the authorisation.

You'll want to know: violare.
violare — "to violate, to break (a rule)".
Chi viola le regole paga una multa. Anyone who breaks the rules pays a fine.
Stress: vìolo.

The word, taken apart.
violare is a learned borrowing from Latin violāre "to treat with violence, to break" (Lewis & Short), related to vīs "force".

What you've built.

Lesson 3 of 6.
l'infrazione — the offence, the infringement (of a rule).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to violate, then the Italian for the violation.

You'll want to know: l'infrazione.
l'infrazione — "the offence, the infringement (of a rule)".
Passare con il rosso è un'infrazione grave. Going through a red light is a serious offence.
A breach of a rule rather than a crime: traffic and sport rules.

The word, taken apart.
infrazione is a learned borrowing from Latin īnfrāctiō "a breaking" (Lewis & Short), from īnfringere "to break in", built on frangere "to break".

Lesson 4 of 6.
trasgredire — to break (a rule), to transgress.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the violation, then the Italian for the offence.

You'll want to know: trasgredire.
trasgredire — "to break (a rule), to transgress".
Da ragazzo mi piaceva trasgredire le regole. As a boy I liked breaking the rules.
Conjugated with -isc-: trasgredisco. Trasgressivo — provocative, rule-breaking.

The word, taken apart.
trasgredire is a learned borrowing from Latin trānsgredī "to step across" (Lewis & Short), built from trāns "across" and gradī "to step": you step over the line.

Lesson 5 of 6.
l'eccezione — the exception.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the offence, then the Italian for to break.

You'll want to know: l'eccezione.
l'eccezione — "the exception".
Di solito non mangio dolci, ma oggi faccio un'eccezione. I do not usually eat sweets, but today I am making an exception.
Ad eccezione di — except for. Senza eccezioni — with no exceptions.

The word, taken apart.
eccezione is a learned borrowing from Latin exceptiō "a taking out, an exception" (EtymDB), from excipere "to take out", built on capere "to take".
