Lesson 1 of 5.
تقليد (taqlīd) — a tradition.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a fence, then the Arabic for a courtyard.

You'll want to know: تقليد.
تقليد — taqlīd — "a tradition".

The word, taken apart.
تقليد (taqlīd) is built on the native Arabic root q-l-d, "to hang on the neck; to follow another" (Lane). Taqlīd is the verbal noun of Form II, "following another's lead".

You'll want to know: ثقافة.
ثقافة — thaqāfa — "culture".

The word, taken apart.
ثقافة (thaqāfa) is built on the native Arabic root th-q-f, "to be skilful, to be sharp" (Lane). Thaqāfa is skill, cultivation, and in modern use culture; the same root gives thiqāf, a tool for straightening spears.

Lesson 3 of 5.
دين (dīn) — religion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a tradition, then the Arabic for culture.

You'll want to know: دين.
دين — dīn — "religion".

The word, taken apart.
دين (dīn) is listed by Lane under d-y-n with the senses recompense, obedience and religion. The sense of judgement is shared with Aramaic and Hebrew dīn, and some trace the sense "religion" to Middle Persian dēn; which account holds for "religion" is debated.

Lesson 4 of 5.
صلاة (ṣalāh) — prayer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for culture, then the Arabic for religion.

You'll want to know: صلاة.
صلاة — ṣalāh — "prayer".

The word, taken apart.
صلاة (ṣalāh) is listed by Lane under ṣ-l-w. It is usually traced to Aramaic ṣlōthā, "prayer"; Lane's sources say the related ṣalawāt, "synagogues", is from Hebrew.
