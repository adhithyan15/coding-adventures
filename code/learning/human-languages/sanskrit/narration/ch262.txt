Sanskrit, chapter 262: Us and you all as objects.
4 lessons. All 4 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 4.
अस्मान् (asmān) — us, several people, as the object.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
“Us two” is आवाम् (āvām). “Our” for several possessors is अस्माकम् (asmākam). Keep the asm- opening and make several people the object.

You'll want to know: अस्मान्.
अस्मान् (asmān) means “us” when several people including the speaker are the object.
सः अस्मान् पश्यति। saḥ asmān paśyati. He sees us.
Whitney §491 lists अस्मान् as the independent accusative plural. Do not confuse it with अस्माकम्, “our / of us”: asmān is a person reached by the action; asmākam answers “whose?”.

Taken Apart.
As a grammatical form, अस्मान् (asmān) comes from the first-person pronoun paradigm; it is not a word from a separate lexical root. Whitney's Sanskrit Grammar §491 records it as the independent accusative plural. This lesson makes no deeper historical derivation claim.
Source: Whitney, Sanskrit Grammar (1889), §491.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: “us two” as object — āvām]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: “us, several people” as object — asmān]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: “he sees us” — saḥ asmān paśyati]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — contrast: object asmān; possessor asmākam]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Several people including the speaker are seen. Which object form means “us”? (अस्मान्, asmān.)


Lesson 2 of 4.
युष्मान् (yuṣmān) — you, several people, as the object.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
“Us, several people” is अस्मान् (asmān). “Your” to several people is युष्माकम् (yuṣmākam). Keep the yuṣm- opening and make the several addressees the object.

You'll want to know: युष्मान्.
युष्मान् (yuṣmān) means “you” when several familiar addressees are the object.
सः युष्मान् पश्यति। saḥ yuṣmān paśyati. He sees you all.
Whitney §491 lists युष्मान् (yuṣmān) as the independent accusative plural. Keep it apart from युष्माकम्, “your / of you all”: the first is an object and the second is a possessor.

Taken Apart.
As a grammatical form, युष्मान् (yuṣmān) comes from the second-person pronoun paradigm; it is not a word from a separate lexical root. Whitney's Sanskrit Grammar §491 records it as the independent accusative plural. This lesson makes no deeper historical derivation claim.
Source: Whitney, Sanskrit Grammar (1889), §491.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: “he sees us” — saḥ asmān paśyati]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: “he sees you all” — saḥ yuṣmān paśyati]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: several people with the speaker — asmān; several addressees — yuṣmān]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — contrast: object yuṣmān; possessor yuṣmākam]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Several familiar addressees are seen. Which object form means “you all”? (युष्मान्, yuṣmān.)


Lesson 3 of 4.
Who is seen, and how many? — choose me, us two, us, you, you two, or you all.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Give the singular pair, then the dual pair, then the several-person pair. (Mām, tvām; āvām, yuvām; asmān, yuṣmān.)

Grammar Lens: person first, then number.
Choose in two small steps.
First ask who the object is:
first person includes the speaker;
second person is the familiar person or people addressed.
Then count the people:
one person: माम् / त्वाम् (mām / tvām);
two people: आवाम् / युवाम् (āvām / yuvām);
several people: अस्मान् / युष्मान् (asmān / yuṣmān).
The six are independent full forms. Choose person, choose number, then place the chosen object before the known verb.

Guided Practice.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[your turn — say: first person, one — mām; first person, two — āvām; first person, several — asmān]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: second person, one — tvām; second person, two — yuvām; second person, several — yuṣmān]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: “he sees us two” — saḥ āvām paśyati]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: “he sees you all” — saḥ yuṣmān paśyati]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What are the two choices you make before selecting the object form? (Person: speaker included or addressee; then number: one, two, or several.)


Lesson 4 of 4.
One, two, or several — me/us or you.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say the decision aloud: person first, then number. Do not recite a table; answer each scene separately.

Guided Practice — choose before you speak.
[pause 2 seconds after each]
[your turn — say: the speaker alone is seen — saḥ mām paśyati]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: one familiar addressee is seen — saḥ tvām paśyati]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the speaker and one companion are seen — saḥ āvām paśyati]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: two familiar addressees are seen — saḥ yuvām paśyati]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: several people including the speaker are seen — saḥ asmān paśyati]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: several familiar addressees are seen — saḥ yuṣmān paśyati]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Grammar Lens: the boundary of this chapter.
These six are the independent full forms taught here. Classical Sanskrit also has short alternatives in particular positions, and it has third-person and respectful object forms. Those belong to later lessons. This set is not the whole Classical Sanskrit pronoun system; it is the complete first- and second-person full-form choice across one person, two people, and several people.
Source for the full and short forms: William Dwight Whitney, Sanskrit Grammar (1889), §491.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Without a table, answer six prompts: me, you one, us two, you two, us several, you several. (Mām; tvām; āvām; yuvām; asmān; yuṣmān.)
