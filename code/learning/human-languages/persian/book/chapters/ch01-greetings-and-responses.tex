\chapter{Greetings and responses}
\label{ch:persian-greetings}

This chapter builds one miniature exchange in dependency order: greet, thank,
answer yes, answer no. Four short expressions are enough to establish the
direction of the script, joining, one non-joining letter, omitted short vowels,
and the contrast between borrowed and inherited vocabulary. Companion lessons:
\texttt{FA-C01-*.md}.

\section{\texorpdfstring{\fa{سلام}}{salam} \emph{(sal\^am)} --- hello through peace}

\begin{scriptstep}
Persian runs \textbf{right to left}. Read \fa{سلام} from its right edge:
\fa{س} $s$, \fa{ل} $l$, \fa{ا} long \emph{\^a}, \fa{م} $m$. Most letters join
inside a word. The tall \fa{ا} is a non-joiner on its left side, so the final
\fa{م} begins a new stroke group. No other letters are required yet.
\end{scriptstep}

\begin{rootweb}
\emph{Sal\^am} means ``hello'' and evokes peace. Persian borrowed it from
Arabic, whose consonant family \textbf{s--l--m} carries ideas of peace, safety,
and wholeness. Hebrew \emph{shalom} belongs to the same older Semitic family.
The word is borrowed history and completely ordinary modern Persian at once.
\end{rootweb}

\begin{usage}
Say \fa{سلام} to one person or a group. It is a neutral, friendly greeting.
Hear it, answer with the same word, and resist adding any grammar yet.
\end{usage}

\begin{checkpoint}
Read \fa{سلام} from right to left. Which letter carries the long \emph{\^a}?
Point to \fa{ا}, cover the romanization, and say \emph{sal\^am} once.
\end{checkpoint}

\section{\texorpdfstring{\fa{ممنون}}{mamnun} \emph{(mamnun)} --- thank you}

\begin{scriptstep}
Reuse final \fa{م} from \fa{سلام}, now at the right edge where reading begins.
The visible sequence is \fa{م} $m$, \fa{م} $m$, \fa{ن} $n$, \fa{و} long $u$,
\fa{ن} $n$. Persian usually omits short vowels, so the first $a$ is learned
with the whole word. Here \fa{و} carries the long $u$.
\end{scriptstep}

\begin{rootweb}
Persian received \emph{mamnun} from Arabic. It describes someone as grateful
or obliged and belongs to the Arabic \textbf{m--n--n} family associated with
favor or kindness. In current Persian conversation, the whole word is simply
a useful ``thanks.''
\end{rootweb}

\begin{usage}
Build the smallest exchange: A says \fa{سلام}; B answers \fa{سلام}; A adds
\fa{ممنون}. Treat each expression as one social move.
\end{usage}

\begin{checkpoint}
Which visible letter carries long $u$ in \fa{ممنون}? Point to \fa{و}, then say
\emph{sal\^am --- mamnun} without looking.
\end{checkpoint}

\section{\texorpdfstring{\fa{بله}}{bale} \emph{(bale)} --- yes}

\begin{scriptstep}
Read three letters: \fa{ب} $b$, \fa{ل} $l$, \fa{ه} $h$. The short $a$ and $e$
are supplied by the reader rather than written as separate letters. The final
\fa{ه} remains visible even though careful speech ends with a light vowel-like
sound rather than a forceful English $h$.
\end{scriptstep}

\begin{usage}
\emph{Bale} is a standard polite affirmative. A more casual answer exists, but
one reliable ``yes'' is enough now: \emph{bale, mamnun} --- yes, thank you.
\end{usage}

\begin{checkpoint}
Cover \emph{bale}. Read \fa{بله}; name the two short vowels that the writing
leaves implicit; answer ``yes'' once.
\end{checkpoint}

\section{\texorpdfstring{\fa{نه}}{na} \emph{(na)} --- no, and an inherited bridge}

\begin{scriptstep}
You already know final \fa{ه} from \fa{بله} and \fa{ن} from \fa{ممنون}. Put
\fa{ن} on the right and read \fa{نه}: $n$ plus an unwritten short $a$.
\end{scriptstep}

\begin{rootweb}
Unlike the two Arabic loans at the start of this chapter, Persian \emph{na}
continues an old Indo-Iranian negative family. Related forms occur across
Iranian and Indo-Aryan languages. This is the first bridge from Persian toward
Hindi and Urdu: not an instruction to learn those forms now, just a reason the
tiny word may feel familiar later.
\end{rootweb}

\begin{usage}
Contrast only the answer: \fa{بله} \emph{bale} --- yes; \fa{نه} \emph{na} ---
no. Ask yourself a harmless yes/no question and answer it both ways.
\end{usage}

\begin{checkpoint}
Without looking back, produce the four-step chain: hello, thank you, yes, no.
Then read \fa{سلام}, \fa{ممنون}, \fa{بله}, \fa{نه} in the same order.
\end{checkpoint}
