Japanese, chapter 363: Paying the Bill.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
かいけい (kaikei) — the bill; paying at the till.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for prices in general, then the Japanese for a rental deposit.

You'll want to know: かいけい.
かいけい — kaikei — "the bill; paying at the till".
しょくじが おわったら、かいけいを おねがいします。 — shokuji ga owattara, kaikei o onegai shimasu — "When we have finished eating, the bill, please."

The word, taken apart.
かいけい (kaikei) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are kai, "meeting" (Middle Chinese hwajH) and kei, "plan" (kejH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

What you've built.
One more word for saying what happens if.
