# (review)

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-DEMONSTRATIVE-THREE-DISTANCES] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Three words, three places, in order. (*Este*, *ese*, *aquel*.)

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-DEMONSTRATIVE-ESTE, ES-GRAMMAR-DEMONSTRATIVE-ESE, ES-GRAMMAR-DEMONSTRATIVE-AQUEL] -->

Three chants. Say each one, then say who it belongs to.

[PAUSE 2s] **First.** *Este libro* — **by me**. The word Latin used to point at
you, walked one step closer.

[PAUSE 2s] **Second.** *Ese libro* — **by you**. The word that used to mean
*self*, moved in when the first one left.

[PAUSE 2s] **Third.** *Aquel libro* — **by neither**. The word that also became
*el*, kept stressed and propped up so it could go on pointing.

## Grammar Lens: one question, asked twice
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-DEMONSTRATIVE-BEFORE-NOUN, ES-GRAMMAR-DEMONSTRATIVE-NEUTER] -->

Everything else here is two questions, in this order.

[PAUSE 2s] **Do I have a noun?** If no, use the *-o* form and stop: *esto*,
*eso*, *aquello*. Nothing to agree with, so nothing agrees.

[PAUSE 2s] **If yes — whose side is it on?** Then pick from the three, agree it
with the noun, and put it **in front**, in the article's place. Never *el este
libro*; the pointing word already did the article's job.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-ETYMON-ESE-IPSE, ES-ETYMON-AQUEL-ACCU-ILLE] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Two origins worth keeping. What did *ese* mean in Latin? (***Self***.)

[PAUSE 2s] And which everyday word is *aquel* related to? (***El*** — both from
*ille*, one worn down, one propped up.)
