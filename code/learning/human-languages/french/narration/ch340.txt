5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
fiable — reliable.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for indisputable, then the French for clear.

You'll want to know: fiable.
fiable — "reliable".
Cette voiture est vieille, mais très fiable. This car is old, but very reliable.
For people and things: une source fiable.

The word, taken apart.
fiable is built on se fier "to trust" (EtymDB), which is usually traced to a Vulgar Latin fīdāre, formed on Latin fīdere "to trust" (Lewis & Short).

crédible — "believable, credible".
Son histoire n'est pas très crédible. His story is not very believable.
One form for both genders. Close to croyable, but more formal.

The word, taken apart.
crédible is a learned borrowing from Latin crēdibilis "worthy of belief" (Lewis & Short), from crēdere "to believe".

Lesson 3 of 5.
douteux — doubtful, dubious.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for reliable, then the French for believable.

You'll want to know: douteux.
douteux — "doubtful, dubious".
Ce restaurant a l'air douteux: il n'y a personne. This restaurant looks dubious: there is nobody there.
Feminine douteuse. Il est douteux que takes the subjunctive.

The word, taken apart.
douteux is built from doute "doubt" and the suffix -eux (EtymDB). doute comes from douter, inherited from Latin dubitāre "to waver, to doubt" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
vraisemblable — likely, believable.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for believable, then the French for doubtful.

You'll want to know: vraisemblable.
vraisemblable — "likely, believable".
C'est une explication vraisemblable. It is a likely explanation.
Literally like the truth: vrai + semblable.

The word, taken apart.
vraisemblable is built from vrai "true" and semblable "like", on the model of Latin vērī similis "like the truth" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.
invraisemblable — unlikely, hard to believe.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for doubtful, then the French for likely.

You'll want to know: invraisemblable.
invraisemblable — "unlikely, hard to believe".
Il m'a raconté une histoire invraisemblable. He told me a story that was hard to believe.
With in-, the opposite of vraisemblable: so odd it is hard to believe.

The word, taken apart.
invraisemblable is built from in- "not" and vraisemblable, which is formed from vrai "true" and semblable "like", on the model of Latin vērī similis "like the truth" (Lewis & Short).
