Russian, chapter 24: Six to Ten, and the Pairs That Shaped Each Other.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
шесть (shest) — six.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Count one to five, then five to one.

You'll want to know first — one word.
шесть — shest — six.
One syllable, ending like пять in the soft sign.

The word, taken apart — six, sex, shest.
шесть (shest) continues Indo-European swéḱs, "six." English six, Latin sex and Greek héx are the same word.
Notice what survived: the s. Hold on to it, because the very last number in this slice is where that s turns into the fact that names half the Indo-European family.

Lesson 2 of 6.
семь (sem) — seven.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say шесть (shest) and its Latin cousin.

You'll want to know first — one word.
семь — sem — seven.

The word, taken apart — septem, seven, sem.
семь (sem) continues Indo-European septm, "seven." Latin septem, English seven, Greek heptá and Sanskrit saptá are the same word.
The p has gone and the s has stayed. That combination is worth marking, because the Iranian branch did the opposite: Persian's seven is haft, from the same word, with the s turned into an h. Two branches, one word, and each kept the piece the other lost.

Lesson 3 of 6.
восемь (vósem) — eight, and the seven inside it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say семь (sem), and which sound it lost that Latin kept.

You'll want to know first — one word.
восемь — vósem — eight.
Say семь and восемь (vósem) one after the other. Seven is audible at the end of eight, and the unstressed о at the front reduces towards a — the reduction this book named long ago.

The word, taken apart — a neighbour's ending.
восемь (vósem) continues Indo-European oḱtṓw, "eight" — Latin octō, Greek oktṓ, English eight.
There is no s and no m anywhere in that history, so neither is inherited. They came from семь (sem), standing next to it. Seven and eight are said together more often than almost any other pair of words in any language, and the pair pulls each other into shape: Persian's hasht borrowed its h- from haft for exactly the same reason, in a different family, with no contact between them.

Lesson 4 of 6.
девять (dévyat) — nine.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say восемь (vósem), and where its ending came from.

You'll want to know first — one word.
девять — dévyat — nine.

The word, taken apart — novem, nine, devyat.
девять (dévyat) continues Indo-European h1newn, "nine." Latin novem, English nine and Greek ennéa are the same word; the n at the front became a d in Slavic.
That change is not an accident either, and the next lesson is the reason: ten begins with a d in every branch, and nine stands directly in front of it.

Lesson 5 of 6.
десять (désyat) — ten, and the letter nine borrowed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say девять (dévyat) and which sound changed at its front.

You'll want to know first — one word.
десять — désyat — ten.
Say девять (dévyat) and десять (désyat) back to back. They differ in one consonant in the middle and nothing else, which makes them the pair this whole run is likeliest to confuse — so drill them together rather than in order.

The word, taken apart — decem, decimal, ten.
десять (désyat) continues Indo-European dekm, "ten." Latin decem — and through it decimal, December, decade — English ten and Greek déka are the same word.
The d here is the original one. девять (dévyat) copied it: nine stands in front of ten in every count anybody has ever said aloud, and it took its neighbour's first consonant, exactly as восемь (vósem) took its ending from семь two lessons ago. Two of the last four numbers in this chapter were shaped by the number standing beside them, which is what counting aloud does to words.

Lesson 6 of 6.
Payoff — one to ten.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Count one to five, then ask сколько.

Guided Practice.
Count aloud one to ten without a model, then ten to one.
Hear all ten named singly and out of order, and say each.
Read the ten printed shuffled and say each.
Write all ten from dictation. Not one new Cyrillic letter appears in any of them — this whole slice adds no sign to the alphabet.
Drill the two pairs that shaped each other: семь / восемь (vósem), where eight took seven's ending, and девять (dévyat) / десять (désyat), where nine took ten's d.
Answer сколько? ten times, once with each number.
Then say the English cousin of each. All ten have one.

Wrap-up Recall.
Source: Omniglot, Numbers in Russian.
[question — say your answer, then pause 22 seconds]
Count aloud from one to ten in Russian.
