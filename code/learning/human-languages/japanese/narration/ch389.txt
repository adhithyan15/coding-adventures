Lesson 1 of 6.
おおどおり (ōdōri) — a main street; an avenue.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for free of charge, then the Japanese for requiring a fee.

You'll want to know: おおどおり.
おおどおり — ōdōri — "a main street; an avenue".
おおどおりに ひゃっかてんが あります。 — ōdōri ni hyakkaten ga arimasu — "There is a department store on the main street."

The word, taken apart.
おおどおり (ōdōri) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from ō, "big" and dōri, "street" (from tōru); its kanji were attached later as kun'yomi (JMdict).

ろじ — roji — "an alley; a lane".
ろじに ちいさい みせが あります。 — roji ni chiisai mise ga arimasu — "There is a small shop in the alley."

The word, taken apart.
ろじ (roji) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are ro, "path" (Middle Chinese luH) and ji, "ground" (dijH) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 5 of 6.
ぬける (nukeru) — to get through (to the other side); to come out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a back street, then the Japanese for a footbridge (over a road).

You'll want to know: ぬける.
ぬける — nukeru — "to get through (to the other side); to come out".
ろじを ぬけて、えきに つきました。 — roji o nukete, eki ni tsukimashita — "I went through the alley and got to the station."
Polite ぬけます, nukemasu; an ichidan verb. Past (polite): くるまが ろじを ぬけました。, kuruma ga roji o nukemashita.

The word, taken apart.
ぬける (nukeru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict).
