# ചെവി (cevi) — "ear," where Kannada is the odd one out

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-CONCEPT-C37-KANNU-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The four-way match on *kaṇṇŭ* was close to perfect. This word
breaks the pattern — but not the way you'd expect.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-CONCEPT-C37-CHEVI-01]; assesses=[] -->

> **ചെവി** — *cevi* — **ear**

> **എന്റെ ചെവി.** — *ente cevi.* — "My ear."

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-CONCEPT-C37-CHEVI-02]; assesses=[ML-CONCEPT-C37-KANNU-02] -->

**ചെവി** continues **Proto-Dravidian \*kewi**, "ear." Tamil **செவி**
(*cevi*) and Telugu **చెవి** (*cevi*) match Malayalam almost exactly — all
three softened the old initial ***k-*** into a ***c-/ch-*** sound before
this particular front vowel. **Kannada alone kept the older sound**:
**ಕಿವಿ** (*kivi*), *k-* untouched. So this time it is not Malayalam
standing apart from three sisters, the way *kuṭumbaṁ*'s dressed *-aṁ* did —
it is Kannada, holding onto the sound the other three let go of.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-CONCEPT-C37-CHEVI-01, ML-CONCEPT-C37-CHEVI-02, ML-CONCEPT-C37-KANNU-01, ML-CONCEPT-C37-KANNU-02, ML-CONCEPT-C36-MAKAL-01, ML-CONCEPT-C36-MAKAL-02] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Say "my ear." (*Ente cevi*.) Which three sisters agree on this
word, and which one differs? (**Malayalam, Tamil, Telugu** agree — **Kannada**
alone keeps the older *k-* sound, *kivi*.) Why is that the reverse of what
you might expect? (Usually Malayalam is the one that goes its own way; here
Malayalam sides with the majority and **Kannada** is the outlier.) Which
earlier word was identical between Malayalam and Tamil, sound for sound?
(***മകൾ***, *makaḷ*, daughter.)
