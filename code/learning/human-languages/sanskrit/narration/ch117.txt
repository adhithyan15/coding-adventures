Lesson 1 of 5.
अंगुष्ठः (aṅguṣṭhaḥ) — a thumb.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for an elbow, then the Sanskrit for a wrist.

You'll want to know: अंगुष्ठः.
अंगुष्ठः — aṅguṣṭhaḥ — "a thumb".

The word, taken apart.
aṅguṣṭha "the thumb" is built on aṅgu-, the same base as aṅguli "finger" (CDIAL 137). It lives on as Hindi and Punjabi aṅgūṭhā "thumb" (CDIAL 137).

Lesson 2 of 5.
वक्षः (vakṣaḥ) — the chest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a wrist, then the Sanskrit for a thumb.

You'll want to know: वक्षः.
वक्षः — vakṣaḥ — "the chest".

The word, taken apart.
vakṣaḥ is vakṣas "breast, chest", already in the Rigveda, with the final s said as ḥ at the end of a phrase (MW). MW lists it under √vakṣ "grow, increase" without deriving it, so its source is uncertain; Kashmiri kept it as wach "chest" (CDIAL 11188).

Lesson 3 of 5.
कटिः (kaṭiḥ) — the waist.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for a thumb, then the Sanskrit for the chest.

You'll want to know: कटिः.
कटिः — kaṭiḥ — "the waist".

The word, taken apart.
kaṭi "the hip, the waist" has a disputed origin: CDIAL 2639 notes an Indo-European proposal but thinks it rather borrowed from Dravidian, comparing Kannada kaḍe "side". It lives on as Marathi kaḍ "side, waist" (CDIAL 2639).

Lesson 5 of 5.
त्वक् (tvak) — the skin.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for the waist, then the Sanskrit for an eyebrow.

You'll want to know: त्वक्.
त्वक् — tvak — "the skin".

The word, taken apart.
tvak is tvac "skin, hide", already in the Rigveda, with the final c said as k at the end of a word (MW). MW sets it beside a root √tvac "cover" known only from the grammarians, so its origin is uncertain; Pali kept it as taco (CDIAL 6087).
