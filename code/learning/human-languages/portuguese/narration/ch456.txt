6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
a clareira — the clearing (in a wood).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the bank statement, then the Portuguese for the fee.

You'll want to know: a clareira.
a clareira — "the clearing (in a wood)".
Fizemos um piquenique numa clareira. We had a picnic in a clearing.
Built on claro: the bright place among the trees.

The word, taken apart.
clareira is built on claro 'bright, clear' with -eira (EtymDB); claro is inherited from Latin clārus.

Lesson 2 of 6.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the fee, then the Portuguese for the clearing.

You'll want to know: o riacho.
o riacho — "the brook".
Um riacho passa pelo meio do bosque. A brook runs through the middle of the wood.
Smaller than um rio.

The word, taken apart.
riacho was borrowed from Spanish riacho (EtymDB), a small form of río 'river', from Latin rīvus 'stream' (Lewis & Short).

You'll want to know: a ribeira.
a ribeira — "the stream; the riverside".
As crianças apanham pedras na ribeira. The children pick up stones in the stream.
In old town names a Ribeira is the riverside quarter.

The word, taken apart.
ribeira is inherited from Latin rīpāria 'of the riverbank', from rīpa 'bank' (Lewis & Short); EtymDB lists Spanish ribera and French rivière as cousins.

Um coelho escondeu-se atrás de um arbusto. A rabbit hid behind a bush.
