Italian, chapter 360: In ufficio.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il dirigente — the manager, the executive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the shift, then the Italian for the overtime.

You'll want to know: il dirigente.
il dirigente — "the manager, the executive".
Il dirigente vuole parlare con tutto il gruppo. The manager wants to talk to the whole team.
Higher than il capo of a small team. Un dirigente scolastico is a head teacher.

The word, taken apart.
dirigente is the present participle of dirigere "to direct", from Latin dīrigere "to set straight, to guide" (Lewis & Short), built on regere "to rule, to steer".

Lesson 2 of 5.
il reparto — the department, the section.

You'll want to know: il reparto.
il reparto — "the department, the section".
Lavoro nel reparto vendite. I work in the sales department.
In a hospital, il reparto is the ward; in a shop, the department.

The word, taken apart.
reparto is built on ripartire "to share out", from ri- and partire "to divide", inherited from Latin partīrī "to divide", from pars, partis "a part" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
la mansione — the duty, the task (in a job).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the manager, then the Italian for the department.

You'll want to know: la mansione.
la mansione — "the duty, the task (in a job)".
Le mie mansioni sono rispondere al telefono e organizzare le riunioni. My duties are answering the phone and organising meetings.
Mostly plural in job descriptions: le mansioni.

The word, taken apart.
mansione is usually traced to Latin mānsiō "a stay, a lodging" (Lewis & Short), from manēre "to remain". How it came to mean a duty, perhaps through French, is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
la proroga — the extension (of a deadline).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the department, then the Italian for the duty.

You'll want to know: la proroga.
la proroga — "the extension (of a deadline)".
Ho chiesto una proroga di due settimane. I asked for a two-week extension.
Stress on the first syllable: pròroga. The verb is prorogare.

The word, taken apart.
proroga is built on prorogare "to extend", a learned borrowing from Latin prōrogāre "to prolong, to put off" (Lewis & Short), built from prō and rogāre "to ask".

Lesson 5 of 5.
la consegna — the delivery, the handing in.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the duty, then the Italian for the extension.

You'll want to know: la consegna.
la consegna — "the delivery, the handing in".
La consegna del progetto è fissata per lunedì. The handing in of the project is set for Monday.
La data di consegna — the delivery date, the deadline. Consegna a domicilio — home delivery.

The word, taken apart.
consegna is built on consegnare "to hand over", inherited from Latin cōnsignāre "to seal, to put on record" (Lewis & Short), built on signum "a mark, a seal".
