The same writing, with a clock — the DELF A1 writing paper's clock, its two parts, and the rule that can fail you on one skill alone.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on. You can listen to all of it now and come back to the parts that need looking at once you have stopped.

Writing — timed production.
What France Éducation international publishes. The DELF A1 writing paper runs 30 minutes and holds two parts: a form to complete, and a personal message of at least forty words.
Those numbers are read out of task-shapes/a1.json, which transcribes the published materials. Note what is not there: the paper publishes a minimum for the message and no maximum. Forty is a floor, not a target.
[pause 2 seconds]
Ten minutes for the form, starting now. Fill every field.
[pause 3 seconds]
Now twenty minutes for the message. Forty words is the floor; write past it rather than up to it.
[pause 2 seconds]
Stop when the time is up, even mid-word.

Wrap-up recall.
[pause 2 seconds]
Count your words. Under forty and the task has not been done — this is the one number on the paper that is a hard floor rather than guidance.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now the rule worth carrying out of this lesson, because it changes how you should study rather than how you should write today.
[pause 2 seconds]
DELF A1 passes on 50 out of 100 overall — and any single skill below 5 out of 25 is eliminatory. A candidate with 45 across three papers and 4 in the fourth does not pass, whatever the total says.
[pause 2 seconds]
So the paper that decides your result is the one you are worst at, not the average of four. If that is writing, no amount of reading practice repairs it.
