Latin, chapter 172: To sow, To reap, To hunt, To go fishing, To deny.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
serō, serere — to sow, to plant.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to create, then the Latin for to believe.

You'll want to know: serō, serere.
serō, serere — "to sow, to plant".

The word, taken apart.
serō, "to sow", is inherited from Proto-Italic sizō, "I sow", usually traced to Proto-Indo-European si-sh1-, "to sow", a doubled form of the root (EtymDB 2.1); sēmen, "seed", has the same root. It is a different word from serō, "to join, to bind".

Lesson 3 of 5.
vēnor, vēnārī — to hunt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to sow, then the Latin for to reap.

You'll want to know: vēnor, vēnārī.
vēnor, vēnārī — "to hunt".

The word, taken apart.
vēnor has no sure origin; EtymDB 2.1 records proposed links with Proto-Indo-European wenh1-, "to strive for, to desire", and weyh1-, "to pursue". English venison, "hunted meat", comes from it through French.

Lesson 4 of 5.
piscor, piscārī — to go fishing.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to reap, then the Latin for to hunt.

You'll want to know: piscor, piscārī.
piscor, piscārī — "to go fishing".

The word, taken apart.
piscor is the verb made from piscis, "a fish" (Lewis & Short), which is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European pisk-, "fish" (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 5 of 5.
negō, negāre — to deny, to say no.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to hunt, then the Latin for to go fishing.

You'll want to know: negō, negāre.
negō, negāre — "to deny, to say no".

The word, taken apart.
negō is built on the negative ne, "not", perhaps through nec, "and not"; how the verb was formed is uncertain. English negative and deny come from its family.
