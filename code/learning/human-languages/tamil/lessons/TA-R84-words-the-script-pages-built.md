# (seven words you learned to read before you learned to spell them)

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-SCRIPT-WRITE-SARI-02, TA-SCRIPT-READ-EN-02] -->

[PAUSE 3s] Read **சரி**, then **என்**. You have said both since the first two
chapters.

## Reading: seven words, each one a lesson in disguise
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-SCRIPT-WRITE-ILLAI-03, TA-SCRIPT-WRITE-SARI-02, TA-SCRIPT-READ-EN-02, TA-SCRIPT-READ-PEYAR-03, TA-SCRIPT-READ-EPPADI-02, TA-SCRIPT-READ-QUESTION-02, TA-SCRIPT-READ-TAMIZH-02] -->

| word | what it means | what it was really teaching |
|---|---|---|
| **இல்லை** | no, there is not | the **ai** sign, which stands to the LEFT |
| **சரி** | fine, all right | **ச**, one letter for a whole family of sounds |
| **என்** | my | a word-initial vowel, reached by a rule you had |
| **பெயர்** | name | a second sign that stands to the left of its letter |
| **எப்படி** | how | the retroflex **ட** |
| **இருக்கிறீர்கள்** | are you (respectful) | the **ு** sign, in a word of eight pieces |
| **தமிழ்** | Tamil | **ழ**, and the name of the language |

Every one was a word you could already say. The writing pages never asked you to
decode something unfamiliar — they put a known word in front of you and let the
shapes attach to a sound you owned.

## Script: the signs that sit on the wrong side
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-SCRIPT-WRITE-ILLAI-03, TA-SCRIPT-READ-PEYAR-03] -->

Two of the seven carry the same surprise. In **இல்லை** and in **பெயர்**, a vowel
sign is written **before** the consonant it is pronounced after.

That is worth holding because it breaks the habit every reader brings: the order
on the page is not the order in the mouth. A Tamil reader takes in the group and
then says it.

## Script: த and ழ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-SCRIPT-TA-ZHA-01, TA-SCRIPT-READ-TAMIZH-02] -->

**தமிழ்** is two letters worth stopping on. **த** is the soft dental *t*, the
one made against the teeth. **ழ** is the curled sound with no English partner,
and the language wears it in its own name.

Reading the name correctly is the single clearest proof that the script has
stopped being decoration.
