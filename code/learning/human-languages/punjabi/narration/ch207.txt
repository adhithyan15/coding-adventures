Lesson 2 of 5.
One bounded habitual form of to be — happens; masculine singular habitual form of to be.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Retrieve familiar ਹੋਣਾ, “to be,” then listen for one masculine singular habitual form.

Grammar Lens: direct owner.
ਹੁੰਦਾ — hundaa — happens; masculine singular habitual form.
Hear and say the whole form before reading it. Keep ਹੁੰਦਾ (hundaa) whole. This chapter uses it only for one repeated event in the small frame that follows. It does not open the wider habitual agreement system.

The form, taken apart — source boundary.
Punjabi University Patiala's lexical statistics identifies ਹੁੰਦਾ (hundaa) as the masculine singular non-perfect form of ਹੋ, with continuous or habitual use. Its reference grammar gives ਹੋ as the stem of ਹੋਣਾ and lists ਹੁੰਦਾ (hundaa) as Present II. Punjabi University lesson 13 describes the agreeing -dā construction with a present auxiliary, and its verb reference identifies ਹੈ as the third-person singular present auxiliary. Sources: Punjabi University lexical statistics, Punjabi University reference grammar, Punjabi University lesson 13, and Punjabi University verb reference.
Those sources support the modern form. Reuse the history already attached to ਹੋਣਾ; do not invent a second etymology for an inflected form. This chapter teaches the habitual reading only, not generic “is,” a progressive, counterfactual “would,” a past form, or a feminine or plural form.

Lesson 4 of 5.
Put the new form in one daily frame — this happens every day.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Retrieve the familiar words for “this,” “every day,” and the final auxiliary ਹੈ, then retrieve the new habitual form.

Grammar Lens.
ਇਹ ਰੋਜ਼ ਹੁੰਦਾ ਹੈ — eh roz hundaa hai — this happens every day.
For this one frame, put familiar ਇਹ ਰੋਜ਼ before the new whole form ਹੁੰਦਾ (hundaa), then keep familiar ਹੈ last. Nothing else in the habitual or agreement system is added.
