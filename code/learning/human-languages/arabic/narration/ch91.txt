Arabic, chapter 91: Road, Bank, Station, Car, Bus.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
طريق (ṭarīq) — a road.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a shop, then the Arabic for a street.

You'll want to know: طريق.
طريق — ṭarīq — "a road".

The word, taken apart.
طريق (ṭarīq) is built on the native Arabic root ṭ-r-q, "to strike, to beat" (Lane). Lane's ṭarīq is a beaten track, a road.

Lesson 2 of 5.
بنك (bank) — a bank.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a street, then the Arabic for a road.

You'll want to know: بنك.
بنك — bank — "a bank".

The word, taken apart.
بنك (bank) is borrowed from the European word, Italian banco or French banque, "bank", once a moneychanger's bench; it has no Arabic root.

سيارة (sayyāra) — a car.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a bank, then the Arabic for a station.

You'll want to know: سيارة.
سيارة — sayyāra — "a car".

The word, taken apart.
سيارة (sayyāra) is built on the native Arabic root s-y-r, "to go, to travel" (Lane). Lane gives sayyāra as a company of travellers, a caravan; the car is a modern sense.

Lesson 5 of 5.
حافلة (ḥāfila) — a bus.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a station, then the Arabic for a car.

You'll want to know: حافلة.
حافلة — ḥāfila — "a bus".

The word, taken apart.
حافلة (ḥāfila) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-f-l, "to gather, to fill" (Lane). Lane's ḥāfil is a full udder or a valley in flood; as the word for a bus ḥāfila is a modern coinage.
