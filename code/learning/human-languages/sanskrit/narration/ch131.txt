Lesson 1 of 5.
कृष्णः (kṛṣṇaḥ) — black, dark.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for weak, then the Sanskrit for clear.

You'll want to know: कृष्णः.
कृष्णः — kṛṣṇaḥ — "black, dark".

The word, taken apart.
kṛṣṇa "black, dark" is old, in the Rigveda, and is inherited from Indo-European; Old Prussian kirsnan "black" is a cousin (EtymDB). As a name it is Krishna, "the dark one", whom Hindi calls kānh (CDIAL 3451).

Lesson 2 of 5.
धूसरः (dhūsaraḥ) — grey.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for clear, then the Sanskrit for black.

You'll want to know: धूसरः.
धूसरः — dhūsaraḥ — "grey".

The word, taken apart.
dhūsara "dust-coloured, grey" is linked by CDIAL 6870 with words for dust, while MW suggests the root √dhvaṃs "fall to dust"; its exact formation is uncertain. Hindi and Marathi kept it as dhūsar "grey" (CDIAL 6870).

पाटलः — pāṭalaḥ — "pink".

The word, taken apart.
pāṭala "pale red, pink" also names the trumpet-flower tree (MW). Neither MW nor CDIAL 8033 gives a derivation, so its origin is uncertain; DEDR 4075 compares Dravidian names for that tree.

You'll want to know: स्पष्टः.
स्पष्टः — spaṣṭaḥ — "clear".

The word, taken apart.
spaṣṭa "clearly perceived, distinct" belongs to the root √spaś "see, spy" (MW), inherited from Indo-European (EtymDB). MW sets √spaś beside Greek skeptomai, Latin specio and English spy.

Lesson 5 of 5.
स्थूलः (sthūlaḥ) — thick, fat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for pink, then the Sanskrit for clear.

You'll want to know: स्थूलः.
स्थूलः — sthūlaḥ — "thick, fat".

The word, taken apart.
sthūla "large, thick, stout" comes, according to MW, from √sthū, a form of √sthā "stand": "solid, firm-standing". It is old, in the Atharvaveda, and lives on as Punjabi ṭhullhā "fat, coarse" (CDIAL 13776).
