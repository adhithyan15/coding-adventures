Urdu, chapter 318: Eid Morning.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
نماز (namāz) — the set prayer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for to glitter, then the Urdu for festive bustle.

You'll want to know: نماز.
نماز — namāz — "the set prayer".
صبح سب نے نماز پڑھی۔ subah sab ne namāz paṛhī. In the morning everyone said the prayer.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
نماز (namāz) is usually traced to Persian namāz, "a bowing, a prayer". It is said, in Urdu, by reading it: نماز پڑھی.

بچوں کو دادا سے عیدی ملی۔ bachchoṅ ko dādā se īdī milī. The children got Eid money from their grandfather.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
عیدی (īdī) is built on عید, usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic ʿīd, with the ending -ī: the money elders give children on the day.

Lesson 3 of 5.
سویاں (sivayāṅ) — sweet vermicelli.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for the set prayer, then the Urdu for money given at Eid.

You'll want to know: سویاں.
سویاں — sivayāṅ — "sweet vermicelli".
امی نے سویاں پکائیں۔ ammī ne sivayāṅ pakāīṅ. Mum cooked sweet vermicelli.
Feminine, used in the plural.

The word, taken apart.
سویاں (sivayāṅ) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousins sewaī̃ and sẽwiyā̃, "vermicelli", under Sanskrit śamitā (CDIAL 12307). Urdu says it in the plural: سویاں پکائیں.

Lesson 4 of 5.
مبارک باد (mubārak bād) — congratulations.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for money given at Eid, then the Urdu for sweet vermicelli.

You'll want to know: مبارک باد.
مبارک باد — mubārak bād — "congratulations".
پڑوسیوں نے ہمیں عید کی مبارک باد دی۔ paṛosiyoṅ ne hameṅ īd kī mubārak bād dī. The neighbours wished us a happy Eid.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
مبارک باد (mubārak bād) is usually traced to Arabic mubārak, "blessed", and Persian bād, "may it be", a form of the Persian verb "to be": "may it be blessed". It is feminine, and the one wished takes کو: ہمیں مبارک باد دی.

Lesson 5 of 5.
لپٹنا (lipaṭnā) — to cling; to hug.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for sweet vermicelli, then the Urdu for congratulations.

You'll want to know: لپٹنا.
لپٹنا — lipaṭnā — "to cling; to hug".
بچی ماں سے لپٹ گئی۔ bachchī māṅ se lipaṭ gaī. The little girl clung to her mother.
Past (she …): وہ لپٹ گئی, voh lipaṭ gaī.

The word, taken apart.
لپٹنا (lipaṭnā) is inherited: CDIAL lists the Hindi cousin lipaṭnā, "to stick", under Sanskrit lipyatē (CDIAL 11061). The person you cling to takes سے: ماں سے لپٹ گئی.
