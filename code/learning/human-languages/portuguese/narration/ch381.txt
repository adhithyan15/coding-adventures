Portuguese, chapter 381: As regras.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a norma — the rule, the standard, the norm.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to elect, then the Portuguese for the voter.

You'll want to know: a norma.
a norma — "the rule, the standard, the norm".
A escola tem normas muito claras. The school has very clear rules.
Por norma — as a rule, usually.

The word, taken apart.
norma is a learned borrowing from Latin nōrma "a carpenter's square, a rule, a pattern" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
o regulamento — the regulations, the rules (of a place).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the voter, then the Portuguese for the rule.

You'll want to know: o regulamento.
o regulamento — "the regulations, the rules (of a place)".
O regulamento do prédio proíbe animais. The building's regulations forbid animals.
Cumprir o regulamento — to follow the regulations.

The word, taken apart.
regulamento is built on regular "to regulate", from Late Latin rēgulāre, on rēgula "a ruler, a rule", from regere "to guide, to rule" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
obrigatório — compulsory, mandatory.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the rule, then the Portuguese for the regulations.

You'll want to know: obrigatório.
obrigatório — "compulsory, mandatory".
O uso de capacete é obrigatório. Wearing a helmet is compulsory.
É obrigatório + infinitive: é obrigatório usar cinto.

The word, taken apart.
obrigatório is a learned borrowing from Latin obligātōrius "obligatory, binding" (EtymDB), from obligāre "to bind in obligation", on ligāre "to tie" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
facultativo — optional.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for the regulations, then the Portuguese for compulsory.

You'll want to know: facultativo.
facultativo — "optional".
A visita ao museu é facultativa. The museum visit is optional.
The opposite of obrigatório.

The word, taken apart.
facultativo is built on faculdade in its sense "power, permission", from Latin facultās "capability, means" (Lewis & Short), with the learned suffix -ivo.

Lesson 5 of 5.
a obrigação — the obligation, the duty.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for compulsory, then the Portuguese for optional.

You'll want to know: a obrigação.
a obrigação — "the obligation, the duty".
Votar é um direito e uma obrigação. Voting is a right and a duty.
Ter a obrigação de + infinitive — to be obliged to.

The word, taken apart.
obrigação comes from Latin obligātiō "a binding, an obligation" (Lewis & Short), through Old Portuguese obrigaçon (EtymDB); the -r- for -l- shows popular change.
