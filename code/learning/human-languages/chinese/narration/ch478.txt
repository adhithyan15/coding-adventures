Mandarin Chinese, chapter 478: Injuries.
6 lessons. You can do the first 2 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 6.
tàngshāng — to scald; to burn (with heat).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for one's digestion, then the Mandarin for an appetite.

You'll want to know: tàngshāng.
tàngshāng — "to scald; to burn (with heat)".
wǒ zuò fàn de shíhou tàngshāng le shǒu — I burned my hand while I was cooking.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
tàngshāng is built from tàng 'scalding hot' and shāng 'to hurt'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese syang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to harm'; no dictionary we use records an older form of the first under this spelling, so its history is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 6.
伤口 (shāngkǒu) — wound; cut.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for an appetite, then the Mandarin for to scald oneself.

You'll want to know: 伤口.
伤口 — shāngkǒu — "wound; cut".
我感觉伤口很疼体温也高了 wǒ gǎnjué shāngkǒu hěn téng tǐwēn yě gāo le — My wound really hurts, and my temperature has gone up too.
The new character, in pieces: 伤 (shāng) — six strokes in three pieces: on the left 亻, two strokes; at the top right a short stroke falling to the left and a bar; and under them 力 lì, two strokes. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
伤口 (shāngkǒu) is built from 伤 (shāng) 'to hurt' and 口 'an opening', both inherited from Middle Chinese, syang and khuwX (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to harm' and the second 'the mouth'.

Lesson 3 of 6.
伤 (shāng) — 亻, then a short top over 力.

Script — 亻, then a short top over 力.
伤 (shāng).
Three pieces: the left, then the top right, then the bottom right. Count the pieces, not the strokes.
The left piece, 亻, two strokes. A stroke falling to the left, then a vertical down.
The top right, two strokes. A short stroke falling to the left, then a bar from left to right beside it.
The bottom right, 力, two strokes. A stroke that runs right, turns down and hooks to the left, then a long stroke falling to the left through it.
2 + 2 + 2 = six.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[once you have stopped driving — write: 伤 (shāng), all six, with the model in front of you]
[once you have stopped driving — cover: write it again from memory, and count the pieces, not the strokes]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many pieces does 伤 (shāng) have? (Three, two strokes each.) Which written character ends it? (力.)

Lesson 4 of 6.
重伤 (zhòngshāng) — serious injury; badly hurt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to scald oneself, then the Mandarin for an open wound.

You'll want to know: 重伤.
重伤 — zhòngshāng — "serious injury; badly hurt".
那个事故里有两个人重伤 nàge shìgù lǐ yǒu liǎng ge rén zhòngshāng — Two people were badly hurt in that accident.

The word, taken apart.
重伤 (zhòngshāng) is built from 重 'heavy' and 伤 (shāng) 'to hurt', both inherited from Middle Chinese, drjowngX and syang (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'thick' and the second 'to harm'.

Lesson 6 of 6.
bāozā — to bandage; to dress a wound.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for badly hurt, then the Mandarin for to sprain.

You'll want to know: bāozā.
bāozā — "to bandage; to dress a wound".
hùshi gěi wǒ bāozā le shāngkǒu — The nurse dressed my wound.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
bāozā is built from 包 'to wrap' and zā 'to bind', both inherited from Middle Chinese, paew and tsreat (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to wrap' and the second, in its fuller spelling, as binding the grip of a bow. The regular sound change would give zhá; why it is said zā is not settled.
