## You'll want to know: prōdigium, prōdigiī
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C348-ORACULUM-03]; assesses=[] -->

**prōdigium, prōdigiī** — "a portent, a wonder".

> Lapidibus pluit; hoc prōdigium omnēs terruit.
>
> *It rained stones; this portent frightened everyone.*

Neuter. Roman historians list the *prōdigia* of each year: a rain of stones, a cow that spoke. English *prodigy*.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**prōdigium** is explained by Lewis & Short as standing for *\*prōdicium*, from *prōdīcō*, "to foretell": a sign that speaks of what will come. English *prodigy* comes from it.
