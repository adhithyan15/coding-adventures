Italian, chapter 498: Chi bada ai bambini.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la tata — nanny.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to scold, then the Italian for punishment.

You'll want to know: la tata.
la tata — "nanny".
La tata porta i bambini a scuola. The nanny takes the children to school.
Friendly speech; also la babysitter.

The word, taken apart.
tata is a word from child speech, made by doubling a syllable as small children do; the origin is not settled beyond that.

What you've built.

You'll want to know: il pediatra.
il pediatra — "paediatrician".
Domani porto mio figlio dal pediatra. Tomorrow I am taking my son to the paediatrician.
The same form for a woman: la pediatra.

The word, taken apart.
pediatra is a modern learned word built from Greek paîs, paidós "child" and iatrós "doctor" (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: accudire.
accudire — "to care for, to look after".
Mia zia accudisce la nonna malata. My aunt looks after my sick grandmother.
An -isc- verb: accudisco, accudisci.

The word, taken apart.
accudire was borrowed, according to EtymDB, from Spanish acudir "to come to help"; the deeper origin is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
badare — to look after; to mind.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for paediatrician, then the Italian for to care for.

You'll want to know: badare.
badare — "to look after; to mind".
Chi bada ai bambini stasera? Who is looking after the children tonight?
Badare a + person or thing; non badare a spese — not to worry about the cost.

The word, taken apart.
badare is inherited from Medieval Latin batāre "to gape, to stare" (EtymDB): first to stand and watch, then to look after.

Lesson 5 of 5.
ubbidire — to obey.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to care for, then the Italian for to look after.

You'll want to know: ubbidire.
ubbidire — "to obey".
Il cane ubbidisce solo a mio padre. The dog only obeys my father.
An -isc- verb with a: ubbidire ai genitori. Also obbedire.

The word, taken apart.
ubbidire is inherited from Latin oboedīre "to listen to, to obey" (EtymDB), built on audīre "to hear" (Lewis & Short).
