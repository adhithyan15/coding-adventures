## You'll want to know: jǐngchá
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C216-WRONG216-04]; assesses=[] -->

*jǐngchá* — "police officer; the police".

> *jǐngchá lái le* — The police are here.

Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*jǐngchá* is built from *jǐng* 'to warn' and *chá* 'to watch over', both inherited from Middle Chinese, *kjaengX* and *tsrheat* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to be on guard' and the second 'to supervise'.
