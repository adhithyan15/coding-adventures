# मुझे डर लगता है (mujhe ḍar lagtā hai) — saying how you feel

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C280-LAGNA-01, HI-LEX-C284-KHUSH-01, HI-LEX-C108-FEEL-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I'm hungry" and "I am happy". (*mujhe bhūkh lagī hai*, *maiṁ khuś hūṁ*.) Two ways of feeling, and this lesson sets them side by side.

## You'll want to know: a feeling that comes to you
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-GRAMMAR-C284-FEELING-DATIVE-01]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C108-FEEL-01, HI-LEX-C108-FEEL-02, HI-LEX-C185-THG185-02, HI-CONCEPT-C23-KUTTA-BILLI-01, HI-LEX-C41-ADJ-03, HI-LEX-C280-LAGNA-01, HI-GRAMMAR-C278-ONGOING-PAST-01] -->

> **मुझे कुत्तों से डर लगता है।**
>
> *mujhe kuttoṁ se ḍar lagtā hai.* — "I'm afraid of dogs."

Some feelings come to you, and Hindi says so. The one who feels takes **मुझे**, the feeling is the subject of **लगना**, and what you fear takes **से**.

| the feeling | the sentence |
|---|---|
| **डर**, masculine | **मुझे डर लगता है** (*mujhe ḍar lagtā hai*) — I'm afraid |
| **ठंड**, feminine | **मुझे ठंड लग रही है** (*mujhe ṭhaṇḍ lag rahī hai*) — I'm cold |
| how it seemed | **मुझे अच्छा लगा** (*mujhe acchā lagā*) — I liked it; it felt good |

Anger comes with **आना**, "to come": **मुझे गुस्सा आया** (*mujhe gussā āyā*), "I got angry".

## You'll want to know: a feeling you are in
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-GRAMMAR-C284-FEELING-BE-01]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C284-KHUSH-01, HI-LEX-C108-FEEL-03, HI-LEX-C108-FEEL-04, HI-LEX-C109-MOOD-04, HI-JOIN-KYON-01, HI-GRAMMAR-C276-PAST-STEMS-01] -->

> **मीरा आज उदास है।**
>
> *Mīrā āj udās hai.* — "Mira is sad today."

Other feelings are a state you are in, said with **होना**: **मैं ख़ुश हूँ**; **वह परेशान है** (*vah pareśān hai*), "he is worried". **ख़ुश**, **उदास** and **परेशान** never change; **अकेला** agrees: **वह अकेली है** (*vah akelī hai*), "she is alone".

With **हो गया**, the feeling sets in: **वह उदास हो गई** (*vah udās ho gaī*), "she became sad". To ask: **क्या हुआ? तुम परेशान क्यों हो?** (*kyā huā? tum pareśān kyoṁ ho?*), "What happened? Why are you worried?"
