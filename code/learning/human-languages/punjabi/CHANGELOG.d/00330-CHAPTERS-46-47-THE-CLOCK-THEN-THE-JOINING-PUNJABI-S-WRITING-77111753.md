## Chapters 46–47 — the clock, then the joining: Punjabi's writing ramp closes

Two lessons, no new vocabulary in either, and Punjabi is complete at **every
level** from pre-A1 to C2. The corpus-wide writing-stage debt falls **351 →
340**.

**Chapter 46 — with a clock.** The seventh and last stage. Every number is read
out of `task-shapes/a1.json`, the file the assessment contract points at: 20
minutes, two tasks, a six-to-eight-field form worth 40 points, and a 30–40 word
message worth 60. The learner reads that split *before* writing — the message is
worth more and is shorter, so a perfected field is time taken from the part that
scores. Seven minutes, then thirteen. The phone field is called out because
grouping is the first thing a hurried hand drops, and grouping is what makes a
number readable back.

**Chapter 47 — four lines that hold together.** Connected composition, on the
two joiners chapters 38 and 39 already taught: **ate** adds, **par** sets
against. The lesson adds no vocabulary at all — the exercise is the joining, not
the reaching — and the check is the one that makes the stage real: cover line one
and read line two alone. If it still makes sense, you wrote a list.

**A cost recorded rather than hidden.** A lesson at the very end of a track
cannot be retrieved. Both lessons practise atoms that then have no later return,
so the reinforcement windows move +1/+2/+1/+1 across R1–R4. That is inherent to
any terminal lesson rather than a defect in these, and the next lesson added
after them pays part of it back.
