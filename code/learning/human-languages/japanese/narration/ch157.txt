Lesson 1 of 6.
しょくたく (shokutaku) — a dining table.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to put in, then the Japanese for to arrange in a row.

You'll want to know: しょくたく.
しょくたく — shokutaku — "a dining table".
かぞくと しょくたくで ごはんを たべます。 — kazoku to shokutaku de gohan o tabemasu — "I eat at the dining table with my family."

The word, taken apart.
しょくたく (shokutaku) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are shoku, "eat, food" (Middle Chinese zyik) and taku, "table, desk" (traewk) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for a dining table, then the Japanese for a vase.

You'll want to know: かざる.
かざる — kazaru — "to decorate; to put on display".
げんかんに はなを かざります。 — genkan ni hana o kazarimasu — "I display flowers in the entry hall."
Polite かざります, kazarimasu; a godan verb.

The word, taken apart.
かざる (kazaru) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Japanese for to decorate, then the Japanese for to hang.

You'll want to know: おく.
おく — oku — "to put; to place".
かびんを しょくたくに おきます。 — kabin o shokutaku ni okimasu — "I put the vase on the dining table."
Polite おきます, okimasu; a godan verb. Spelled and said like おきます, okimasu, "I get up"; here it takes an object with を.

The word, taken apart.
おく (oku) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi, and its older origin is not settled (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).
