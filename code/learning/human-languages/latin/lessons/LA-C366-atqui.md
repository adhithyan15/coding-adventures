## You'll want to know: atquī
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C366-PROINDE-03]; assesses=[] -->

**atquī** — "and yet, but (raising an objection)".

> Dīcis tē pauperem esse; atquī vīllam magnam habēs.
>
> *You say you are poor; and yet you have a large country house.*

*Atquī* brings in a fact that tells against what was said. It takes the indicative.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*atquī* is built from *at* "but" and *quī*, an old ablative form meaning "in any way" (Lewis & Short): but in any case.

## What you've built

One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.
