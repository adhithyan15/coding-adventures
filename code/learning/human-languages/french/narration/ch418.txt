French, chapter 418: À bord.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
embarquer — to board; to take along.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to search, then the French for to change planes.

You'll want to know: embarquer.
embarquer — "to board; to take along".
Les passagers embarquent par l'avant de l'avion. The passengers board at the front of the plane.
Informal: embarquer quelqu'un — to take someone along.

The word, taken apart.
embarquer is built from en- and barque 'small boat', which came through Italian or Provençal from Latin barca (Lewis & Short). EtymDB lists the verb without a source.

Lesson 2 of 5.
l'hôtesse — the flight attendant (a woman); the hostess.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for to change planes, then the French for to board.

You'll want to know: l'hôtesse.
l'hôtesse — "the flight attendant (a woman); the hostess".
L'hôtesse sert des boissons pendant le vol. The flight attendant serves drinks during the flight.
Une hôtesse de l'air; today also un ou une agent de bord.

The word, taken apart.
hôtesse is the feminine of hôte 'host', which goes back to Latin hospes 'host, guest' (Lewis & Short). EtymDB does not list the feminine.

Lesson 5 of 5.
l'allée — the aisle; the path, the drive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the French for the steward, then the French for the porthole.

You'll want to know: l'allée.
l'allée — "the aisle; the path, the drive".
Il préfère une place côté allée. He prefers an aisle seat.
A feminine noun. In a park, une allée is a path lined with trees.

The word, taken apart.
allée is built on aller 'to go' (EtymDB): a place where you walk.
