Tamil, chapter 101: Grandparents, Aunts and Uncles.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
தாத்தா (tāttā) — a grandfather.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a wife, then the Tamil for a relative.

You'll want to know: தாத்தா.
தாத்தா (tāttā) — "grandfather".
என் தாத்தா (tāttā) — "my grandfather".

What you've built.
A grandfather.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: tāttā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: tāttā, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: say eṉ tāttā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Tamil for a wife, then the Tamil for a relative, then say tāttā again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does தாத்தா (tāttā) mean? ("A grandfather".) Say it once more.

Lesson 3 of 5.
மாமா (māmā) — uncle, the mother's brother.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a grandfather, then the Tamil for a grandmother.

You'll want to know: மாமா.
மாமா (māmā) — "uncle", above all your mother's brother; also "father-in-law".
Tamil kinship names the side of the family, not only the generation.

What you've built.
An uncle, named by side.

Lesson 4 of 5.
அத்தை (attai) — aunt, the father's sister.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for a grandmother, then the Tamil for an uncle.

You'll want to know: அத்தை.
அத்தை (attai) — "aunt", your father's sister; also "mother-in-law".
மாமா (māmā) and அத்தை (attai) pair up across the two sides of the family.

What you've built.
An aunt, named by side.

Lesson 5 of 5.
சித்தப்பா (cittappā) — the father's younger brother.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Tamil for an uncle, then the Tamil for an aunt.

You'll want to know: சித்தப்பா.
சித்தப்பா (cittappā) — your father's younger brother.
It ends in அப்பா, "father": he is the little father.

What you've built.
Grandparents, uncle, aunt, and a little father.
