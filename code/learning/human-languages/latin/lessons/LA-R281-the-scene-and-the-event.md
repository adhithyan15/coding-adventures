# Review — the scene, and what happened in it

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-GRAMMAR-C281-PERFECTUM-01, LA-GRAMMAR-C281-ERAM-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "they saw". (*Vīdērunt.*) Then "we were", the open past.
(*Erāmus.*)

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-GRAMMAR-C281-PERFECTUM-AVI-02, LA-GRAMMAR-C281-IMPERFECTUM-01, LA-GRAMMAR-C281-SCAENA-01] -->

Four lessons, and both of Latin's story pasts are in your hands. The
**perfect**, a stem and six endings: *vīdī*, *vīdit*, *vīdērunt*, with
*-āvī* for most *-āre* verbs. The **imperfect**: *eram*, *erat*, *erant* for
*sum*, and *-ba-* before the ending for every other verb: *ambulābam*,
*habēbam*, *legēbam*, *veniēbam*.

[PAUSE 2s] And the division of labour between them: the imperfect holds
the scene still, the perfect drops the events into it.

> *Pluēbat, et in caupōnā sedēbam. Subitō amīcus intrāvit.*
> — It was raining, and I was sitting in the inn. Suddenly a friend came in.

[PAUSE 1s] Two verbs of scene, *pluēbat* and *sedēbam*; one event,
*intrāvit*, and *subitō* in front of it.

## Wrap-up Recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[LA-GRAMMAR-C281-PERFECTUM-01, LA-GRAMMAR-C281-SCAENA-01, LA-GRAMMAR-C281-ERAM-01, LA-ETYMON-C281-ERAM-02, LA-ETYMON-C281-BAM-02, LA-ETYMON-C281-PERFECTUM-02] -->

[PAUSE 3s] "He came in"? (***Intrāvit.***) "I was sitting"? (***Sedēbam.***)
Which ending says "they" in the perfect? (***-ērunt.***) In *pluēbat …
intrāvit*, which verb is the scene? (***Pluēbat.***) What does *perfectum*
mean? (**Carried through.**) What was the *r* of *eram* once? (An ***s***.)
And which language still says *cantaba*? (**Spanish**, keeping the *-b-*.)
