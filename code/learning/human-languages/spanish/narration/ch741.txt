Spanish, chapter 741: Cansancio y descanso.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el cansancio — tiredness.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to faint, then the Spanish for the allergy.

You'll want to know: el cansancio.
el cansancio — "tiredness".
Siento mucho cansancio. I feel very tired.

The word, taken apart.
cansancio is built on cansar "to tire", which comes from Latin campsāre "to bend, to sail round a headland" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). How the sense moved to tiring is not settled.

Lesson 2 of 5.
agotado — exhausted; sold out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the allergy, then the Spanish for tiredness.

You'll want to know: agotado.
agotado — "exhausted; sold out".
Después del viaje estoy agotado. After the trip I am exhausted.
On a ticket office: agotado — sold out.

The word, taken apart.
agotado is the participle of agotar, usually explained as built on gota "drop", from Latin gutta: emptied down to the last drop.

Lesson 3 of 5.
dormirse — to fall asleep.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for tiredness, then the Spanish for exhausted.

You'll want to know: dormirse.
dormirse — "to fall asleep".
Me dormí viendo la tele. I fell asleep watching TV.
Dormirse also means to oversleep: me dormí y llegué tarde.

The word, taken apart.
dormirse is the reflexive of dormir, which is usually traced to Latin dormīre "to sleep".

Lesson 4 of 5.
el insomnio — insomnia.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for exhausted, then the Spanish for to fall asleep.

You'll want to know: el insomnio.
el insomnio — "insomnia".
Sufro de insomnio. I suffer from insomnia.

The word, taken apart.
insomnio is usually traced to Latin insomnia "want of sleep" (Lewis & Short), built on in- "not" and somnus "sleep"; its ending follows Latin insomnium, which in Latin meant a dream.

Lesson 5 of 5.
la siesta — the nap, the siesta.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to fall asleep, then the Spanish for insomnia.

You'll want to know: la siesta.
la siesta — "the nap, the siesta".
Después de comer echo la siesta. After lunch I have a nap.
Echar la siesta (Spain) or dormir la siesta.

The word, taken apart.
siesta is inherited from Latin sexta, short for sexta hōra "the sixth hour" (EtymDB): counted from dawn, the sixth hour is midday.
