Russian, chapter 72: Here, There, Far, Near, Up Above.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
здесь (zdes) — here.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for summer, then the Russian for autumn.

You'll want to know: здесь.
здесь (zdes) — "here".

The word, taken apart.
здесь (zdes) from Old Russian сьде, with a later -сь: Proto-Slavic sĭde, "here", from the old pronoun sĭ, "this", the one in сей and сегодня, "today", built as где, "where", is built from the question word (Vasmer).

Lesson 3 of 5.
далеко (dalekó) — far.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for here, then the Russian for there.

You'll want to know: далеко.
далеко (dalekó) — "far".

The word, taken apart.
далеко (dalekó) is далёкий, "distant", made into an adverb with -о; it is built on даль, "the distance", which Vasmer reports is usually linked with долгий, "long", and Greek dolichós, "long".

Lesson 4 of 5.
близко (blízko) — near, close by.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for there, then the Russian for far.

You'll want to know: близко.
близко (blízko) — "near, close by".

The word, taken apart.
близко (blízko) is близкий, "close", made into an adverb, from близ, "near", Proto-Slavic blizŭ. Vasmer relates it to Latvian blaîzît, "to press", and Latin flīgere, "to strike", but also cites a Lithuanian alternative, so the deeper origin is open.

Lesson 5 of 5.
наверху (naverkhú) — up above, upstairs.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for far, then the Russian for near.

You'll want to know: наверху.
наверху (naverkhú) — "up above, upstairs".

The word, taken apart.
наверху (naverkhú) is на, "on", with верху, a form of верх, "the top": "on top". верх from Proto-Slavic vĭrxŭ, a cousin of Lithuanian viršùs, "top" (Vasmer).
