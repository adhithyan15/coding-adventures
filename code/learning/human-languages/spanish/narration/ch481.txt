Spanish, chapter 481: Problemas y soluciones.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
resolver — to solve, to sort out.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to promise, then the Spanish for to complain.

You'll want to know: resolver.
resolver — "to solve, to sort out".
Vamos a resolver el problema hoy. We are going to sort out the problem today.
The o breaks into ue under stress: resuelvo, resuelve.

The word, taken apart.
resolver from Latin resolvere, "to untie, to loosen" (EtymDB 2.1): re- and solvere, "to loosen". To solve a problem is to undo its knot. It bends like volver, "to return": resuelvo.

What you've built.

Lesson 2 of 5.
recuperar — to get back, to recover.

Warm-up.

You'll want to know: recuperar.
recuperar — "to get back, to recover".
Quiero recuperar mi dinero. I want to get my money back.
Recuperarse is to get better after an illness.

The word, taken apart.
recuperar from Latin recuperare, "to get back" (EtymDB 2.1) — a learned borrowing. Worn down in speech, the same verb gave recobrar, "to recover", and its shortening cobrar, which you met as "to charge".

Lesson 4 of 5.
prever — to foresee, to expect.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to get back, then the Spanish for to rule out.

You'll want to know: prever.
prever — "to foresee, to expect".
Se prevé lluvia para el fin de semana. Rain is expected for the weekend.
Built on ver, and conjugated like it: preveo, prevé.

The word, taken apart.
prever is pre-, "before", and ver, "to see", matching Latin praevidere, "to foresee" (EtymDB 2.1); it bends like ver.

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: vigilar.
vigilar — "to watch, to keep an eye on".
Vigila a los niños en la piscina. Keep an eye on the children at the pool.
Zona vigilada on a sign means the area is watched.

The word, taken apart.
vigilar from Latin vigilare, "to stay awake, to keep watch" (EtymDB 2.1) — a learned borrowing. Worn down in speech, the same verb became velar, "to stay up, to keep vigil".
