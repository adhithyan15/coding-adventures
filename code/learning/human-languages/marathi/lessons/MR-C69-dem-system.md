# हा · तो — two rows, and what the far one is really for

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-LEX-HA-HI-HE, MR-LEX-TO-TI-TE] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say the six words, near row first, in the order masculine, feminine,
neuter.

## Grammar Lens: two rows, not three
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-GRAMMAR-DEM-TWO-WAY]; assesses=[MR-LEX-HA-HI-HE, MR-LEX-TO-TI-TE] -->

**Marathi points in two directions and stops.** **ह-** is here; **त-** is not
here. There is no third word for *that one over there, further away*.

Spanish needs three — *este*, *ese*, *aquel* — and English is unstable about it,
keeping *yonder* alive only in corners. Marathi draws the line **once**, between
what is at hand and what is not, and everything not at hand shares one row.

That is fewer words to learn and one fewer decision to make while speaking.

## Grammar Lens: the far row is also he, she and it
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-GRAMMAR-DEM-DOUBLES-AS-PRONOUN]; assesses=[MR-LEX-TO-TI-TE, MR-LEX-MI, MR-LEX-TU-TUMHI] -->

Here is the part that matters most, and it is not about pointing at all.

> **तो** — *that* — is also **he**.
> **ती** — *that* — is also **she**.
> **ते** — *that* — is also **it**.

Marathi does not keep a separate set of third-person pronouns. **It reuses the
far row.** Whoever is not you and not the person you are speaking to is, by
default, *the one over there*.

So this chapter did more than teach pointing:

| | |
|---|---|
| **मी** | I |
| **तू** / **तुम्ही** | you |
| **तो** / **ती** / **ते** | he / she / it |

**That is the whole set of people a sentence can be about**, and until now you
had the first two rows and not the third.
