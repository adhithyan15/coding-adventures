Italian, chapter 445: In piazza.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
il campanile — the bell tower.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the house number, then the Italian for the intercom.

You'll want to know: il campanile.
il campanile — "the bell tower".
Dal campanile si vede tutta la città. From the bell tower the whole city is in view.
Il campanilismo — loyalty to your own town and its bell tower.

The word, taken apart.
campanile is built on campana 'bell', which is inherited from Late Latin campāna (EtymDB).

You'll want to know: il duomo.
il duomo — "the main church (of a town), the cathedral".
Il duomo è in fondo alla via. The main church is at the end of the street.
Many towns call their main church il Duomo.

The word, taken apart.
duomo is inherited from Latin domus 'house', short for domus ecclēsiae 'the house of the church' (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
la cattedrale — the cathedral.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the bell tower, then the Italian for the main church.

You'll want to know: la cattedrale.
la cattedrale — "the cathedral".
La cattedrale ha tre porte di bronzo. The cathedral has three bronze doors.
The bishop's church; la cattedra is the bishop's chair, and also a teacher's desk.

The word, taken apart.
cattedrale is a learned borrowing from Late Latin cathedrālis, from cathedra 'chair', from Greek kathédra 'seat' (EtymDB): the church with the bishop's chair.

Lesson 4 of 5.
il portico — the arcade, the portico.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the main church, then the Italian for the cathedral.

You'll want to know: il portico.
il portico — "the arcade, the portico".
Quando piove camminiamo sotto i portici. When it rains we walk under the arcades.
Often plural: i portici.

The word, taken apart.
portico is a learned borrowing from Latin porticus 'colonnade, porch' (EtymDB), from porta 'gate'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
la loggia — the loggia, the open gallery.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the cathedral, then the Italian for the arcade.

You'll want to know: la loggia.
la loggia — "the loggia, the open gallery".
Dalla loggia del palazzo si vede la piazza. The square is in view from the loggia of the palace.
A covered space open on one side.

The word, taken apart.
loggia was borrowed from Old French loge 'arbour, covered walk' (EtymDB), from Frankish laubja 'arbour'.
