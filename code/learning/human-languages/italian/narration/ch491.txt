Italian, chapter 491: Innamorati.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la cotta — crush (on someone).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to yawn, then the Italian for to stretch.

You'll want to know: la cotta.
la cotta — "crush (on someone)".
A quindici anni avevo una cotta per un compagno di classe. At fifteen I had a crush on a classmate.
Prendersi una cotta per qualcuno — to get a crush on someone. Informal.

The word, taken apart.
cotta is the feminine past participle of cuocere "to cook", from Latin coquere (Lewis & Short): someone cotto is cooked through with love.

Lesson 2 of 5.

You'll want to know: corteggiare.
corteggiare — "to court, to woo".
L'ha corteggiata per mesi prima del primo appuntamento. He courted her for months before the first date.
Built on corte "court": at first it meant to serve at a court.

The word, taken apart.
corteggiare is built on corte "court", inherited from Latin cohors, cohortis "an enclosed yard" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
l'innamorato — sweetheart; in love.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for crush, then the Italian for to court.

You'll want to know: l'innamorato.
l'innamorato — "sweetheart; in love".
Gli innamorati passeggiano sul lungomare. The sweethearts stroll along the seafront.
As an adjective: è innamorato di lei — he is in love with her.

The word, taken apart.
innamorato is the past participle of innamorare "to make fall in love" (EtymDB), built on amore "love", inherited from Latin amor.
