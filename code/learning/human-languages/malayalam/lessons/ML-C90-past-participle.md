# വന്ന (vanna) — that came

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ML-GRAMMAR-C90-PARTICIPLE-01, ML-CONCEPT-C32-VARUKA-02] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say *the child who is going*. Then say the past of *to come* — the
one form on this track that reshapes its stem.

## Grammar Lens: the rule was never about the present
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ML-GRAMMAR-C90-PAST-PARTICIPLE-01]; assesses=[] -->

The describing form was made by taking a vowel sign off the end. **Nothing in
that said which tense it had to be.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **വന്നു** *vannu* | he came — a whole sentence |
| **വന്ന** *vanna* | **that came** — describes something |

The **ു** is gone, exactly as before.

**വന്ന അധ്യാപകൻ** — *vanna adhyāpakan* — **the teacher who came.**

So there are two describing forms, and the difference between them is **when**,
not how:

| | |
|---|---|
| **പോകുന്ന കുട്ടി** | the child who **is going** |
| **വന്ന അധ്യാപകൻ** | the teacher who **came** |

Both sit in front. Both dropped a vowel sign. **Neither has a joining word.**

What gets taken off is whatever vowel sign the **last letter** carries. A past
form ending in **ി** loses that sign the same way — what is left in every case is
the last letter carrying its own built-in *a*. **Look at the final letter and
take its vowel sign off; that is the whole operation, whatever the tense was.**
