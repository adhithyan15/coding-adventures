## Twenty-two lessons stop asking for untaught letters

- Script closure (a lesson may ask the reader to decode only letters an
  earlier lesson taught, its own romanized headword aside) found twenty-two
  Bengali violations; there are now none.
- Words whose letters are not yet taught are given in romanization, not in
  Bengali script. These include the second-pass goodbyes (*ābār dækhā hôbe*,
  *kāl dækhā hôbe*), example sentences that run ahead of the letters
  (*āmi bhālo āchhi*, *āpni kēmon āchhen?*, *maf korben, êktu jāygā debên?*),
  the Sanskrit and tatsama cousins (*śveta*/*śveto*, *kṣamā korben*, *bhū*,
  *jñ*), cross-references to later verbs (*thākā*, *jijñāsā kôrā*, *bujhi*,
  *pôṛā*), and *nil* in the color review, whose long-ī sign is not yet taught.
- `BN-C15-shari` names the third sibilant as *ṣ* until its letter is taught.
