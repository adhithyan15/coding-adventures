# ஆசை (ācai) — a wish, a longing

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[TA-GRAMMAR-C290-FEELING-STATE-01, TA-LEX-C229-ACT229-03, TA-LEX-C125-THINGS125-05] -->

## You'll want to know: ஆசை
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[TA-LEX-C290-ACAI-01]; assesses=[TA-LEX-C125-THINGS125-05, TA-LEX-C152-ACT152-03, TA-GRAMMAR-DATIVE-SUBJECT-02] -->

**ஆசை** — *ācai* — "a wish, a longing, a desire".

> **எனக்கு ஒரு ஆசை இருக்கிறது.**
>
> *eṉakku oru ācai irukkiṟatu.* — "I have a wish."

> **எனக்குக் கடலில் நீந்த ஆசை.**
>
> *eṉakkuk kaṭalil nīnta ācai.* — "I long to swim in the sea."

**ஆசை** goes the way of the feelings: the one who wishes takes **-க்கு**. What you long to do comes as a verb's to-form before it: **நீந்த ஆசை**, "a longing to swim". It covers a child's wish for a sweet and a grown person's hope for a whole life; the sentence tells you which.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**ஆசை** (*ācai*) is a loan from Sanskrit *āśā*, which Monier-Williams glosses "wish, desire, hope". The final *-ā* of the Sanskrit word became **-ஐ** in Tamil, the change you can hear in **கதை**, "a story", from Sanskrit *kathā*.
