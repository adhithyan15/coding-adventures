Latin, chapter 89: Cloud, Rain, Snow, Plain, Shore.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
nūbēs, nūbis — a cloud.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a valley, then the Latin for a cave.

You'll want to know: nūbēs, nūbis.
nūbēs, nūbis — "a cloud".

The word, taken apart.
nūbēs is probably inherited, from a Proto-Indo-European root (s)newdh-, "to cover, to veil" (EtymDB 2.1): a cloud covers the sky.

Lesson 2 of 5.
pluvia, pluviae — rain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a cave, then the Latin for a cloud.

You'll want to know: pluvia, pluviae.
pluvia, pluviae — "rain". The noun beside pluit, it rains.

The word, taken apart.
pluvia is the feminine of pluvius, "rainy", used as a noun with aqua, "water", understood (Lewis & Short): rainy water. pluvius is built on pluit, "it rains" (EtymDB 2.1).

Lesson 3 of 5.
nix, nivis — snow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a cloud, then the Latin for rain.

You'll want to know: nix, nivis.
nix, nivis — "snow".

The word, taken apart.
nix, nivis, is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European snigwhs, "snow" (EtymDB 2.1); Latin lost the first s. English snow and Greek nipha, "snow" (Lewis & Short), are cousins.

Lesson 4 of 5.
campus, campī — a plain, an open field.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for rain, then the Latin for snow.

You'll want to know: campus, campī.
campus, campī — "a plain, an open field". English campus.

The word, taken apart.
campus has no sure origin. EtymDB 2.1 tentatively links it to a Proto-Indo-European root kamp-, "to bend", and Lewis & Short compare Greek kēpos, "garden"; neither link is settled.

Lesson 5 of 5.
lītus, lītoris — a shore, a beach.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for snow, then the Latin for a plain.

You'll want to know: lītus, lītoris.
lītus, lītoris — "a shore, a beach".

The word, taken apart.
lītus has no sure origin; Lewis & Short compare Greek limēn, "harbour", but the link is uncertain. It is a different word from litus, "smeared", from linō, "to smear".
