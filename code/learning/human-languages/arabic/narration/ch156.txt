Arabic, chapter 156: Recently, Already, Last, Once, To Spend (Time).
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
حديثا (ḥadīthan) — recently.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for hot, then the Arabic for crowded.

You'll want to know: حديثا.
حديثا — ḥadīthan — "recently". Arabic's past for "I" ends in -تُ (-tu): قرأت حديثا كتابا جميلا — qaraʾtu ḥadīthan kitāban jamīlan — "I recently read a lovely book."

The word, taken apart.
حديثا (ḥadīthan) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-d-th, "to be new; to happen" (Lane). Ḥadīthan is ḥadīth, "new, recent", with the adverb ending -an.

Lesson 2 of 5.
بالفعل (bi-l-fiʿl) — already; indeed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for crowded, then the Arabic for recently.

You'll want to know: بالفعل.
بالفعل — bi-l-fiʿl — "already; indeed". With the past: أكلت بالفعل — akaltu bi-l-fiʿl — "I have already eaten."

The word, taken apart.
بالفعل (bi-l-fiʿl) is built from bi-, "by, in", the article al-, and fiʿl, "a deed", from the root f-ʿ-l, "to do" (Lane): "in deed", hence "already, indeed".

Lesson 3 of 5.
الماضي (al-māḍī) — last, past.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for recently, then the Arabic for already; indeed.

You'll want to know: الماضي.
الماضي — al-māḍī — "last, past". After a time word: الأسبوع الماضي — al-usbūʿ al-māḍī — "last week"; العام الماضي — al-ʿām al-māḍī — "last year".

The word, taken apart.
الماضي (al-māḍī) is built on the native Arabic root m-ḍ-y, "to pass, to go on" (Lane). Al-māḍī is the active participle with the article, "the one that has passed".

Lesson 4 of 5.
ذات مرة (dhāta marra) — once, one time.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for already; indeed, then the Arabic for last.

You'll want to know: ذات مرة.
ذات مرة — dhāta marra — "once, one time". It opens a story: ذات مرة كانت هنا بحيرة — dhāta marra kānat hunā buḥayra — "Once there was a lake here." كانت is the past of to be.

The word, taken apart.
ذات مرة (dhāta marra) is built from dhāta, "one possessing", and marra, "a time, a single passing", from the root m-r-r, "to pass" (Lane): "on one occasion".

Lesson 5 of 5.
قضى (qaḍā) — to spend (time).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for last, then the Arabic for once.

You'll want to know: قضى.
قضى — qaḍā — "to spend (time)". Said of time: قضيت العطلة في القرية — qaḍaytu al-ʿuṭla fī al-qarya — "I spent the holiday in the village."

The word, taken apart.
قضى (qaḍā) is built on the native Arabic root q-ḍ-y, "to decide; to accomplish" (Lane). Lane's qaḍā is to accomplish, to finish, hence to spend time.
