Lesson 1 of 6.
il rapporto — relationship; report.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for relatives, then the Italian for to look like.

You'll want to know: il rapporto.
il rapporto — "relationship; report".
Ho un buon rapporto con i miei genitori. I have a good relationship with my parents.
At work, un rapporto can also be a report.

The word, taken apart.
rapporto is built on rapportare "to bring back, to report", from ri-, a- and portare "to carry", inherited from Latin portāre (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 6.
separarsi — to separate, to split up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to look like, then the Italian for relationship.

You'll want to know: separarsi.
separarsi — "to separate, to split up".
I miei genitori si sono separati quando ero piccolo. My parents separated when I was little.
It takes essere. Separati — separated, not yet divorced.

The word, taken apart.
separarsi is the reflexive of separare, a learned borrowing from Latin sēparāre "to part, to divide" (Lewis & Short), from sē- "apart" and parāre.

Lesson 4 of 6.
lasciarsi — to break up.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to separate, then the Italian for separation.

You'll want to know: lasciarsi.
lasciarsi — "to break up".
Ci siamo lasciati dopo tre anni insieme. We broke up after three years together.
The reflexive of lasciare "to leave": mi ha lasciato — he left me.

The word, taken apart.
lasciarsi is the reflexive of lasciare "to leave", inherited from Latin laxāre "to loosen, to release" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 6.
risposarsi — to remarry.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for separation, then the Italian for to break up.

You'll want to know: risposarsi.
risposarsi — "to remarry".
Dopo il divorzio mia madre si è risposata. After the divorce my mother remarried.
Ri- "again" plus sposarsi.

The word, taken apart.
risposarsi is built from ri- "again" and sposarsi "to marry", inherited from Latin spōnsāre "to betroth" (Lewis & Short), from spondēre "to promise".
