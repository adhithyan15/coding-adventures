Spanish, chapter 555: La vejez.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
la vejez — old age.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the age of majority, then the Spanish for to grow old.

You'll want to know: la vejez.
la vejez — "old age".
Vivió una vejez tranquila en el campo. He lived a quiet old age in the country.

The word, taken apart.
vejez is built on viejo 'old' with the ending -ez. Viejo is inherited from Latin vetulus, from vetus 'old' (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: muerto.
muerto — "dead; the dead person".
El accidente dejó dos muertos. The accident left two people dead.
Used with estar: Está muerto. Muerto de hambre — starving.

The word, taken apart.
muerto is inherited from Latin mortuus 'dead' (EtymDB), the participle of morī 'to die', through spoken Latin mortus.

Lesson 5 of 5.
el difunto — the deceased, the late.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for death, then the Spanish for dead.

You'll want to know: el difunto.
el difunto — "the deceased, the late".
La familia del difunto llegó a la iglesia. The family of the deceased arrived at the church.
Formal. As an adjective: mi difunto marido — my late husband.

The word, taken apart.
difunto comes from Latin dēfūnctus, the participle of dēfungī 'to finish, to discharge' (Lewis & Short). The dead are those who have finished their life's work.
