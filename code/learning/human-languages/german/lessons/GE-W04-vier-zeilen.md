# Four lines, and the joiner you can see

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-UND-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The last lesson asked for writing that was **finished on time**. This
one asks for writing that **holds together**.

[PAUSE 1s] Pick one person who will read it.

## You'll want to know
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-ABER-01, GE-LEX-WEIL-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Connected writing is not **longer** writing. Four short lines can be
connected; one long line can fail to be. What makes it connected is that a
sentence stops standing on its own.

[PAUSE 2s] You hold three joiners, and German does something with the third that
the first two do not. **und** adds. **aber** sets against. Both leave the
sentence they join exactly as it was.

[PAUSE 2s] **weil** does not. It picks the verb up and puts it at the **end**.

[PAUSE 2s] That is worth stopping on, because it means German shows you the join
in the word order itself. In most languages you can only tell that two clauses
were joined by reading the joining word. In German the second clause visibly
rearranges — and a reader who sees a verb arrive late knows a reason is being
given before they have finished the sentence.

## Writing — connected composition
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-WEIL-01, GE-GRAMMAR-VERB-LETZT-01] -->
<!-- hl-writing-stage: connected-composition -->

[PAUSE 2s] Write **four lines** to the person you picked.

At least one must use **und** or **aber**. At least one must use **weil** — and
in that one, check the verb has actually moved.

[PAUSE 3s] Use only words you already write. This lesson adds no vocabulary; the
whole exercise is the joining.

## Wrap-up recall
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-VERB-LETZT-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Cover your first line and read the second alone. Does it still make
sense without the first? If it does, you wrote a **list** — which is where you
were one lesson ago.

[PAUSE 2s] Then find your **weil** line and point at the verb. Is it last? If it
is sitting where it would sit in a sentence on its own, the word is doing
nothing: you wrote *und* with a different spelling.
