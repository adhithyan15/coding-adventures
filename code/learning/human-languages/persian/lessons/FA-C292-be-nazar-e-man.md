# به نظر من (be nazar-e man) — to my view, which is to say, in my opinion

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[FA-LEX-C146-KONESH146-04, FA-LEX-FEKR-KARDAN, FA-GRAMMAR-STORY-ORDER-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "to seem", then "to think". (*Be nazar residan*, *fekr
kardan*.)

[PAUSE 1s] A story says what happened. Giving reasons starts one step
earlier, with what you think about it.

## You'll want to know: به نظر من
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-LEX-BE-NAZAR-E-MAN-01]; assesses=[FA-GRAMMAR-EZAFE-OWNER, FA-LEX-C243-CHIZ243-03, FA-LEX-SHOMA-TO] -->

**به نظر من** — *be nazar-e man* — "in my opinion", "if you ask me".

> **به نظر من این فیلم خیلی خوب است.** — *be nazar-e man in film kheyli khub
> ast* — In my opinion this film is very good.

Word for word it is "to the view of me": *be*, "to", *nazar*, "view", and the
ezafe joining *man* as its owner. Change the owner to hand the opinion back:
**به نظر شما؟**, *be nazar-e shomâ?*, "what do you think?".

It is the *nazar* of *be nazar residan*, "to seem", "to reach the view". The
present of that verb opens a careful opinion: **به نظر میرسد که...**, *be nazar
miresad ke...*, "it seems that...".

## Grammar Lens: fekr mikonam ke
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[FA-GRAMMAR-FEKR-MIKONAM-KE-01]; assesses=[FA-GRAMMAR-PRESENT-MI-01, FA-GRAMMAR-NEGATIVE-NE-01, FA-LEX-C66-TIME66-04, FA-LEX-C70-TOWN70-05] -->

The verb you have held since chapter seven, in the present, with **که**,
*ke*, "that":

> **فکر میکنم که قطار دیر میرسد.** — *fekr mikonam ke qatâr dir miresad* — I
> think the train will be late.

*Ke* opens the thought, and the thought keeps its ordinary verb, *miresad*.
In speech *ke* is often dropped: *fekr mikonam qatâr dir miresad*.

"I don't think so" is the negative with nothing after it: **فکر نمیکنم**,
*fekr nemikonam*.

## The phrase, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

*nazar* is borrowed from Arabic *naẓar*, "a look, a view" (EtymDB 2.1); *be*
and *man* are Persian. So the phrase sets an Arabic noun inside a Persian
frame, the way *fekr kardan* sets Arabic *fekr*, "thought", in front of a
Persian verb.
