Arabic, chapter 307: Armies and wars.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
جيش (jaysh) — an army.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to escort a bride, then the Arabic for to ululate for joy.

You'll want to know: جيش.
جيش — jaysh — "an army".
عبر الجيش النهر في الليل. ʿabara al-jaysh an-nahr fī al-layl. The army crossed the river at night.
Plural: جيوش, juyūsh.

The word, taken apart.
جيش (jaysh) is built on the native Arabic root j-y-sh (Lane), where jāsha means "to rise, to heave, to surge". Lane gives jaysh as an army, or men going out to war.

كان جدي جنديا في شبابه. kāna jaddī jundiyyan fī shabābihi. My grandfather was a soldier in his youth.
Plural: جنود, junūd.

The word, taken apart.
جندي (jundī) is built on jund, "troops, a host", filed under the root j-n-d (Lane), with the nisba ending -ī: "one of the troops".

Lesson 3 of 5.
حرب (ḥarb) — a war.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for an army, then the Arabic for a soldier.

You'll want to know: حرب.
حرب — ḥarb — "a war".
كان جدي صغيرا في زمن الحرب. kāna jaddī ṣaghīran fī zaman al-ḥarb. My grandfather was a small boy in wartime.
Plural: حروب, ḥurūb.
حرب (ḥarb) is feminine: حرب (ḥarb) طويلة, a long war.

The word, taken apart.
حرب (ḥarb) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-r-b (Lane), where ḥaraba is "to strip a man of his goods". War, which strips people of their goods, is usually explained from it.

Lesson 4 of 5.
معركة (maʿraka) — a battle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a soldier, then the Arabic for a war.

You'll want to know: معركة.
معركة — maʿraka — "a battle".
وقعت معركة كبيرة قرب النهر. waqaʿat maʿraka kabīra qurba an-nahr. A big battle took place near the river.
Plural: معارك, maʿārik.

The word, taken apart.
معركة (maʿraka) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-r-k (Lane), "to rub hard, to grind". Maʿraka is the place where fighters grind against each other: the field of battle.

Lesson 5 of 5.
حارب (ḥāraba) — to wage war; to fight against.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a war, then the Arabic for a battle.

You'll want to know: حارب.
حارب — ḥāraba — "to wage war; to fight against".
حارب الأمير جيشا كبيرا. ḥāraba al-amīr jayshan kabīran. The prince fought a large army.
Present: يحارب, yuḥārib.
It also fights a problem: حاربت المدينة التلوث.

The word, taken apart.
حارب (ḥāraba) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-r-b (Lane), the root of ḥarb, war. It is Form III, "to make war with someone".
