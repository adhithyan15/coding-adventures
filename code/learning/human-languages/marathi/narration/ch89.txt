Lesson 1 of 5.
कांदा (kāndā) — an onion.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for an egg, then the Marathi for yoghurt.

You'll want to know: कांदा.
कांदा — kāndā — "an onion".

The word, taken apart.
कांदा (kāndā) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit kanda, "bulbous root" (CDIAL 2723). CDIAL takes the Sanskrit word as probably from Dravidian.

Lesson 3 of 5.
खिडकी (khiḍkī) — a window.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for an onion, then the Marathi for a mango.

You'll want to know: खिडकी.
खिडकी — khiḍkī — "a window".

The word, taken apart.
खिडकी (khiḍkī) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from khaṭakkikā, "side door" (CDIAL 3770). CDIAL glosses the Marathi word "back door, window", and suggests the word first named something made of matting.

Lesson 4 of 5.
खुर्ची (khurchī) — a chair.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Marathi for a mango, then the Marathi for a window.

You'll want to know: खुर्ची.
खुर्ची — khurchī — "a chair".

The word, taken apart.
खुर्ची (khurchī) is a loan from Arabic kursī, "chair, throne", which reached Marathi through Persian. Its Arabic root is k-r-s (Lane). Marathi writes it with kh-.
