## You'll want to know: 只有
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ZH-LEX-C399-ONLYIF399-02]; assesses=[] -->

**只有** — *zhǐyǒu* — "only if (with cái after it)".

> **只有你去他才会去** *zhǐyǒu nǐ qù tā cái huì qù* — He'll only go if you go.

The new character, in pieces: 只 — five strokes: 口 on top, and 八 under it. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

Two third tones meet in this word: zhǐ is said with a rising tone before yǒu, and the spelling keeps both marks.

## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

**只有** (*zhǐyǒu*) is built from *zhǐ* 'only' and 有 'to have'. The second is inherited from Middle Chinese *hjuwX* (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription); the dictionary lists the first, *tsyeX* there, as a particle.

## What you've built
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[] -->

One more word for saying what would happen.
