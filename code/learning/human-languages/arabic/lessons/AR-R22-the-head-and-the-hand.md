# The head and the hand

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C11-RAS-01, AR-CONCEPT-C11-YAD-01] -->

[PAUSE 4s] Touch your head, then your hand, and give the Arabic for each. Both
arrived a dozen lessons ago in single words and have not been asked for since.

## Across the family: five words older than Arabic
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C11-RAS-01, AR-CONCEPT-C11-YAD-01, AR-CONCEPT-C11-YAD-02, AR-CONCEPT-C10-AB-UMM-02, AR-CONCEPT-C10-AKH-UKHT-02] -->

| Arabic | meaning | Hebrew cousin |
|---|---|---|
| **رأس** *raʾs* | head | **ראש** *rosh* |
| **يد** *yad* | hand | **יד** *yad* |
| **أب** *ab* | father | **אב** *av* |
| **أم** *umm* | mother | **אם** *em* |
| **أخ** *akh* | brother | **אח** *ach* |

These are not borrowings in either direction. Both languages inherited them, and
**يد** is the most striking of the set: spelled and said almost identically on
both sides, worn down hardly at all in several thousand years.

**رأس** is the word inside *Rosh Hashanah*, the head of the year.

## Across the family: what the roots still build
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C11-RAS-02, AR-CONCEPT-C10-AB-UMM-02, AR-CONCEPT-C10-AKH-UKHT-02] -->

None of these roots is a museum piece.

- **رئيس** *raʾīs* — **president, chief** — is built straight on *head*, the same
  way English *capital* and *captain* grow out of Latin *caput*.
- **أم** and **أبو** still form the **kunya**, a living honorific: *mother of*
  and *father of*, plus a child's name.
- **أخت** *ukht* is **أخ** with a single letter added — the simplest gendering in
  the language, against the reshaped templates the colour words needed.

## Grammar Lens: an age is a thing you own
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[AR-CONCEPT-C17-KAM-UMRUKA-01, AR-CONCEPT-C17-KAM-UMRUKA-02] -->

**عمر** *ʿumr* is **age, lifetime, life span**, from the root **ع-م-ر**, tied to
living and flourishing.

> **كم عمرك؟** *kam ʿumruka?* — literally **how much is your lifetime?**

The Romance languages **have** their years; the Germanic ones **are** their years.
Arabic does neither. It hangs the same *your* suffix on a noun and drops the verb
altogether, exactly as the name and wellbeing questions do.
