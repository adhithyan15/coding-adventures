Japanese, chapter 518: Slips and Second Chances.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
とりもどす (torimodosu) — to get back; to regain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to judge, then the Japanese for an option.

You'll want to know: とりもどす.
とりもどす — torimodosu — "to get back; to regain".
もし びょうきが なおったら、かれは げんきを とりもどすでしょう。 — moshi byōki ga naottara, kare wa genki o torimodosu deshō — "If his illness gets better, he will get his strength back."
Polite とりもどします, torimodoshimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): かれが げんきを とりもどしました。, kare ga genki o torimodoshimashita.

The word, taken apart.
とりもどす (torimodosu) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, built from toru, "to take", and modosu, "to put back"; its kanji was attached later as a kun'yomi (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
しくじる (shikujiru) — to slip up; to fail (at something).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to learn one's lesson, then the Japanese for so close.

You'll want to know: しくじる.
しくじる — shikujiru — "to slip up; to fail (at something)".
もし しけんで しくじったら、らいねん もう いちど うけます。 — moshi shiken de shikujittara, rainen mō ichido ukemasu — "If I slip up in the exam, I will take it again next year."
Polite しくじります, shikujirimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): おとうとが しけんで しくじりました。, otōto ga shiken de shikujirimashita.

The word, taken apart.
しくじる (shikujiru) is usually counted a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: JMdict gives it no kanji spelling, and no dictionary we use records an older form, so its history is not settled (JMdict).

Lesson 5 of 5.
おちど (ochido) — a fault; an oversight.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for so close, then the Japanese for to slip up.

You'll want to know: おちど.
おちど — ochido — "a fault; an oversight".
もし みせに おちどが あったら、あやまります。 — moshi mise ni ochido ga attara, ayamarimasu — "If the shop is at fault, we will apologize."

The word, taken apart.
おちど (ochido) mixes the two layers, native and Sino-Japanese: native ochi, "falling", from ochiru, "to fall", and Sino-Japanese do, "degrees" (Middle Chinese duH), borrowed from Chinese (JMdict, KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
