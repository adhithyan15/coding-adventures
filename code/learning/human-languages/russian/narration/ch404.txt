Russian, chapter 404: Under the bonnet.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
капот (kapót) — a bonnet (of a car); a hood.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to speed up, then the Russian for to sound the horn.

You'll want to know: капот.
капот (kapót) — "a bonnet (of a car); a hood".
Водитель вышел и открыл капот. Vodítel výshel i otkrýl kapót. The driver got out and opened the bonnet.
Masculine. In American English, the hood.

The word, taken apart.
капот (kapót) was borrowed from French capote, a hooded cloak (EtymDB): the engine wears a hood. EtymDB traces capote back to Latin caput 'head'.

Lesson 2 of 5.
двигатель (dvígatel) — an engine, a motor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to sound the horn, then the Russian for a bonnet.

You'll want to know: двигатель.
двигатель (dvígatel) — "an engine, a motor".
У новой машины тихий двигатель. U nóvoy mashíny tíkhiy dvígatel. The new car has a quiet engine.
Masculine, though it ends in -ь: мой двигатель (dvígatel).

The word, taken apart.
двигатель (dvígatel) is built on двигать 'to move' with the ending -тель, 'the thing that does it'. Двигать is inherited from Proto-Slavic dvigati 'to lift, to move' (EtymDB).

Lesson 3 of 5.
аккумулятор (akkumulyátor) — a car battery; a rechargeable battery.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a bonnet, then the Russian for an engine.

You'll want to know: аккумулятор.
аккумулятор (akkumulyátor) — "a car battery; a rechargeable battery".
Ночью был сильный холод, и аккумулятор сел. Nóchyu byl sílnyy khólod, i akkumulyátor sel. It was very cold in the night, and the battery went flat.
Аккумулятор сел — the battery is flat. Small batteries for a remote are батарейки.

The word, taken apart.
аккумулятор (akkumulyátor) is usually traced to Latin accumulātor 'one who heaps up', from accumulāre 'to pile up' (EtymDB lists the Latin words): a battery stores up charge. EtymDB does not list the Russian word, and the route into Russian, probably through German, is not settled.

Lesson 4 of 5.
заводить (zavodít) — to start (an engine); to wind up (a clock).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for an engine, then the Russian for a car battery.

You'll want to know: заводить.
заводить (zavodít) — "to start (an engine); to wind up (a clock)".
Каждое утро сосед заводит машину в семь часов. Kázhdoye útro soséd zavódit mashínu v sem chasóv. Every morning the neighbour starts his car at seven.
Imperfective; the perfective is завести (заведу, заведёшь). Present завожу, заводишь. Машина не заводится — the car will not start.

The word, taken apart.
заводить (zavodít) is built from за- and водить 'to lead'. Водить is inherited from Proto-Slavic voditi 'to lead' (EtymDB).

Lesson 5 of 5.
глохнуть (glókhnut) — to stall (of an engine); to go deaf.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for a car battery, then the Russian for to start.

You'll want to know: глохнуть.
глохнуть (glókhnut) — "to stall (of an engine); to go deaf".
Старый мотор часто глохнет на светофоре. Stáryy motór chásto glókhnet na svetofóre. The old engine often stalls at the traffic lights.
Imperfective; perfective заглохнуть. Past глох, глохла: the -ну- drops out.

The word, taken apart.
глохнуть (glókhnut) is inherited from Proto-Slavic glŭxnǫti (EtymDB), the verb that goes with глухой 'deaf': an engine that stalls goes silent.
