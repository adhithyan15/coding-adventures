## The word, taken apart
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[LA-LEX-C44-HOUSE-02]; assesses=[] -->

**porta** — "**a door**," or a **gate**.

The root is Proto-Indo-European ***\*per-***, "to pass through," and that is
worth holding onto, because it explains a family of English words that look
unrelated.

A **portal** is a *porta* made grand. A **porch** is a *porticus*, the covered
way in front of one. And a **port** is a ***portus*** — the same root again, a
place where you pass through, but from the sea rather than from the street.

Rome named its gates and remembered them: the *Porta Capēna*, the *Porta
Flāminia*. A city's gates were its addresses.

Two things to keep apart, because Latin does. **Porta** is the *opening*.
**Ianua** — the door leaf that swings in it — is a different word, and it is the
one that named **January**, the month standing in the doorway of the year.
