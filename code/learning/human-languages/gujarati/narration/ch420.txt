Gujarati, chapter 420: Small Houses and Fences.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
કમાડ (kamāḍ) — a wooden door; a door leaf.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for a resident, then the Gujarati for to wander aimlessly.

You'll want to know: કમાડ.
કમાડ — kamāḍ — "a wooden door; a door leaf".
પવનથી ઘરનું જૂનું કમાડ ખખડે છે. pavanthī gharnũ jūnũ kamāḍ khakhḍe che. The old wooden door of the house rattles in the wind.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
કમાડ (kamāḍ) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit kavāṭa, "the leaf of a door"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati word, "a door" (CDIAL 2963).

નળિયું — naḷiyũ — "a roof tile (a curved clay tile)".
તોફાનમાં ઘરનાં ઘણાં નળિયાં તૂટી ગયાં. tophānmā̃ gharnā̃ ghaṇā̃ naḷiyā̃ tūṭī gayā̃. Many roof tiles of the house broke in the storm.
A neuter noun.

The word, taken apart.
નળિયું (naḷiyũ) seems to be built on નળ, a pipe, which goes back to Sanskrit naḍa, "a reed" (CDIAL 6936): a curved tile is like half a pipe.

You'll want to know: છાવું.
છાવું — chāvũ — "to thatch; to roof (a house)".
દાદાએ ઘાસથી ઘર છાયું. dādāe ghāsthī ghar chāyũ. Grandfather thatched the house with grass.
Past, with તેણે (he or she …): તેણે ઘર છાયું, teṇe ghar chāyũ.

The word, taken apart.
છાવું (chāvũ) is a tadbhava of Sanskrit chādáyati, "covers, veils"; CDIAL lists the Gujarati verb, "to thatch" (CDIAL 5018): to cover a house with a roof.
