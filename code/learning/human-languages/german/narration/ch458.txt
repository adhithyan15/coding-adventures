German, chapter 458: Tiere im Wald.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
das Eichhörnchen — the squirrel.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the bay, then the German for the shell.

You'll want to know: das Eichhörnchen.
das Eichhörnchen — "the squirrel".
Ein Eichhörnchen sammelt Nüsse für den Winter. A squirrel is collecting nuts for the winter.
Same form in the plural. In Austria and the south also das Eichkätzchen.

The word, taken apart.
Eichhörnchen goes back to Old High German eihhorno and Proto-Germanic aikwernô 'squirrel' (EtymDB); later speakers heard Eiche 'oak' and Horn in it and added -chen 'little'.

Lesson 3 of 5.

You'll want to know: das Wildschwein.
das Wildschwein — "the wild boar".
Nachts kommen die Wildschweine bis an das Dorf. At night the wild boar come right up to the village.
Plural: die Wildschweine.

The word, taken apart.
Wildschwein is built from wild 'wild' and Schwein 'pig', both inherited: Schwein goes back to Proto-Germanic swīną and is a cousin of English swine (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: der Hirsch.
der Hirsch — "the stag, the deer".
Im Herbst hört man die Hirsche im Wald rufen. In autumn you can hear the stags calling in the forest.
Plural: die Hirsche. The female is die Hirschkuh.

The word, taken apart.
Hirsch is inherited through Middle High German hirz from Old High German hiruz and Proto-Germanic herutaz (EtymDB); English hart 'male deer' is a cousin.

Lesson 5 of 5.
zwitschern — to chirp, to twitter.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the German for the wild boar, then the German for the stag.

You'll want to know: zwitschern.
zwitschern — "to chirp, to twitter".
Am Morgen zwitschern die Vögel vor dem Fenster. In the morning the birds chirp outside the window.
Das Zwitschern — birdsong.

The word, taken apart.
zwitschern is a sound word that imitates the calls of small birds; EtymDB lists the sense 'to twitter', and English twitter is formed the same way.
