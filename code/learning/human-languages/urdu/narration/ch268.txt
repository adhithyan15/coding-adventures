Urdu, chapter 268: What I Think, and Why.
7 lessons. You can do the first 3 of them in the car; after that you will want to have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 7.
لگنا (lagnā) — to seem, to be felt.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "hunger" and "thirst". (bhūk, pyās.) Urdu usually says "I am hungry" another way: hunger has struck you.

You'll want to know: لگنا.
لگنا — lagnā — "to seem; to be felt".
مجھے بھوک لگی ہے۔ mujhe bhūk lagī hai. — "I'm hungry."
The one who feels it takes مجھے (mujhe), and the verb follows the thing felt: بھوک (bhūk) is feminine, so لگی (lagī).
مجھے یہ فلم اچھی لگی۔ mujhe yih film achchhī lagī. — "I liked this film."
Word for word, "this film seemed good to me". فلم is feminine: اچھی لگی.

The word, taken apart.
لگنا (lagnā) is inherited. CDIAL traces it to Sanskrit lagyati, "adheres to" (CDIAL 10895): a hunger or a liking attaches itself to the one who feels it.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: lagnā, then mujhe bhūk lagī hai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I'm thirsty" — mujhe pyās lagī hai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I liked this film" — mujhe yih film achchhī lagī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does لگنا (lagnā) mean? ("To seem, to be felt".) "I'm hungry"? (مجھے بھوک لگی ہے.) Why لگی? (بھوک is feminine.)


Lesson 2 of 7.
خیال (khayāl) — a thought, an idea.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "I liked it", of a film. (mujhe achchhī lagī.) Then "to think". (sochnā.)

You'll want to know: خیال.
خیال — khayāl — "a thought; an idea". It is masculine.
یہ اچھا خیال ہے۔ yih achchhā khayāl hai. — "That's a good idea."
مجھے ایک خیال آیا۔ mujhe ek khayāl āyā. — "An idea came to me."
An idea comes to you, as hunger strikes you: مجھے, and آیا (āyā) for a masculine thought.

The word, taken apart.
خیال (khayāl) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic khayāl, "a fancy, an image in the mind".

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: khayāl, then yih achchhā khayāl hai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "That's a bad idea" — yih burā khayāl hai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "An idea came to me" — mujhe ek khayāl āyā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does خیال (khayāl) mean? ("A thought, an idea".) Masculine or feminine? (Masculine: اچھا خیال (khayāl).)


Lesson 3 of 7.
غلط (ghalat) — wrong.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say "a good idea". (achchhā khayāl.) Then "fine, correct". (ṭhīk.) This lesson gives its opposite.

You'll want to know: غلط.
غلط — ghalat — "wrong".
یہ جواب غلط ہے۔ yih javāb ghalat hai. — "This answer is wrong."
میں غلط تھا۔ maiṅ ghalat thā. — "I was wrong."
It does not change for a man or a woman: میں غلط تھی (maiṅ ghalat thī).

The word, taken apart.
غلط (ghalat) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic ghalaṭ, "an error". Its first letter is new to your hand; the next lesson takes it out.

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: ghalat, then yih javāb ghalat hai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "This answer is right" — yih javāb ṭhīk hai]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I was wrong", as a woman — maiṅ ghalat thī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does غلط (ghalat) mean? ("Wrong".) Its opposite? (ٹھیک.)


Lesson 4 of 7.
غ (ghain) — the letter that opens غلط (ghalat).

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on, your eyes, for letter shapes on the page and your eyes or your hands for the steps that ask you to read, point, write or gesture. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Warm-up, Script you'll notice: غ and Guided Practice.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write ع once, and name it. (ain.)
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say غلط (ghalat), "wrong". This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Script you'll notice: غ.
غ — ghain, the sound gh, made far back in the throat.
It opens غلط (ghalat). You already write ع: غ (ghain) is that shape with one dot above it. At the start of a word, as here, it is the small open head alone, joined on to the letter after it; standing alone it keeps the deep bowl below the line.

Writing: غ.
Put your pen on غ (ghain) and follow its line. Copy the shape you can see — slowly, and larger than it is printed.
This book does not yet tell you where to start this letter or which way to travel. It is not written down here until it can be written down with a source. Copying what is in front of you needs no such source, and it is how the shape gets into your hand in the meantime.

[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[once you have stopped driving — look: at غلط (ghalat) and point at غ (ghain) inside it]
[once you have stopped driving — trace: غ (ghain) three times, saying gh as you finish each one]
[once you have stopped driving — write: غلط (ghalat) once, joined, with the dot above its first letter]
[once you have stopped driving — read: غلط (ghalat) aloud once more]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
Which letter is this — غ? (ghain, gh.) What tells it from ع? (The dot.) Which word did it come from? (غلط, ghalat, "wrong".)


Lesson 5 of 7.
مجھے لگتا ہے (mujhe lagtā hai) — what I think.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "I'm hungry". (mujhe bhūk lagī hai.) Then "it will rain". (bārish ho gī.) Join the two verbs, and you have an opinion.

You'll want to know: مجھے لگتا ہے کہ.
مجھے لگتا ہے۔ mujhe lagtā hai. — "I think so."
Word for word, "it seems to me". کہ (ki), "that", hangs the thought on it:
مجھے لگتا ہے کہ بارش ہو گی۔ mujhe lagtā hai ki bārish ho gī. — "I think it will rain."
مجھے لگتا ہے کہ وہ نہیں آئے گا۔ mujhe lagtā hai ki voh nahīṅ āe gā. — "I don't think he'll come."

You'll want to know: in my view.
میرے خیال میں یہ ٹھیک ہے۔ mere khayāl meṅ yih ṭhīk hai. — "In my view, this is right."
میری رائے میں یہ غلط ہے۔ merī rāe meṅ yih ghalat hai. — "In my opinion, this is wrong."
خیال (khayāl) is masculine, so میرے (mere); رائے (rāe) is feminine, so میری (merī). To ask:
آپ کا کیا خیال ہے؟ āp kā kyā khayāl hai? — "What do you think?"

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "I think it will rain" — mujhe lagtā hai ki bārish ho gī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I think she will come" — mujhe lagtā hai ki voh āe gī]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "I think they will come" — mujhe lagtā hai ki voh āeṅ ge]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 22 seconds]
Say that you think it will rain, then ask what your friend thinks.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"I think so"? (مجھے لگتا ہے (mujhe lagtā hai).) "In my view"? (میرے خیال (khayāl) میں.) Why میری رائے? (رائے is feminine.)


Lesson 6 of 7.
کی وجہ سے (kī vajah se) — the reason.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Say "because" and "a reason". (kyūṅki, vajah.) Then "I think so". (mujhe lagtā hai.) An opinion wants a reason.

You'll want to know: کیونکہ and چونکہ.
میں نہیں آیا۔ maiṅ nahīṅ āyā. — "I didn't come."
میں نہیں آیا کیونکہ میں بیمار تھا۔ maiṅ nahīṅ āyā kyūṅki maiṅ bīmār thā. — "I didn't come because I was ill."
کیونکہ (kyūṅki) puts the reason second. چونکہ (chūṅki) puts it first, and the other half opens with اس لیے (is liye):
چونکہ میں بیمار تھا، chūṅki maiṅ bīmār thā, اس لیے میں نہیں آیا۔ is liye maiṅ nahīṅ āyā. — "Since I was ill, I didn't come."

You'll want to know: a noun as the reason.
بارش کی وجہ سے ہم گھر پر رہے۔ bārish kī vajah se ham ghar par rahe. — "Because of the rain we stayed at home."
وجہ (vajah) is feminine, so کی (kī). The noun goes in front:
دیر کی وجہ سے وہ ناراض تھی۔ der kī vajah se voh nārāz thī. — "She was cross because of the delay."

Guided Practice.
[pause 1 second]
[your turn — say: "Because of the rain I stayed at home" — bārish kī vajah se maiṅ ghar par rahā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Because of the delay I stayed at home" — der kī vajah se maiṅ ghar par rahā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: "Because of the heat I stayed at home" — garmī kī vajah se maiṅ ghar par rahā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[question — say your answer, then pause 26 seconds]
Say that you didn't come because you were ill, then that you stayed at home because of the rain.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
"Because I was ill"? (کیونکہ میں بیمار تھا.) "Because of the rain"? (بارش کی وجہ سے (kī vajah se).) Why کی? (وجہ is feminine.)
