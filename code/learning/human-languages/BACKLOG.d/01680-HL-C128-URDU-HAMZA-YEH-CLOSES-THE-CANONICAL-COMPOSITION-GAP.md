## HL-C128 — Urdu hamza-ye closes the canonical composition gap

After Urdu **ت** landed, decomposed Arabic **ي** led the measured queue at
**3 affected realizations**. All three came from Urdu words containing
precomposed **ئ**: Unicode canonically decomposes U+0626 into U+064A plus
U+0654 even though standalone Arabic yeh is not an Urdu inventory letter.

Zer o Zabar's *Ain and hamza* lesson prints initial and medial hamza on its
tooth carrier, supplies handwriting animations for both, and identifies the
mark's vowel-separator role with **بھائی** among its examples. The sourced
composition writes the right-to-left main-line carrier first and adds hamza
after a lift; the lesson also records the accepted diagonal-squiggle variant.

This closes all 3 canonical **ي**/**ٔ** gaps through the verified **ئ**
composition without conflating Urdu **ی** (U+06CC) with Arabic **ي**
(U+064A). Japanese **わ** now leads the measured queue at **2 affected
realizations**.
