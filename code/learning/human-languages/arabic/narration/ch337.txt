Arabic, chapter 337: Wills and inheritance.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
وصية (waṣiyya) — a will, a testament; advice.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a dowry, then the Arabic for the eve.

You'll want to know: وصية.
وصية — waṣiyya — "a will, a testament; advice".
كتب الرجل وصيته قبل موته. kataba ar-rajul waṣiyyatahu qabla mawtihi. The man wrote his will before he died.
Plural: وصايا, waṣāyā.

The word, taken apart.
وصية (waṣiyya) is built on the native Arabic root w-ṣ-y (Lane), "to enjoin, to give a charge". A waṣiyya is the charge a person leaves behind.

ترك الأب لأولاده ميراثا صغيرا. taraka al-ab li-awlādihi mīrāthan ṣaghīran. The father left his children a small inheritance.

The word, taken apart.
ميراث (mīrāth) is built on the native Arabic root w-r-th (Lane), "to inherit". The w of the root shows as ī after the mi- of the pattern.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ورث (waritha) — to inherit.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a will, then the Arabic for an inheritance.

You'll want to know: ورث.
ورث — waritha — "to inherit".
ورثت البنت بيتا من جدتها. warithat al-bint baytan min jaddatihā. The girl inherited a house from her grandmother.
Present: يرث, yarith.
The present drops the و: يرث.

The word, taken apart.
ورث (waritha) is built on the native Arabic root w-r-th (Lane), "to inherit"; mīrāth and turāth are both what is inherited.

كان الابن الوارث الوحيد. kāna al-ibn al-wārith al-waḥīd. The son was the only heir.

The word, taken apart.
وارث (wārith) is built on the native Arabic root w-r-th (Lane), "to inherit". Wārith is the active participle, "one who inherits".

Lesson 5 of 5.
أرملة (armala) — a widow.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to inherit, then the Arabic for an heir.

You'll want to know: أرملة.
أرملة — armala — "a widow".
عاشت الأرملة مع ابنتها. ʿāshat al-armala maʿa ibnatihā. The widow lived with her daughter.
Plural: أرامل, arāmil.
A widower is أرمل.

The word, taken apart.
أرملة (armala) is built on the native Arabic root r-m-l (Lane), where Lane gives armala as a woman without a husband, and armal, a man in need.
