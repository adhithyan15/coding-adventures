Spanish, chapter 647: Cruzar la calle.
6 lessons. All 6 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 6.
el semáforo — the traffic lights.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the fuel, then the Spanish for the kilometre.

You'll want to know: el semáforo.
el semáforo — "the traffic lights".
Espera, el semáforo está en rojo. Wait, the lights are red.
One word for the whole set of lights: el semáforo.

The word, taken apart.
semáforo was borrowed from French sémaphore (EtymDB), a word made from Greek sêma "sign" and -phóros "carrying": a thing that carries signals.

What you've built.

la bocacalle — "the turning, the side-street opening".
Gire en la segunda bocacalle a la derecha. Take the second turning on the right.
Built from boca and calle: the mouth of a street.

The word, taken apart.
bocacalle is built from boca "mouth", inherited from Latin bucca "cheek", and calle "street", from Latin callis "footpath".

You'll want to know: la calzada.
la calzada — "the roadway, the carriageway".
No juegues en la calzada. Do not play in the road.
The part of the street for cars; people walk beside it.

The word, taken apart.
calzada is usually traced to Vulgar Latin (via) calciāta "a paved road", built on Latin calx, calcis "limestone" (Lewis & Short); the details are not settled.

Lesson 5 of 6.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the roadway, then the Spanish for the pedestrian.

You'll want to know: la rotonda.
la rotonda — "the roundabout".
En la rotonda, toma la segunda salida. At the roundabout, take the second exit.
In Spain also la glorieta.

The word, taken apart.
rotonda was borrowed from Italian rotonda "a round building", from Latin rotundus "round" (Lewis & Short), which is built on rota "wheel".
