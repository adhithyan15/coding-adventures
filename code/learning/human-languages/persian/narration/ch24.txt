Persian, chapter 24: Letters From Asking and Saying.
4 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 4.
پ (pe) — the letter that opens پرسیدن.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the sections called Script you'll notice: پ and Guided Practice until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: write خ and چ once each, and name them.
[pause 2 seconds]
Now say پرسیدن (porsidan), "to ask". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: پ.
پ — pe, the sound p.
It opens پرسیدن (porsidan), "to ask". It is ب with three dots below instead of one, another letter Persian added to the Arabic alphabet, because Arabic has no p.

Writing: پ.
1. start here: sweep the shallow bowl from right to left.
2. lift, then place the left dot below.
3. lift again and place the right dot below.
4. lift again and place the lower-center dot — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 3.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Persian Online, How to Write Persian Characters, پ (pe) demonstration at 00:16–00:21 (Liberal Arts Instructional Technology Services, Univ. of Texas at Austin).

[pause 2 seconds]
Now say چیست (chist), "what is?". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ی.
ی — ye, the sound y, or a long i.
It is inside چیست (chist), where it gives the long i. Persian ی (ye) changes its coat by position: two dots below at the start or in the middle of a word, and no dots when it ends a word or stands alone, which is the shape this lesson writes.

Writing: ی.
1. start here: sweep left from the upper right and descend through the S curve.
2. continue around the below-baseline bowl and finish at its rising tip without lifting — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 0. The pen never leaves the paper.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Persian Online, How to Write Persian Characters, closing ی (ye) demonstration at 02:55–02:58 (Liberal Arts Instructional Technology Services, Univ. of Texas at Austin).

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. [pause 2 seconds]

[pause 2 seconds]
Now say فکر کردن (fekr kardan), "to think". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: ک.
ک — kâf, the sound k.
It is in both halves of فکر کردن (fekr kardan), "to think". A tall stem, a shallow bowl, and one slash across the top.

Writing: ک.
1. start here: draw the independent stem downward.
2. continue left through the shallow bowl and final hook without lifting.
3. lift once, then draw the long slash down from the upper right toward the stem — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 1.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Persian Online, How to Write Persian Characters, ک (kâf) demonstration at 02:19–02:23 (Liberal Arts Instructional Technology Services, Univ. of Texas at Austin).

Now say گفتن (goftan), "to say". You have read it for a long time. This lesson takes one letter out of it and puts it in your hand.

Script you'll notice: گ.
گ — gâf, the sound g.
It opens گفتن (goftan), "to say". It is ک (kâf), from the last lesson, with a second slash above the first, the last of Persian's four added letters you will write here.

Writing: گ.
1. start here: draw the independent stem downward.
2. continue left through the shallow bowl and final hook without lifting.
3. lift once, then draw the long slash down from the upper right toward the stem.
4. lift again, then draw the shorter floating slash above the first — and only now lift.
Pen lifts: 2.
Stroke order is one attested teaching order; hands and teachers vary. Source: Persian Online, How to Write Persian Characters, گ (gâf) demonstration at 02:24–02:28 (Liberal Arts Instructional Technology Services, Univ. of Texas at Austin).
