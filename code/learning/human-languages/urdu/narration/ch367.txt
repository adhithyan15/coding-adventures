Urdu, chapter 367: Formal Conditions.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
محض (mahz) — mere; merely.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for on no account, then the Urdu for to forbid.

You'll want to know: محض.
محض — mahz — "mere; merely".
اگر یہ محض غلط فہمی ہے تو کوئی بات نہیں۔ agar yih mahz ghalat-fahmī hai to koī bāt nahīṅ. If this is a mere misunderstanding, it does not matter.

The word, taken apart.
محض (mahz) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic maḥḍ, "pure, sheer".

You'll want to know: خلاف ورزی.
خلاف ورزی — khilāf-varzī — "a breach; a violation".
اگر قانون کی خلاف ورزی ہوئی تو جرمانہ ہو گا۔ agar qānūn kī khilāf-varzī huī to jurmānā ho gā. If the law is broken, there will be a fine.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
خلاف ورزی (khilāf-varzī) is built from خلاف, "against", usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic khilāf, and Persian varzī, "practising", from varzīdan. Acting against a rule.

Lesson 4 of 5.
شق (shiq) — a clause (in an agreement).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for a regulation, then the Urdu for a breach.

You'll want to know: شق.
شق — shiq — "a clause (in an agreement)".
اگر آپ اس شق سے متفق نہیں تو بتائیے۔ agar āp is shiq se muttafiq nahīṅ to batāiye. If you do not agree with this clause, say so.
Feminine.

The word, taken apart.
شق (shiq) is usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic shiqq, "a split; one of two halves"; Urdu uses it for a clause of a written agreement or a law, a sense our sources do not trace.

Past (it …): وہ قانون نافذ ہو گیا, voh qānūn nāfiz ho gayā.

The word, taken apart.
نافذ ہونا (nāfiz honā) is built from nāfiz, usually traced, through Persian, to Arabic nāfidh, "piercing; effective", and ہونا, "to be".
