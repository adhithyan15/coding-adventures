Marwadi (Marwari), chapter 1: Ram-Ram Sa: A Greeting the Hand Can Make.
7 lessons. The first lesson already needs your eyes or your hands, so save this one for when you have stopped.


Lesson 1 of 7.
र (ra) — one sign, and the beginning of a greeting.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — notice the sign until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
You do not need an alphabet first. Today your eye and hand need one sign.

Script — notice the sign.
र — ra.
Devanagari consonants carry a short a by default, so this is ra, not a bare r. The r is a quick tongue-tip tap.

Writing — guided copy.
Put a finger over र and follow the printed shape. Then copy it three times, large and slow. Say ra each time your pen stops.
This lesson asks you to copy the shape you can see. It does not invent one universal stroke order where school traditions differ.

Wrap-up recall.
Cover the model. Point to the one you copied most clearly and say its sound.
[question — say your answer, then pause 8 seconds]
What sound does र (ra) carry?


Lesson 2 of 7.
ा — make ra into rā.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — add one mark until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Read र. (ra.) Copy it once.

Script — add one mark.
The mark ा (ā) sits on the right of a consonant and lengthens its vowel:
र ra + ा ā = रा rā.
Hold ā steady; do not turn it into English ray.

Writing — guided copy.
copy र (ra).
add ा (ā) on its right.
read the result: रा rā.
Do that three times. On the last copy, wait five seconds after looking at the model before you write.

Wrap-up recall.
Which is ra: र or रा? Which is rā?
[question — say your answer, then pause 9 seconds]
What does ा (ā) do to the built-in vowel of a consonant?


Lesson 3 of 7.
राम (rām) — your first whole written word.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — add म until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Write रा after looking away from the model. Check it, then begin.

Script — add म.
म — ma.
Join it to the piece you know:
रा rā + म ma = राम rām.
At the end of this word, the built-in vowel of म is not pronounced in ordinary speech. Read the whole word rām. राम is the name Ram. The Marwadi greeting you are building will repeat this name. Today you only need the one word राम; the complete greeting still comes later.

Writing — from copy to memory.
copy म twice.
copy राम (rām) once while looking.
hide the model for ten seconds.
write राम (rām) from memory, then compare.
If the memory copy stalls, return only to the missing sign.

Wrap-up recall.
Read राम (rām) without using the romanization, say that it is the name Ram, then write it once more.
[question — say your answer, then pause 12 seconds]
Build and name the written word rām from its three visible pieces.


Lesson 4 of 7.
स (sa) — a second consonant.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — notice and copy until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read राम (rām), say what it means, then write it from memory. Check that your last sign is म.

Script — notice and copy.
स — sa.
Like र (ra), this consonant carries a short a. Do not confuse the two:
र ra, स sa.
Finger-trace स, then copy it three times. Say sa as each copy finishes.

Wrap-up recall.
Cover the large model and write स (sa) once from the copy you remember.
[question — say your answer, then pause 7 seconds]
Which sign is sa: र (ra) or स?


Lesson 5 of 7.
सा (sā) — reuse a mark instead of learning a new rule.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now — leave the section called Script — assemble until you have stopped, and I will say so again when we reach it.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
What did ा (ā) do to र (ra)? Apply the same change to स.

Script — assemble.
स sa + ा ā = सा sā.
The writing rule is old; only the combination is new. In the greeting you are building, सा (sā) is a respectful form of address.

Writing — delayed copy.
Copy सा once. Look away for ten seconds, say sā, and write it again. Then retrieve the two known pieces separately rather than using them as a phrase before the next lesson teaches the greeting:
राम (rām) सा (sā).

Wrap-up recall.
Read राम (rām), then सा (sā). Circle the copy of ा in each separate word.
[question — say your answer, then pause 10 seconds]
Write sā by adding the long-vowel mark to स (sa).


Lesson 6 of 7.
राम-राम सा (rām-rām sā) — your first complete Marwadi greeting.

Warm-up.
[pause 3 seconds]
Read राम (rām) and सा (sā) separately. You already own every sign in today's line.

The exchange — meet the greeting.
राम-राम सा (rām-rām sā)। — Rām-rām sā. — a respectful greeting.
Repeat राम (rām), then add respectful सा (sā). Rajasthan Sahitya Akademi's Jāgtī Jot prints this exchange:
A: राम-राम सा (rām-rām sā)। B: राम-राम सा (rām-rām sā), राम-राम।.
That attestation is why this track begins here rather than substituting a Hindi greeting merely because the scripts are shared.

Guided Practice — use and write it.
[your turn — hear: rām-rām sā]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — answer: राम-राम सा (rām-rām sā)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — read: राम-राम सा (rām-rām sā)]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: the line without romanization]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — copy: राम-राम सा (rām-rām sā) once, keeping the hyphen and space]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up recall.
Hide the line. Say the greeting once; then write as much as you can remember.

Lesson 7 of 7.
Practice — hear it, answer it, write it.

Warm-up.
[pause 5 seconds]
With the page covered, write राम (rām) and सा (sā) separately. Check only after both are down.

The exchange.
A: राम-राम सा (rām-rām sā)। B: राम-राम सा (rām-rām sā), राम-राम।.
Run both voices once. Then cover B and answer A aloud. Switch roles.

Guided Practice — four-skill checkpoint.
Listen: ask someone or a screen reader to say rām-rām sā without showing the text; identify it as a greeting.
Speak: give the greeting without reading.
Read: read राम-राम सा (rām-rām sā) without romanization.
Write: close the book for fifteen seconds, then write राम-राम सा (rām-rām sā) from memory. Check the repeated word, hyphen, space, and final सा.
Writing counts only if the model was hidden. Copying was the ramp; this is recall.

Wrap-up recall.
If one sign stalled, mark that sign and repeat only its short lesson tomorrow.
[question — say your answer, then pause 18 seconds]
You hear rām-rām sā. Write the respectful reply from memory.
