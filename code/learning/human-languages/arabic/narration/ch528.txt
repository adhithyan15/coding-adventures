Arabic, chapter 528: Single, engaged and married.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
خطيب (khaṭīb) — a fiancé.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to go bankrupt, then the Arabic for to put aside.

You'll want to know: خطيب.
خطيب — khaṭīb — "a fiancé".
زار خطيب أختي بيتنا أمس. zāra khaṭīb ukhtī baytanā ams. My sister's fiancé visited our house yesterday.
The same word also means a public speaker; خطيبة is a fiancée.

The word, taken apart.
خطيب (khaṭīb) is built on the native Arabic root kh-ṭ-b, "to address; to ask in marriage" (Lane). Lane gives the root both senses: speaking to people and asking for a woman's hand.

Lesson 2 of 5.
زواج (zawāj) — marriage.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to put aside, then the Arabic for a fiancé.

You'll want to know: زواج.
زواج — zawāj — "marriage".
احتفل جدي وجدتي بخمسين سنة من الزواج. iḥtafala jaddī wa-jaddatī bi-khamsīna sana min az-zawāj. My grandparents celebrated fifty years of marriage.

The word, taken apart.
زواج (zawāj) is built on the native Arabic root z-w-j, "a pair, one of a pair" (Lane). Zawj, a husband, and tazawwaja, to marry, which you know, are from the same root.

تزور حماتي بيتنا كل جمعة. tazūru ḥamātī baytanā kull jumʿa. My mother-in-law visits our house every Friday.

The word, taken apart.
حماة (ḥamāh) is built on the native Arabic root ḥ-m-w, "a husband's or wife's kin" (Lane). Lane gives ḥam as the husband's father and ḥamāh as his mother.

Lesson 4 of 5.
أعزب (aʿzab) — single, unmarried.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for marriage, then the Arabic for a mother-in-law.

You'll want to know: أعزب.
أعزب — aʿzab — "single, unmarried".
أخي الكبير ما زال أعزب. akhī al-kabīr mā zāla aʿzab. My older brother is still single.
عزباء is a single woman.

The word, taken apart.
أعزب (aʿzab) is built on the native Arabic root ʿ-z-b, "to be distant, remote" (Lane). Lane gives ʿazaba as to be far away: one far from marriage.
