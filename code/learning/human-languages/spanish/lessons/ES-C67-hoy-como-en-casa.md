# hoy como en casa — the pieces that move

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[ES-LEX-HOY, ES-GRAMMAR-AR-PRESENT-1SG] -->

[PAUSE 2s] *Como en casa.* — I eat at home. Now add *hoy*, and put it wherever
sounds right to you.

[PAUSE 1s] Whatever you chose was probably correct, and that is the lesson.

## Grammar Lens: three placements, one meaning
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-CIRCUMSTANTIAL-MOVES]; assesses=[ES-LEX-CASA] -->

> ***Hoy*** *como en casa.*
> *Como* ***hoy*** *en casa.*
> *Como en casa* ***hoy***.

[PAUSE 2s] All three are Spanish, and all three mean the same thing. The pieces
that say **when** and **where** are not pinned to a position the way the subject
and the verb are.

[PAUSE 2s] You can stack them, too: *Hoy como en casa con María.* When, where,
with whom — and the order among them is yours.

## Why it's said this way
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[ES-GRAMMAR-WORD-ORDER-EMPHASIS]; assesses=[ES-GRAMMAR-SUBJECT-VERB-AGREEMENT-RULE, ES-GRAMMAR-PERSONAL-A] -->

[PAUSE 2s] The freedom is not free, and it is not decoration. **The front of the
sentence is the emphatic position.** Putting *hoy* first says *today*, as
against some other day. Leaving it at the end mentions it and moves on.

[PAUSE 2s] So the choice is not stylistic. It is how a Spanish speaker does what
an English speaker does by stressing a word out loud.

[PAUSE 2s] And notice why this is possible at all. The verb ending already says
who is acting, and the personal *a* already marks who is on the receiving end —
so nothing depends on position to keep the roles straight. Latin's endings paid
for word order when they fell; the pieces Spanish kept are what let the rest
move.
