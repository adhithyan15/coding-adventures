Spanish, chapter 580: El porqué.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
el motivo — the reason, the motive.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for to admit, then the Spanish for absurd.

You'll want to know: el motivo.
el motivo — "the reason, the motive".
¿Cuál es el motivo del viaje? What is the reason for the trip?
Por este motivo — for this reason. Close to la razón.

The word, taken apart.
motivo is a learned borrowing from Late Latin motivus 'moving', built on movere 'to move': a motive is what moves you (EtymDB).

Lesson 2 of 5.
a raíz de — as a result of, following.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for absurd, then the Spanish for the reason.

You'll want to know: a raíz de.
a raíz de — "as a result of, following".
A raíz del accidente, cambiaron las normas. Following the accident, they changed the rules.
Followed by a noun; it names the event that started something.

The word, taken apart.
a raíz de is built on raíz 'root', inherited from Latin radicem, a form of radix 'root' (EtymDB): something grows from the event.

Lesson 3 of 5.
ya que — since, as.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for the reason, then the Spanish for as a result of.

You'll want to know: ya que.
ya que — "since, as".
Ya que estás aquí, ayúdame. Since you are here, help me.
Gives a reason the listener already knows; it often goes first in the sentence.

The word, taken apart.
ya que is built on ya, inherited from Latin iam 'already', and que, from Latin quid (EtymDB).

Lesson 4 of 5.
puesto que — seeing as; since.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for as a result of, then the Spanish for since.

You'll want to know: puesto que.
puesto que — "seeing as; since".
Puesto que llueve, no salimos. Seeing as it is raining, we are not going out.
More formal than porque; common in writing.

The word, taken apart.
puesto que is built on puesto, the participle of poner, which is inherited from Latin ponere 'to put' (EtymDB): literally 'it being put that'.

Lesson 5 of 5.
dado que — given that.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Spanish for since, then the Spanish for seeing as.

You'll want to know: dado que.
dado que — "given that".
Dado que no hay dinero, esperamos. Given that there is no money, we wait.
Formal, like puesto que; it is followed by the indicative.

The word, taken apart.
dado que is built on dado, inherited from Latin datus 'given', the participle of dare 'to give' (EtymDB).
