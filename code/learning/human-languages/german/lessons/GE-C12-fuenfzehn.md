# fünfzehn — "fifteen"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-LEX-VIERZEHN-01, GE-SOUND-FUENF-UE-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Third run of the rule, and the first place English is the messier of
the two languages.

## You'll want to know: fünfzehn
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-LEX-FUENFZEHN-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-FUENF-01, GE-GRAMMAR-ZIFFER-PLUS-ZEHN-01] -->

> **fünfzehn** — "fifteen." *fünf* + *zehn*.

The digit is whole. English is not: **five** became *fif-*, so *fifteen* no
longer shows its own number. German keeps *fünf* visible and audible.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-SOUND-FUENF-UE-01, GE-LEX-FUENFZEHN-01] -->

- `umlaut-ue` — the *ü* from *fünf*, unchanged: say *ee*, then round the lips.
- `z-ts` — and *-TSAYN*.

Two syllables, the weight on *fünf* and the ending unstressed.
