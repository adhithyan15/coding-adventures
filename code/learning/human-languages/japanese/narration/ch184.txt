Japanese, chapter 184: Goods, Queues and Exchanges.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
しなもの (shinamono) — goods; article, item.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to choose, then the Japanese for to line up.

You'll want to know: しなもの.
しなもの — shinamono — "goods; article, item".
この みせの しなものは やすいです。 — kono mise no shinamono wa yasui desu — "The goods in this shop are cheap."

The word, taken apart.
しなもの (shinamono) is native Japanese, built from shina, "article, goods", and mono, "thing", both inherited rather than borrowed (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 2 of 5.
れつ (retsu) — queue; line, row.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to line up, then the Japanese for goods.

You'll want to know: れつ.
れつ — retsu — "queue; line, row".
みせの まえに ながい れつが あります。 — mise no mae ni nagai retsu ga arimasu — "There is a long queue in front of the shop."

The word, taken apart.
れつ (retsu) is Sino-Japanese (kango): one Chinese character, "row, file", read with its on'yomi, a reading borrowed from Chinese (Middle Chinese ljet) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).

Lesson 3 of 5.
とりかえる (torikaeru) — to exchange; to swap, to replace.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for goods, then the Japanese for queue.

You'll want to know: とりかえる.
とりかえる — torikaeru — "to exchange; to swap, to replace".
てんいんが くつを とりかえます。 — ten'in ga kutsu o torikaemasu — "The shop assistant exchanges the shoes."
Polite とりかえます, torikaemasu; an ichidan verb.

The word, taken apart.
とりかえる (torikaeru) is native Japanese, built from tori, the stem of toru, "to take", and kaeru, "to exchange", both inherited rather than borrowed (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 4 of 5.
ぜんぶ (zenbu) — all of it; everything, in all.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for queue, then the Japanese for to exchange.

You'll want to know: ぜんぶ.
ぜんぶ — zenbu — "all of it; everything, in all".
ぜんぶで せんえんです。 — zenbu de sen'en desu — "It comes to a thousand yen in all."

The word, taken apart.
ぜんぶ (zenbu) is Sino-Japanese (kango): two Chinese characters read with their on'yomi, readings borrowed from Chinese. They are zen, "whole" (Middle Chinese dzjwen) and bu, "part, section" (buX) (KANJIDIC2; Middle Chinese in Baxter's transcription).
