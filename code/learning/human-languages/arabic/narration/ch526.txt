Arabic, chapter 526: Trades and crafts.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
ميكانيكي (mīkānīkī) — a mechanic.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for to program, then the Arabic for a programmer.

You'll want to know: ميكانيكي.
ميكانيكي — mīkānīkī — "a mechanic".
أصلح الميكانيكي سيارة أبي. aṣlaḥa al-mīkānīkī sayyārat abī. The mechanic repaired my father's car.

The word, taken apart.
ميكانيكي (mīkānīkī) is a modern loan, usually traced to French mécanique, from Greek mēkhanē, "a device", with the Arabic ending -ī.

Lesson 2 of 5.
مقاول (muqāwil) — a building contractor.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Arabic for a programmer, then the Arabic for a mechanic.

You'll want to know: مقاول.
مقاول — muqāwil — "a building contractor".
بنى المقاول بيتنا الجديد. banā al-muqāwil baytanā al-jadīd. The contractor built our new house.

The word, taken apart.
مقاول (muqāwil) is usually traced to the native Arabic root q-w-l, "to say", the root of qāla: Form III qāwala is to talk terms with someone, and the muqāwil is the one who takes on the work by agreement.

دهان (dahhān) is built on the native Arabic root d-h-n, "oil, grease; to anoint" (Lane). Dahana, to paint a wall, which you know, is from the same root.

أصلح الإسكافي حذاء أبي. aṣlaḥa al-iskāfī ḥidhāʾ abī. The cobbler repaired my father's shoe.

The word, taken apart.
إسكافي (iskāfī) is an old Arabic word for a shoemaker or cobbler. Lane gives iskāf, a maker of boots or shoes, under s-k-f; the origin is not settled.
