Portuguese, chapter 333: Um bolo caseiro.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
a batedeira — the electric mixer.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to grill, then the Portuguese for to boil.

You'll want to know: a batedeira.
a batedeira — "the electric mixer".
A minha mãe bate as claras na batedeira. My mother beats the egg whites in the mixer.
Built on bater, ‘to beat’.

The word, taken apart.
batedeira is built on bater "to beat", which comes from Latin battuere "to beat" (Lewis & Short), with the ending -deira for a tool.

What you've built.
One more word for telling, explaining and describing at B1.

O padeiro amassa o pão de madrugada. The baker kneads the bread before dawn.
Also to dent a car: amassar o carro.

The word, taken apart.
amassar is built from a- and massa "dough", from Latin massa "a lump, a mass" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 4 of 5.
untar — to grease (a tin), to rub with fat.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Portuguese for to sift, then the Portuguese for to knead.

You'll want to know: untar.
untar — "to grease (a tin), to rub with fat".
Antes de pôr a massa, ela unta a forma com manteiga. Before putting the mixture in, she greases the tin with butter.
Untar a forma — to grease the tin.

The word, taken apart.
untar comes down from Old Portuguese (EtymDB) and goes back to Latin ūnctus "anointed", from unguere "to smear with oil" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.

You'll want to know: polvilhar.
polvilhar — "to sprinkle, to dust".
No fim, ele polvilha o bolo com açúcar. At the end he dusts the cake with sugar.
Of a powder: sugar, flour, cinnamon.

The word, taken apart.
polvilhar is built on polvilho "fine powder", a word built on pó "dust", from Latin pulvis "dust" (Lewis & Short).
