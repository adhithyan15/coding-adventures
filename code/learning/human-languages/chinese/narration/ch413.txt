Lesson 1 of 7.
dēngjī — to board a plane.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for to come in to shore, then the Mandarin for a passenger ship.

You'll want to know: dēngjī.
dēngjī — "to board a plane".
dēngjī yǐqián wǒmen zuò zài zhèr děng — Until we board, we'll sit here and wait.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
dēngjī is built from dēng 'to go up' and 机 'a plane', both inherited from Middle Chinese, tong and kj+j (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'to rise' and, after an older dictionary, 'to get up into a carriage'.

Lesson 2 of 7.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for a passenger ship, then the Mandarin for to board a plane.

You'll want to know: 飞.
飞 — fēi — "to fly".
飞机飞得很高 fēijī fēi de hěn gāo — The plane is flying very high.
The new character, in pieces: 飞 (fēi) — three strokes: a bar that turns and slants down to the right, ending in a hook up; then a short stroke falling to the left and a dot, near the top on the right. The next lesson puts it in your hand.

The word, taken apart.
飞 (fēi) is inherited from Middle Chinese pj+j (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), glossed 'to fly, to soar'.

Script — a hooked turn, then two small strokes.
飞 (fēi).
One piece, three strokes.
First, a bar to the right that turns and slants down to the right, then flicks up at the end: one stroke, without lifting the pen.
Then a short stroke falling to the left, on the right of the turn.
Last, a dot under it, falling to the right.
One long stroke and two short ones: three.

Guided Practice.

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
How many strokes in 飞 (fēi)? (Three.) Which stroke is the longest? (The first, the bar that turns and hooks.)

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: jiàngluò.
jiàngluò — "to land; to come down onto the runway".
fēijī jiàngluò yǐhòu wǒmen zuò dìtiě qù bīnguǎn — Once the plane has landed, we'll take the metro to the hotel.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
jiàngluò is built from jiàng 'to come down' and luò 'to fall', both inherited from Middle Chinese, kaewngH and lak (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'down, to come down' and the second as leaves falling from a tree.

Lesson 6 of 7.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]

You'll want to know: 飞快.
飞快 — fēikuài — "very fast; as fast as flying".
时间过得飞快 shíjiān guò de fēikuài — Time flies.

The word, taken apart.
飞快 (fēikuài) is built from 飞 (fēi) 'to fly' and 快 'fast', both inherited from Middle Chinese, pj+j and khwaejH (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'to one's liking'; the sense 'fast' is not given there.
