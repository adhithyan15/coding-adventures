Latin, chapter 417: Property.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
possessiō, possessiōnis — possession; (plural) estates.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to restrain, then the Latin for to claim.

You'll want to know: possessiō, possessiōnis.
possessiō, possessiōnis — "possession; (plural) estates".
Possessiōnēs magnās in agrīs habēbat. He had large estates in the country.
possessiō, possessiōnis f., the noun of possideō. In the plural, landed estates. English possession.

The word, taken apart.
possessiō is the noun built on possideō "to possess" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English possession comes from it.

Lesson 2 of 5.
proprius, propria, proprium — one's own; peculiar (to).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to claim, then the Latin for possession.

You'll want to know: proprius, propria, proprium.
proprius, propria, proprium — "one's own; peculiar (to)".
Domum propriam nōn habet. He has no house of his own.
proprius, propria, proprium + genitive or dative: belonging to one alone. English proper and property.

The word, taken apart.
The origin of proprius is not settled: EtymDB traces it to Proto-Italic prijos, but Lewis & Short call it doubtful and suggest, with a "perhaps", the root of prae "before". English proper and property come from it.

Warm-up.

aliēnus, aliēna, aliēnum — "belonging to another; foreign; unfavourable".
Rem aliēnam cupere nōn dēbēmus. We should not desire what belongs to another.
aliēnus, aliēna, aliēnum, built on alius, other. The opposite of suus and proprius. English alien.

The word, taken apart.
aliēnus is built on alius "other" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short). English alien comes from it through French.

Lesson 4 of 5.
dominium, dominiī — ownership.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for one's own, then the Latin for belonging to another.

You'll want to know: dominium, dominiī.
dominium, dominiī — "ownership".
Dominium agrī ad fīlium pervēnit. The ownership of the field passed to the son.
dominium, dominiī n., built on dominus, master. In Roman law, full ownership. English dominion.

The word, taken apart.
dominium is built on dominus "a master" (EtymDB), which comes from domus "a house" (Lewis & Short): the right of the master of the house. English dominion is of the same family.

Lesson 5 of 5.
mancipium, mancipiī — formal purchase; a slave.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for belonging to another, then the Latin for ownership.

You'll want to know: mancipium, mancipiī.
mancipium, mancipiī — "formal purchase; a slave".
Servum mancipiō accēpit. He received the slave by formal purchase.
mancipium, mancipiī n., manus + capiō: taking in the hand, the old ceremony of purchase with scales and bronze; then the thing bought, a slave. English emancipate.

The word, taken apart.
mancipium is built on manceps "a purchaser" (EtymDB; Lewis & Short), from manus "hand" and capiō "to take". English emancipate comes from its family.
