Sanskrit, chapter 68: How It Feels.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
दुःखम् (duḥkham) — sorrow, pain.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for rest, then the Sanskrit for ease.

You'll want to know: दुःखम्.
दुःखम् (duḥkham) — "sorrow", the opposite of सुखम्. The two words share their ending.

The word, taken apart.
CDIAL 6375 says duḥkha was formed after sukha, with duḥ- "bad" in place of su- "good"; MW also suggests a Prakrit form of duḥ-stha. It lives on as Hindi dukh.

What you've built.
The other side of ease.

[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

What does दुःखम् (duḥkham) mean? ("Sorrow, pain".) Say it once more.


Lesson 2 of 5.
भयम् (bhayam) — fear.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for ease, then the Sanskrit for sorrow.

You'll want to know: भयम्.
भयम् (bhayam) — "fear". मम भयम् अस्ति — "I am afraid".

The word, taken apart.
Bhaya is built on the root √bhī, "to fear" (MW, CDIAL 9390). It is inherited: MW compares Lithuanian bijotis and Slavic bojati, "to fear".

What you've built.
What the dark brings.

Lesson 4 of 5.

You'll want to know: लज्जा.
लज्जा (lajjā) — "shyness", the feeling that turns the face away.

Lajjā is built on the root √lajj, "to be ashamed, blush" (MW). It lives on as Hindi lāj, "shame" (CDIAL 10910).

Lesson 5 of 5.
हर्षः (harṣaḥ) — joy.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Sanskrit for anger, then the Sanskrit for shyness.

You'll want to know: हर्षः.
हर्षः (harṣaḥ) — "joy", bright and sudden, where आनन्दः is deep and lasting.

The word, taken apart.
Harṣa is built on the root √hṛṣ, "to bristle, thrill": joy is the hair standing on end (MW, CDIAL 13999). MW compares Latin horreō, "I bristle", the source of English horror.

What you've built.
The bright feeling.
