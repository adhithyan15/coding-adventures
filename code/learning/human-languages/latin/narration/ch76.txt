Lesson 1 of 5.
lavō, lavāre — to wash.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to laugh, then the Latin for to weep.

You'll want to know: lavō, lavāre.
lavō, lavāre — "to wash". English lavatory.

The word, taken apart.
lavō is inherited, usually traced to Proto-Indo-European lewh3-, "to wash" (EtymDB 2.1); Greek louō, "to wash", is a cousin (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 2 of 5.
coquō, coquere — to cook.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to weep, then the Latin for to wash.

You'll want to know: coquō, coquere.
coquō, coquere — "to cook". What a coquus does.

The word, taken apart.
coquō is inherited from Proto-Italic kwekwō, "I cook", usually traced to Proto-Indo-European pekw-, "to cook, to ripen" (EtymDB 2.1); the first p changed to match the second sound. Greek peptō, "to cook, to digest", is a cousin (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 3 of 5.
vendō, vendere — to sell.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to wash, then the Latin for to cook.

You'll want to know: vendō, vendere.
vendō, vendere — "to sell". English vendor.

The word, taken apart.
vendō is a contraction of vēnum dō, "I put up for sale", from vēnum, "sale", and dō, "I give" (Lewis & Short).

Lesson 5 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for to sell, then the Latin for to swim.

You'll want to know: vocō, vocāre.
vocō, vocāre — "to call". English vocal.

The word, taken apart.
vocō belongs with vōx, "voice", and the pair is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European wekw-, "to speak" (EtymDB 2.1). English invoke comes from this family.
