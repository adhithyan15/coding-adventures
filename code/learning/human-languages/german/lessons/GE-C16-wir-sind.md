# wir sind — "we are"

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-ER-IST-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Three singular forms down. The plural starts here, and after
*bin*, *bist*, *ist*, this one will finally look like the verb it belongs to.

## You'll want to know: wir sind
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-WIR-SIND-01]; assesses=[GE-LEX-SEIN-01] -->

> **wir sind** — "we are."

**wir** is "we." And **sind** shares its first two letters with the infinitive
*sein*, which is the first visible family resemblance in this verb.

Its Latin twin is *sunt*, "they are" — same **s**, same **n**, same closing
stop, the same root wearing a different vowel.

## Sounds you'll need
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[GE-SOUND-FINAL-DEVOICING-D-01, GE-GRAMMAR-SEIN-WIR-SIND-01] -->

- `w-as-v` — German **w** is English **v**. *Wir* begins like *veer*.
- `ie-long-ee` — **ie** is a long **ee**. *Wir* is *veer*, not *wire*.
- `d-t-final` — the **d** at the end of *sind* is spoken as a **t**, the same
  final devoicing you met on *und*. Say *zint*.
