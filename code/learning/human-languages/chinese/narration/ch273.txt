Mandarin Chinese, chapter 273: Honest and Careful.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
chéngshí — honest.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for an uncle, then the Mandarin for an auntie.

You'll want to know: chéngshí.
chéngshí — "honest".
tā shì yí ge chéngshí de rén — He's an honest person.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
chéngshí is built from chéng and shí, both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzyeng and zyit (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'trustworthy' and the second 'full' and 'sincere'.

What you've built.
One more word for describing things.

Lesson 2 of 5.
好客 (hàokè) — hospitable.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for an auntie, then the Mandarin for honest.

You'll want to know: 好客.
好客 — hàokè — "hospitable".
tāmen jiā fēicháng hàokè — Their family is very hospitable.

The word, taken apart.
好客 (hàokè) is built from 好, here said with a falling tone, 'to be fond of', and 客 'a guest', both inherited from Middle Chinese, xawH and khaek (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the second 'a guest'.

Lesson 4 of 5.
cūxīn — careless.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Mandarin for hospitable, then the Mandarin for putting your heart into it.

You'll want to know: cūxīn.
cūxīn — "careless".
nǐ tài cūxīn le — You're so careless.
Its characters come later; for now it is a word for the ear.

The word, taken apart.
cūxīn is built from cū 'coarse' and 心 'the heart', both inherited from Middle Chinese, dzuX and sim (Guangyun, in Baxter's transcription), which glosses the first 'rough'. Middle Chinese dzuX would regularly give zù; the initial and the level tone of cū are irregular.
