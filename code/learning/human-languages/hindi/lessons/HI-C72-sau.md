# सौ — the round figure

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C72-COST-02, HI-LEX-C72-COST-05] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say the word for a rupee, and the word for buying. You can ask what
a thing costs — but you cannot yet say the number the answer usually is.

## You'll want to know: सौ
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[HI-LEX-C72-HUNDRED-01]; assesses=[] -->

**सौ** (*sau*) — a hundred.

## Grammar Lens: two letters, and the number a price lands on
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[HI-LEX-C72-HUNDRED-01, HI-LEX-C72-COST-02] -->

| said | meaning |
|---|---|
| सौ | a hundred |
| सौ रुपये | a hundred rupees |

Number first, then the thing. That is the whole construction, and both halves
are now yours.

**रुपये** rather than **रुपया**, because more than one is being counted — the
same bend the track has been making since the adjectives, costing nothing new
here.

It is two letters, the shortest number word you will meet, and it does more work
than any of the bigger ones: a price, an age, a distance, and the round figure
anybody reaches for when they mean *a great many*.

Your counting still stops at **बीस**, so the numbers between twenty and this one
are still ahead of you. **सौ** arrives early because a price cannot wait for
them.
