Latin, chapter 133: Spider, Frog, Hare, Fox, Bull.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
arānea, arāneae — a spider.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for an ant, then the Latin for a butterfly.

You'll want to know: arānea, arāneae.
arānea, arāneae — "a spider".

The word, taken apart.
arānea is closely related to Greek arakhnē, "spider" (Lewis & Short); whether Latin borrowed it or both took it from a third language is uncertain.

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a spider, then the Latin for a frog.

You'll want to know: lepus, leporis.
lepus, leporis — "a hare".

The word, taken apart.
lepus has no sure origin; Varro already compared a Sicilian Greek leporis, "hare" (Lewis & Short), and it may be a loan from a language of the Mediterranean.

Lesson 4 of 5.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Latin for a frog, then the Latin for a hare.

You'll want to know: vulpēs, vulpis.
vulpēs, vulpis — "a fox".

The word, taken apart.
vulpēs is inherited from Proto-Italic wolpi-, "fox" (EtymDB 2.1); Greek alōpēx, "fox", is often compared (Lewis & Short), but the details are uncertain.

taurus is usually traced to Proto-Indo-European tauros, "bull" (EtymDB 2.1); Greek tauros, "bull", is a cousin (Lewis & Short).
