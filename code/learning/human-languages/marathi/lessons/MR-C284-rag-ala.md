# मला राग आला (malā rāg ālā) — feelings that come to you

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[MR-LEX-C284-RAG-01, MR-LEX-C284-BHITI-01, MR-PHRASE-BHETUN-ANAND-JHALA] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Say "I got very angry" and "I am afraid of snakes". (*malā khūp rāg ālā*, *malā sāpācī bhītī vāṭte*.) Then the first line you learned: *bheṭūn ānand jhālā*.

## You'll want to know: the feeling is the subject
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-GRAMMAR-C284-FEELING-DATIVE-01]; assesses=[MR-GRAMMAR-DATIVE-FEELING, MR-GRAMMAR-DATIVE-LIKING, MR-LEX-C170-AAROGYA170-03, MR-LEX-ANAND] -->

> **मला राग आला.**
>
> *malā rāg ālā.* — "I got angry."

The one who feels stands in the dative, as with **आवडणे** and **वाटणे**. The feeling is the subject, and the past agrees with it: **राग आला** (masculine), **भीती वाटली** (feminine), **कंटाळा आला** (*kaṇṭāḷā ālā*), "I got bored". Ask it the same way: **तुम्हाला राग आला का?** (*tumhālā rāg ālā kā?*)

Two feeling words you know are adjectives instead, and they agree with the person: **ती आनंदी आहे** (*tī ānandī āhe*), "she is happy"; **तो दुःखी होता** (*to duḥkhī hotā*), "he was sad".

## You'll want to know: which verb each feeling takes
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[MR-GRAMMAR-C284-FEELING-VERBS-01]; assesses=[MR-LEX-C170-AAROGYA170-01, MR-LEX-ANCHOR-DUKH, MR-LEX-C73-HEALTH73-05, MR-LEX-C74-HEALTH74-01] -->

Three verbs carry feelings, and each feeling has its own:

- **आला**, "came": **राग आला**, **कंटाळा आला**
- **वाटलं**, "was felt": **भीती वाटली**, **लाज वाटली** (*lāj vāṭlī*), "I felt ashamed"
- **झालं**, "came about": **आनंद झाला**, **दुःख झालं** (*duḥkh jhālaṁ*), "I was sad"

Learn the feeling with its verb, as you learned **भेटून आनंद झाला** whole.
