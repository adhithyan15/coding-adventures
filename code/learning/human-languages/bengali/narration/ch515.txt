Lesson 1 of 6.
পরিবেশন (pôribeshôn) — serving food.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for to steam, then the Bengali for to sauté.

You'll want to know: পরিবেশন.
পরিবেশন — pôribeshôn — "serving food".
বিয়েতে ওয়েটাররা খাবার পরিবেশন করল। Biyete oyeṭārrā khābār pôribeshôn kôrlô. At the wedding the waiters served the food.

The word, taken apart.
পরিবেশন (pôribeshôn) is a tatsama, Sanskrit pariveṣaṇa, "waiting at table, serving up a meal" (Monier-Williams).

Lesson 4 of 6.
এঁটো (ẽṭo) — leftover from a meal; touched by eating.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a mouthful, then the Bengali for a flavour.

You'll want to know: এঁটো.
এঁটো — ẽṭo — "leftover from a meal; touched by eating".
খাওয়ার পরে আমি এঁটো থালাগুলো বেসিনে রাখলাম। Khāoyār pôre āmi ẽṭo thālāgulo besine rākhlām. After the meal I put the used plates in the basin.

The word, taken apart.
এঁটো (ẽṭo) comes from a reconstructed ācaṣṭa, "tasted"; CDIAL lists Bengali ẽṭo, "the leavings of a meal" (CDIAL 1067). Food, or a plate, that someone has already eaten from is এঁটো.

Lesson 5 of 6.
আঁচানো (ā̃chāno) — to rinse after a meal (the mouth and hands).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Bengali for a flavour, then the Bengali for leftover from a meal.

You'll want to know: আঁচানো.
আঁচানো — ā̃chāno — "to rinse after a meal (the mouth and hands)".
খাওয়ার পরে সবাই উঠে আঁচাল। Khāoyār pôre sôbāi uṭhe ā̃chālô. After the meal everyone got up and rinsed their mouths and hands.
Past (he/she …): তিনি খাওয়ার পরে আঁচালেন, tini khāoyār pôre ā̃chālen.
Rice is often eaten by hand, so mouth and hand are rinsed when the meal is over.

The word, taken apart.
আঁচানো (ā̃chāno) comes down from Sanskrit ācāmati, taking a sip of water from the hollow of the hand (CDIAL 1069, which lists Bengali ā̃cāna). The everyday sense, rinsing mouth and hand at the end of a meal, probably grew out of that sip.
