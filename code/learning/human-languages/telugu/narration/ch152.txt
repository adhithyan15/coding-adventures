ఝరి (jhari) — a stream.

Picture a narrow stream moving down a hillside. This lesson gives that stream a name before it asks you to study the uncommon first letter.

You'll want to know: ఝరి.
ఝరి — jhari — "a stream" or "mountain stream".
Say it in two gentle pieces: jha, ri. Say the word as ఝ-రి. The first sound is a breathy jh; the second piece is the familiar రి.
ఝరి (jhari) is an authentic learned or literary word shared with Sanskrit. It is not an everyday word you must force into conversation. Keep its concrete image today: a stream running down a slope.

What you've built.
Meaning comes first. You can recognise and say the whole word before the next lesson asks your hand to take its first shape apart.

Lesson 2 of 3.

Before we start: this one needs your hands, so it is not a driving lesson. You will want a pen and something to write on and your eyes, for letter shapes on the page. You can listen to everything else now. I will say so again when we reach them, but leave these sections until you have stopped: Script you'll notice: ఝ and Guided Practice.

Script you'll notice: ఝ.
[once you have stopped driving — this part needs your eyes or your hands; if you are driving, skip ahead to the next part]
ఝ — jha.
In ఝరి (jhari), the first base letter is ఝ. The rest, రి, is already familiar. Read the whole word in two pieces: ఝ-రి.
This aspirated letter is uncommon in everyday Telugu vocabulary. That is why the book gave you the authentic word ఝరి (jhari) before asking you to practise the shape.

Writing: ఝ — five movements, five runs.
Follow the filmstrip slowly. Movement 1 circles the broad left bowl. Lift. Movement 2 circles the middle bowl. Lift again. Movement 3 circles the right bowl. Movement 4 sweeps through the separate upper flourish. Movement 5 draws the separate stem downward below the middle.
The numbered route is one attested school-style order; Telugu handwriting can vary. Keep all five pen-down runs distinct and let the three bowls stay round.
