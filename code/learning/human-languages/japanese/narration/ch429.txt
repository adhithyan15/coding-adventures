Japanese, chapter 429: Feeling Down.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
がっかりする (gakkari suru) — to be disappointed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to frolic, then the Japanese for to be full of energy.

You'll want to know: がっかりする.
がっかりする — gakkari suru — "to be disappointed".
しあいに まけて、がっかりしました。 — shiai ni makete, gakkari shimashita — "We lost the match, and I was disappointed."
Polite がっかりします, gakkarishimasu; a する verb. Past (polite): せんしゅが がっかりしました。, senshu ga gakkari shimashita.

The word, taken apart.
がっかりする (gakkari suru) is a native Japanese mimetic word, gakkari, inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese, made a verb with suru, "to do"; its older origin is not settled (JMdict).

Lesson 2 of 5.
おちこむ (ochikomu) — to feel down; to be depressed.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Japanese for to be full of energy, then the Japanese for to be disappointed.

You'll want to know: おちこむ.
おちこむ — ochikomu — "to feel down; to be depressed".
しけんの あと、あには おちこんでいました。 — shiken no ato, ani wa ochikonde imashita — "After the exam my elder brother was feeling down."
Polite おちこみます, ochikomimasu; a godan verb. Past (polite): あにが おちこみました。, ani ga ochikomimashita.

The word, taken apart.
おちこむ (ochikomu) is a native Japanese compound (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese: ochi, "falling (ochiru)" and komu, "going in" (JMdict, KANJIDIC2).

Lesson 3 of 5.

Warm-up.

Before the new one: say the Japanese for to be disappointed, then the Japanese for to feel down.

You'll want to know: ためいき.
ためいき — tameiki — "a sigh".
ちちは ながい ためいきを つきました。 — chichi wa nagai tameiki o tsukimashita — "My father let out a long sigh."
tameiki o tsuku is "to let out a sigh": this tsuku goes with tameiki and is not tsuku, "to arrive".

The word, taken apart.
ためいき (tameiki) is a native Japanese word (wago), inherited rather than borrowed from Chinese; its kanji spelling was attached to it later (JMdict).
