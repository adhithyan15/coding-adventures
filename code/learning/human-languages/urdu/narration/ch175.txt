Urdu, chapter 175: Rasmalai, Falooda, Kulfi, Quail, Dove.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
رس ملائی (ras malāī) — rasmalai.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for kidney beans, then the Urdu for a fig.

You'll want to know: رس ملائی.
رس ملائی — ras malāī — "rasmalai".

The phrase, taken apart.
رس ملائی (ras malāī) is "cream in syrup". ras, "juice, syrup", comes from Sanskrit rasa, "juice" (CDIAL 10650). The origin of malāī, "cream", is not settled; CDIAL has no entry for it.

Lesson 3 of 5.
قلفی (qulfī) — kulfi.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for rasmalai, then the Urdu for falooda.

You'll want to know: قلفی.
قلفی — qulfī — "kulfi".

The word, taken apart.
قلفی (qulfī) is usually linked with qulf, "a lock", the Urdu and Persian form of Arabic qufl (Lane, root q-f-l): the sweet is frozen in a sealed mould.

Lesson 4 of 5.
بٹیر (baṭer) — a quail.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Urdu for falooda, then the Urdu for kulfi.

You'll want to know: بٹیر.
بٹیر — baṭer — "a quail".

The word, taken apart.
بٹیر (baṭer) is a tadbhava, a word inherited and reshaped by sound change. It comes from vartakara, "making turns (of the quail)", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 11350). CDIAL suggests, with a query, that it is a reshaping of Sanskrit vartikā, "quail".
