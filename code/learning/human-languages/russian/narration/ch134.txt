Russian, chapter 134: To tell, To go for a walk, To tidy up, To find, To look for.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
рассказывать (rasskázyvat) — to tell (a story).

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to rent; to take off, then the Russian for to repair.

You'll want to know: рассказывать.
рассказывать (rasskázyvat) — "to tell (a story)".

The word, taken apart.
рассказывать (rasskázyvat) is рас-, "out, through", with -сказывать, from сказать, "to say", which Vasmer derives from казать, "to show": telling is showing in words.

Lesson 2 of 5.
гулять (gulyát) — to go for a walk.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to repair, then the Russian for to tell.

You'll want to know: гулять.
гулять (gulyát) — "to go for a walk".

The word, taken apart.
гулять (gulyát) — "a difficult word", says Vasmer. Berneker and Brückner tied it to гул, "a hum", Potebnya and Sobolevsky to a first sense "to drink"; each is disputed, and Trubachev's note traces the sense from "to play ball".

Lesson 4 of 5.
находить (nakhodít) — to find.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to go for a walk, then the Russian for to tidy up.

You'll want to know: находить.
находить (nakhodít) — "to find".

The word, taken apart.
находить (nakhodít) is на-, "onto", with ходить, "to walk": to find is to come upon a thing. Vasmer ties ход to the Indo-European root sed-, "to sit", its х grown from an s after prefixes.

Lesson 5 of 5.
искать (iskát) — to look for.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Russian for to tidy up, then the Russian for to find.

You'll want to know: искать.
искать (iskát) — "to look for".

The word, taken apart.
искать (iskát) from Proto-Slavic iskati — inherited. Vasmer relates it to Lithuanian ieškóti, "to seek", Sanskrit iccháti, "seeks, wishes", and Old English āscian, so English ask is a cousin.
