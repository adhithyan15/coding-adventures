Gujarati, chapter 46: Head to Foot.
5 lessons. All 5 can be done entirely by ear.


Lesson 1 of 5.
માથું (māthũ) — the head.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for nine, then the Gujarati for ten.

You'll want to know: માથું.
માથું (māthũ) — "the head". The top of the body.

The word, taken apart.
માથું (māthũ) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from mastaka, "head, skull" (CDIAL 9926).

What you've built.
One more word for head to foot.

Guided Practice.

[your turn — say: māthũ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: māthũ, once more]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — say: say māthũ]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]
[your turn — recall: say the Gujarati for nine, then the Gujarati for ten, then say māthũ again]
[pause 8 seconds for the answer]

Wrap-up Recall.
[pause 3 seconds]
What does માથું (māthũ) mean? ("The head".) Say it once more.


Lesson 2 of 5.
પગ (pag) — a leg, a foot.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for ten, then the Gujarati for the head.

You'll want to know: પગ.
પગ (pag) — "a leg, a foot". One word for both.

The word, taken apart.
પગ (pag) is a tadbhava, a word inherited from Sanskrit and reshaped by sound change. It comes from padga, "afoot; footsoldier" (CDIAL 7766). It is pad, "foot", and ga, "going".

Lesson 3 of 5.
પેટ (peṭ) — the belly.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Gujarati for the head, then the Gujarati for a leg.

You'll want to know: પેટ.
પેટ (peṭ) — "the belly".

The word, taken apart.
પેટ (peṭ) is a tadbhava. It comes from peṭṭa, "belly", a form CDIAL reconstructs from its descendants (CDIAL 8376). CDIAL notes that the words for "basket" show the same range of forms and may be the same word.
