Lesson 1 of 7.
la frusta — the whisk; the whip.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to stuff, then the Italian for to garnish.

You'll want to know: la frusta.
la frusta — "the whisk; the whip".
Prendi la frusta, non il cucchiaio. Take the whisk, not the spoon.
La frusta elettrica — the electric whisk.

The word, taken apart.
frusta is traced in EtymDB to Latin fūstis "a stick, a club".

Lesson 3 of 7.
montare — to whip (cream, eggs); to climb; to assemble.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for the whisk, then the Italian for to beat.

You'll want to know: montare.
montare — "to whip (cream, eggs); to climb; to assemble".
Monta le uova con la frusta per dieci minuti. Whip the eggs with the whisk for ten minutes.
Montare a neve — to beat until stiff. Also montare un mobile — to put furniture together.

The word, taken apart.
montare is built on monte "mountain", from Latin mōns, montis (Lewis & Short): to go up, and in the kitchen, to make it rise.

Lesson 4 of 7.
amalgamare — to blend, to mix until smooth.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to beat, then the Italian for to whip.

You'll want to know: amalgamare.
amalgamare — "to blend, to mix until smooth".
Amalgama bene tutti gli ingredienti. Blend all the ingredients well.
A recipe word: until the mixture is even.

The word, taken apart.
amalgamare is built on amalgama, from Medieval Latin amalgama, a word of the alchemists; its deeper origin is not settled (EtymDB has no entry).

Lesson 5 of 7.
il mestolo — the ladle.

Warm-up.
[pause 2 seconds]
Before the new one: say the Italian for to whip, then the Italian for to blend.

You'll want to know: il mestolo.
il mestolo — "the ladle".
Aggiungi l'acqua un mestolo alla volta. Add the water one ladleful at a time.
Wooden or steel; un mestolo di brodo is a ladleful of broth.

The word, taken apart.
mestolo is built on mestare "to stir" (EtymDB), from Vulgar Latin mixtāre, built on miscēre "to mix".
