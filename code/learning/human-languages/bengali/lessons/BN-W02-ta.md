# ত — the consonant ta, the dental one

## Warm-up
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[]; assesses=[BN-SCRIPT-I-INDEP-01] -->

[PAUSE 2s] Read **নাম** aloud, then write **ই** and **ি** and say which is which.

## Script
<!-- hl-knowledge: introduces=[BN-SCRIPT-TA-01]; assesses=[BN-SCRIPT-AA-MATRA-01] -->

> ত

**tô** — and the tongue goes somewhere an English speaker does not put it. Press
the tip flat against the **back of the top front teeth**, so the contact is on
enamel, and release. That is this letter.

The English *t* of *top* is made a centimetre further back, on the ridge behind
the teeth. Bengali has a **separate letter** for that further-back sound, and it
arrives later in this chapter's family. The two are not accents of one letter:
they spell different words. So an English *t* dropped into a Bengali word is not
a slight accent — it is the other letter, and the listener hears it as one.

The test is physical, not auditory. Say English *top* and feel where the tongue
lands. Slide it forward onto the teeth and say it again. The second one is **ত**.

With the mātrā:

> **ত** + **া** → **তা**
